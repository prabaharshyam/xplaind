Most AI guides start with Python. If you work in operations, sales, HR, finance, teaching or management, that is the wrong place to start. The people who benefit most from AI right now are often not programmers: they are people who know a job well and learn to apply AI to it.

Here are three paths that need no coding, what each leads to, and how to start for free.

## Path 1: Use AI well at work

**Who it suits:** anyone. Students, professionals, business owners, parents.

**What you learn:** what AI is and is not; how to get useful results from assistants such as ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude for writing, research, analysis and images; how to check answers; what never to paste into a chatbot; and the basics of AI rules, including India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act.

**How long:** three to six weeks at a few hours a week.

**Why it matters:** this is fast becoming a basic workplace skill, like using a spreadsheet. People who do it well can save hours a week and make fewer AI-caused mistakes.

Start with our [AI for Beginners path](/roadmap/ai-for-beginners), or the lesson on [using an AI assistant](/stop/use-an-assistant). Free courses that fit: [AI For Everyone](/go/dlai-ai-for-everyone), OpenAI Academy's [Apply AI at Work](/go/openai-academy-ai-at-work) and Anthropic's [Claude 101](/go/anthropic-claude-101).

## Path 2: Automate work with AI, using low-code tools

**Who it suits:** people in operations, admin, sales operations and small businesses who are comfortable with spreadsheets and logic, but do not write code.

**What you learn:** how to map a process and find what to automate; tools such as n8n, Zapier, Make and Power Automate; how apps talk to each other through webhooks and JSON; adding AI steps that read emails, extract invoice data or answer customers on WhatsApp; and handling errors, approvals and personal data safely.

**How long:** about three months at 5 to 6 hours a week.

**Where it leads:** automation specialist, operations roles, freelance automation work for small businesses, or your own business processes running faster.

Start with our [AI Automation Specialist path](/roadmap/ai-automation-specialist) and the lesson [What automation is](/stop/what-is-automation). You will meet a little JSON along the way, but no programming.

## Path 3: AI product management

**Who it suits:** product managers, business analysts, consultants and founders who decide what gets built.

**What you learn:** how AI products work underneath; how to choose problems AI is good for; designing for answers that are sometimes wrong; measuring quality with evaluations; pricing features whose cost grows with use; and responsible AI and regulation.

**How long:** about three months at 4 to 5 hours a week.

**Where it leads:** AI product manager roles, or better decisions in your current product or strategy role.

Start with our [AI Product Manager path](/roadmap/ai-product-manager) and the lesson [How AI products work](/stop/how-ai-products-work). For a credential, Google Cloud's Generative AI Leader exam is designed for non-engineers; see our [certifications guide](/blog/free-ai-certifications-big-tech).

## Myths that hold non-programmers back

- **"AI is only for engineers."** Engineers build the systems; most of the value comes from people who know where to use them.
- **"I need maths first."** Not for these paths. You need clear thinking and curiosity.
- **"AI will take my job, so why learn?"** Research such as the International Labour Organization's work on generative AI suggests most jobs are more likely to change than disappear. The people best placed are those who learn to work with it.
- **"I have to learn everything."** You do not. Pick one path and finish it.
