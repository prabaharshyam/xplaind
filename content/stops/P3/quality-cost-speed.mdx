## What it is

Every LLM app trades three things against each other:

- **Quality**: how often answers are correct, complete and safe.
- **Cost**: money per request and per month.
- **Speed**: how long users wait.

Bigger models, more retrieved context, rerankers, reflection steps and agent loops usually raise quality and also raise cost and latency. Good engineering makes these trade-offs visible and decides them on purpose, with the people who own the product.

## How it works

**Measure all three on the same test set.** For each option, record:

- quality from your eval (by category, not only overall);
- cost per request (tokens times prices);
- latency at the median and the 95th percentile, and time to first token.

**Put the options in a table.** For example: small model, large model, large model plus reranker. Then the trade-off becomes a business conversation: "the reranker improves accuracy from 82% to 88% and adds 400 ms per request at p95; worth it?"

**Ask what quality matters.** For a casual chat, 85% may be fine; for medical or financial answers, quality and safety dominate. Weight errors by their consequences.

**Look for free wins first.** Some changes improve speed or cost without hurting quality:

- streaming (perceived speed);
- prompt caching (cost and speed for repeated prefixes);
- shorter outputs and prompts;
- running independent steps in parallel;
- removing unnecessary steps.

**Route by difficulty.** Send simple requests to a fast, cheap model and hard ones to a strong model. Measure how often the router gets it wrong.

**Set budgets.** Agree a latency budget (for example, first token under 1.5 s at p95) and a cost per request ceiling, then maximise quality within them.

**Revisit.** Prices fall, models improve and usage grows; the best trade-off changes. Re-run the table every few months.

## A worked example

A Hyderabad health-insurance company's assistant answers policy questions for its call-centre agents.

The team compares four options on 150 test questions:

```text
option                         accuracy   cost/request   p95 latency
small model                      76%        Rs 0.08        1.6 s
large model                      85%        Rs 0.60        3.9 s
large model + reranker           89%        Rs 0.64        4.3 s
router (small, hard -> large)    86%        Rs 0.24        2.8 s
```

The product owner's view: agents are on live calls, so latency matters a lot; wrong coverage answers cause complaints and refunds, so accuracy matters more. They choose the router plus the reranker for questions about exclusions and claims (the riskiest categories), reaching 88% overall at about ₹0.30 per request and 3.1 s at p95. Streaming makes the first words appear in under a second.

## Common mistakes

- **Optimising one dimension silently.** Show all three.
- **Overall accuracy only.** Weight by error consequences and categories.
- **Ignoring tail latency.** Users remember slow requests.
- **Missing free wins.** Streaming and caching first.
- **Never revisiting the decision.**

## Practise

Reread Chip Huyen's production article linked below, focusing on cost and latency.

1. Build a table of quality, cost per request and p95 latency for two or three options in your app.
2. Write a one-paragraph recommendation for a product owner, with the trade-off stated plainly.
3. Apply one change that improves speed without hurting quality, and measure it.

**You are done when you can** present a trade-off table and justify a choice in terms a non-engineer understands.
