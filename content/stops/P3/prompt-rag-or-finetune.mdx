## What it is

When a language model does not do what you need, there are three main ways to improve it:

- **Better prompting**: clearer instructions, examples, structure and format.
- **RAG (retrieval-augmented generation)**: fetching relevant information from your documents or data and putting it in the prompt.
- **Fine-tuning**: training the model further on your own examples so it learns a behaviour.

They solve different problems and are often combined. Choosing well saves months.

## How it works

**Diagnose the problem first.**

- *The model does not know something* (your products, policies, recent events, a customer's account): that is a knowledge problem. Use **RAG** or tools. Fine-tuning is a poor way to add facts: it is costly to update and the model may still misremember them.
- *The model knows enough but does the task the wrong way* (format, tone, steps, edge cases): try **prompting** first: instructions, examples, a clearer format.
- *Prompting works but is long, expensive, slow or still inconsistent for a narrow, high-volume task*: consider **fine-tuning** a model, often a smaller one, on many good examples.

**Why prompting first.** It is fast, cheap and reversible: you can test a change in minutes. Many problems blamed on "the model" disappear with clearer instructions and two good examples.

**When RAG fits.** Information that changes often (a handbook updated weekly), is large (thousands of documents), is private, or must be cited. Updating means re-indexing, not retraining.

**When fine-tuning fits.**

- a consistent style or format across very many requests;
- a narrow classification or extraction task with lots of labelled examples;
- making a small, cheap model match a large one on one task;
- shortening prompts that carry many examples.

It needs hundreds to thousands of quality examples, evaluation, and repeating when the base model changes.

**Combine.** A common mature setup: a well-designed prompt, RAG for knowledge, and possibly a fine-tuned small model for one high-volume step. Evaluate each change on the same test set.

## A worked example

Three problems at a Pune software company:

1. *The support bot must answer from a product handbook that changes every week.* Knowledge that changes: **RAG** over the handbook, re-indexed on every update. Fine-tuning would be out of date within a week.
2. *Ticket summaries vary in format and miss the customer's priority.* The model has the information; the instruction is unclear. **Prompting**: a fixed template, a rule about priority and two examples fix 90% of cases in an afternoon.
3. *Classifying 2 million log lines a month into 30 categories with a large model costs too much.* They have 50,000 labelled lines from past work. **Fine-tune** a small model: on their test set it matches the large model's accuracy at a fraction of the cost and latency.

## Common mistakes

- **Fine-tuning to add facts.** Use retrieval.
- **Skipping prompting.** It is the cheapest fix and often enough.
- **Choosing by fashion.** Diagnose knowledge versus behaviour.
- **No evaluation set.** You cannot compare approaches without one.
- **Forgetting maintenance.** Fine-tuned models need retraining; RAG needs fresh indexes.
