## What it is

A **portfolio** is the evidence that you can do the job. For AI engineering and FDE roles it usually means:

- **A GitHub profile** that leads with your three to five best projects, each with a clear README.
- **Case-study write-ups** of one or two projects, written like the end-to-end case study: problem, numbers, decisions, results, what went wrong.
- **A resume** of one page (two at most for experienced engineers) with results, not duties.
- **Optionally**, short demo videos and a few public posts explaining what you learned.

A **job search** is the plan to put that evidence in front of the right people.

```text
   ONE LINK, 60 SECONDS
   github.com/you  (profile README)
   ├── pinned: enterprise RAG assistant      "recall at 5: 68% -> 93% on 60 questions"
   ├── pinned: operations agent with approvals  "0 unapproved actions in 200 test runs"
   ├── pinned: document QA API               "96% field accuracy; 4 s per file"
   └── case study: hospital chain capstone   3 pages + 2-minute video

   resume: 1 page | results, not duties | tools in context
   search: 40 target companies -> contacts at 15 -> specific notes -> tracked in a sheet
```

This lesson follows Nandhini, a backend developer in Coimbatore, from finishing this path to receiving offers.

## How it works

**Choose projects that match the job.** For FDE roles: projects with a realistic client problem, messy data, evals, deployment and governance. The six stage projects on this path are designed for this. Three strong, finished projects beat ten tutorials.

**READMEs that sell.** Top of each README: one sentence on the problem, a screenshot or 60-second video, the result with a number ("field accuracy 96% on 300 real-style documents; 4 seconds per file"), the architecture in a small diagram, and how to run it. Then details. Pin these repositories on your profile and add a profile README linking to your case studies.

**Resume bullets with results.** Pattern: action, what, measurable result. "Built a RAG assistant over 4,000 pages of insurance policies with hybrid search and reranking; raised retrieval recall at 5 from 68% to 93% on a 60-question golden set." Put AI, cloud and tools in context, not as a long keyword list.

**Target companies.** In India and remotely: AI labs and AI-first startups with enterprise customers, data and AI platform companies, global capability centres of large firms building AI teams, and IT services and consulting firms with AI practices. Read job posts carefully: some say "forward deployed", others say "solutions engineer", "AI engineer, customer-facing" or "applied AI engineer".

**Get referrals and conversations.** Message engineers at target companies with a specific note and a link to one relevant project. Contribute to open-source tools you used. Share short write-ups of what you built and learned.

**Track it.** A simple sheet of applications, contacts, stages and follow-ups. Expect many rejections; adjust your materials after every ten applications based on what gets responses.

**Weak and strong portfolio pieces:**

| Piece | Weak | Strong |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Project | Chatbot over a PDF from a video | Real problem, messy data, golden set, deployed |
| README opening | "pip install ..." | Problem, visual, result with a number |
| Resume bullet | "Worked on RAG" | "Raised recall at 5 from 68% to 93% on a 60-question golden set" |
| Outreach note | "Please refer me" | "I built X for problems like your customers'; here is the link" |

**Writing an outreach note.** Keep it to four sentences: who you are, the one project relevant to their company and its result, a specific question or ask, and thanks. For example: "I'm a backend developer in Coimbatore moving into AI engineering. I built a claims-document assistant with 96% field accuracy on 300 test files, close to what your team sells to insurers. Would you have 15 minutes to tell me how your FDE team works? Thank you." Specific notes get replies; generic referral requests rarely do.

**Reviewing after every ten applications.** Count replies and first rounds. If replies are low, rework the top third of the resume and the first lines of your READMEs. If first rounds go badly, practise the round that failed. Change one thing at a time, so you know what helped.

## A worked example

Nandhini, a backend developer in Coimbatore with two years of Java experience, finishes this path in seven months.

Her portfolio: a pinned repository for each of four projects (the document QA API, the enterprise RAG assistant with its eval report, the operations agent with approval gates, and the capstone for a fictional hospital chain), each with a result in the first line. Her capstone case study is three pages and has a two-minute video.

Her resume moves the Java work to concise bullets and adds a "Projects" section with numbers. She lists 40 target companies, finds engineers at 15 of them, and sends short notes linking the project most relevant to each company's customers.

Results over eight weeks: 31 applications, 9 replies, 6 first rounds, 3 final loops, 2 offers, one of them a forward deployed role at a startup selling to hospitals, who said the capstone case study was the reason they called.

## Common mistakes

- **Tutorial clones.** A chatbot over a PDF, built from a video, shows little. Add a real problem, evals and deployment.
- **READMEs that start with installation.** Lead with the problem and the result.
- **Duties instead of results.** "Worked on RAG" says nothing; numbers do.
- **Applying everywhere with one resume.** Tailor the top third to each role.
- **Waiting until "ready".** Apply while finishing the last projects; interviews teach you what to improve.
