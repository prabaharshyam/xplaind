## What it is

A **portfolio** is the evidence that you can do the job. For AI engineering and forward deployed engineer (FDE) roles it usually has five parts:

- **A GitHub profile** that leads with your three to five best projects, each with a clear README.
- **Case-study write-ups** of one or two projects: the problem, the numbers, the decisions, the results and what went wrong.
- **A resume** of one page (two at most for experienced engineers) that lists results, not duties.
- **A LinkedIn profile** that tells the same story, because many Indian recruiters search there.
- **Optionally**, short demo videos and a few public posts about what you learned.

A **job search** is the plan that puts this evidence in front of the right people, at the right time, at the right pay.

```flow
title: One link, 60 seconds: github.com/you
Profile README -> Pinned: enterprise RAG assistant | "recall at 5: 68% to 93% on my 60-question golden set"
Profile README -> Pinned: operations agent with approvals | "0 unapproved actions in 200 scripted test runs"
Profile README -> Pinned: document QA API | "96% field accuracy on 300 synthetic files; 4 s per file"
Profile README -> Case study: hospital chain capstone | 3 pages + a 2-minute video
```

```flow
dir: LR
title: The search
40 target companies -> Referrals or contacts at 15 -> Coding test and interviews -> Offers compared on fixed pay -> Notice served, then join
```

This lesson follows Nandhini, a backend developer in Coimbatore, from finishing this path to choosing between offers.

## How it works

### Choose projects that match the job

For FDE roles, choose projects with a realistic client problem, messy data, evals, deployment and governance, as the stage projects on this path have. Three finished projects beat ten tutorials, because interviewers pick one and question it for half an hour.

### READMEs that sell

Start each README with one sentence on the problem and a screenshot or 60-second video. Then give the result with a number, a small architecture diagram and how to run it. Pin these repositories and add a profile README linking to your case studies.

Test it the way a reviewer will. Ask a friend to clone one project and run it using only the README. If it takes more than 10 minutes, or needs a message to you, fix the README or add a hosted demo.

### Numbers you can defend

Every number on your portfolio is an invitation to be questioned. Interviewers ask: "What was that measured on? Can you show me?" Follow three rules:

1. **Name the test set and its size.** "96% field accuracy" is a claim. "96% field accuracy on 300 synthetic insurance files I made" is evidence.
2. **Be honest about where the data came from.** A capstone for a fictional hospital chain is fine. Calling it "reduced discharge delays by 30%" is not, because no hospital used it. Say what you measured: accuracy, latency, cost per request.
3. **Keep the eval runnable.** Put the golden set and the eval script in the repository, so you can rerun it live if asked.

A modest number you can explain beats a big one you cannot.

### Resume, screening software and LinkedIn

**Bullets with results.** The pattern is action, what you built, and a measured result. For example: "Built a RAG assistant over 4,000 pages of insurance policies with hybrid search and reranking; raised retrieval recall at 5 from 68% to 93% on a 60-question golden set."

**The first look is short.** In a 2018 eye-tracking study, the job site Ladders found that recruiters spent about 7.4 seconds on a first look at each resume. They looked mostly at titles, companies and dates. Simple layouts with clear headings held attention; cluttered, multi-column ones did badly. Its sample size is unreported, but the lesson holds: put your strongest result in the top third.

**Applicant tracking systems.** Many employers load resumes into an applicant tracking system (ATS), software that stores applications and lets recruiters filter them by keyword. Two-column templates, text in images and icons can come out scrambled, so use one column, standard headings (Experience, Projects, Skills, Education) and a text-based PDF. Add one short skills line that repeats the exact terms from the job post ("RAG", "LangGraph", "AWS"), as long as they are true. Elsewhere, put tools in context inside your result bullets rather than in a long keyword list.

**LinkedIn.** Use the same headline as your GitHub profile, for example "Backend engineer building RAG and agent systems for healthcare". Pin one project in the Featured section and write a short About section with your best two results. LinkedIn lets you mark yourself open to work, visible to recruiters only if you prefer.

### Where Indian AI jobs are posted

| Channel | Best for | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Naukri | Volume: IT services, GCCs, large firms; recruiters search its resume database | Keep your profile and notice period current; recruiters filter on both |
| LinkedIn | Product companies, GCCs, AI startups; finding the people who hire | Many applications per post; use it to find contacts, not only the Apply button |
| Instahyre, foundit | Tech roles (Instahyre); a broad mix (foundit) | Fill in skills and expected pay carefully; matching depends on them |
| Wellfound | Startups, including early-stage AI startups | Check funding and runway before you join |
| Company careers pages | GCCs and large firms | Search by the titles each company uses |

Read titles carefully. FDE-style work may be called "forward deployed engineer", "solutions engineer", "AI engineer, customer-facing" or "applied AI engineer".

### Referrals and outreach

In India, a referral is often the fastest way to get a human to read your resume. Many companies run employee referral programmes, so a referrer may get a bonus if you are hired. That makes it a fair request, not a favour. But a referrer puts their name on you, so make it easy and safe for them:

- **Ask people who know you first:** college alumni, former colleagues, seniors, people from open-source projects and meetups.
- **Send everything at once:** the job link or ID, your resume, and two lines on your most relevant project with its result.
- **For strangers, ask for a conversation, not a referral.** Use four sentences: who you are, one relevant project and its result, a specific question, and thanks. For example: "I'm a backend developer in Coimbatore moving into AI engineering. I built a claims-document assistant with 96% field accuracy on 300 synthetic test files, close to what your team sells to insurers. Would you have 15 minutes to tell me how your FDE team works? Thank you." Referrals often follow a good conversation.

"Please refer me" with no context rarely gets a reply.

### The coding round

Many Indian product companies, GCCs and IT services firms start with an online coding test on a platform such as HackerRank or HackerEarth. Candidate reports often describe one to two hours and two or three easy-to-medium problems on data structures and algorithms (DSA): arrays, strings, hash maps, two pointers, sorting, trees and graph search, sometimes basic dynamic programming. Candidates also report cut-off scores, so a strong portfolio may never be seen if you fail this step.

FDE and AI-startup loops lean more towards practical coding: cleaning messy data, calling an API, adding validation and tests (see the lesson "The FDE interview loop"). The decision rule:

- **Ask the recruiter for the format of round one** before you prepare.
- **If it is a DSA test,** practise about 60 to 80 common problems by pattern, not 400 at random. Time yourself, and say your approach aloud.
- **If it is practical coding,** practise on messy CSVs and JSON with tests, and talk through trade-offs.
- **Either way, keep it warm:** 30 minutes a day during the search beats a panic week.

### Notice periods and timing

Your **notice period** is how long you must keep working after you resign. Your appointment letter and company policy set it, not a national rule. Large IT services firms commonly use 90 days. Many product companies and startups use shorter periods. Read three clauses before you plan: the notice length, whether you may pay a **buyout** (salary in place of the days you do not serve), and any bond or recovery of training costs.

Long notice shapes your whole search:

- **Interview before you resign,** so you are not without pay or options for three months.
- **State your notice up front** on Naukri and to recruiters. Some posts want people who can join in 30 days or less; hiding a 90-day notice wastes everyone's time.
- **Ask the new employer two questions:** can they wait, and will they reimburse a buyout? Some do; your employer may refuse a buyout.
- **Expect offers to be at risk during long notice.** Keep in touch with your new manager until you join, and do not lie about having another offer.

### Salary research

Research the band for the role you are applying for, not for your current job. Xplaind's Jobs page collects typical yearly CTC bands, in lakh rupees, from Glassdoor, AmbitionBox, Instahyre and job posts, **checked 2 November 2026**. They are middle-of-the-road bands, not offers, and the Jobs page wins if it differs from this table.

| Role | 0 to 2 years | 3 to 5 years | 6+ years |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| AI Engineer | 6 to 12 | 12 to 25 | 25 to 50 |
| Generative AI Engineer | 8 to 15 | 18 to 35 | 35 to 65 |
| Forward Deployed Engineer | 12 to 25 | 25 to 50 | 50 to 90 |

How to use it:

- **Narrow the band.** Big tech, global AI labs and Bengaluru pay more; IT services and smaller cities pay less. The early FDE band is rarely a fresher job.
- **Check the specific company** on Glassdoor and AmbitionBox, and ask people you meet what the band is.
- **Answer "current and expected CTC" carefully.** Indian recruiters usually ask both early. Give your current CTC honestly, because it may be checked against payslips. Base your expected figure on the new role's band and your evidence, not on a fixed percentage over your current pay.

### Comparing offers

CTC (cost to company) is not take-home pay. Break each offer into fixed pay, variable pay, joining bonus (often repayable if you leave within a year), stock or ESOPs (which may never pay out) and benefits. Compare the cash you are sure of in year one, then the work, the learning, the team and the new notice period. Get any change in writing before you accept.

### Remote roles and jobs abroad

Foreign companies hire Indian engineers remotely through an employer of record (a firm that employs you in India on their behalf) or as contractors. Competition is global, so your portfolio and written English matter even more. As a contractor you are self-employed: you send invoices and handle your own tax, so speak to a chartered accountant before you sign. Moving abroad needs a work visa. Sponsorship for juniors is rare, and US work-visa rules changed sharply in 2025 and are still changing, so check the official government source before you plan around them.

### Track and review

Keep a simple sheet: company, role, channel (cold, referral, recruiter), date, stage and next follow-up. After every ten applications, find the stage where people drop out and change one thing at a time:

| Where it stops | Likely cause | What to change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No replies | Wrong roles, weak top third, no referrals, notice hidden or too long | Tailor the top third; move to referrals; target better-fitting roles |
| Fail the coding test | Format not practised | Ask the format; practise by pattern, timed |
| Fail first rounds | Shaky project deep dive | Rehearse your project's decisions and numbers aloud |
| Fail final loops | Weak design or client role-play | Practise those rounds with a friend |
| Offers fall through | Pay mismatch or notice period | Research bands earlier; raise notice in the first call |

Apply before you feel "ready": interviews show you what to fix.

## A worked example

Nandhini, a backend developer in Coimbatore with two years of Java experience, finishes this path in seven months. The numbers below are an illustration, not a benchmark. Your own funnel may look quite different.

**Portfolio.** She pins four projects, each with a result and its test set in the first lines: the document QA API, the enterprise RAG assistant with its eval report, the operations agent with approval gates, and the capstone for a fictional hospital chain. The capstone case study is three pages with a two-minute video. Its README says plainly that the hospital is fictional and the data synthetic. A friend runs the RAG assistant from its README in eight minutes.

**Resume and LinkedIn.** She switches to a one-column resume, moves the Java work to short bullets and adds a "Projects" section with numbers. Her LinkedIn headline matches her GitHub profile.

**Timing and pay.** Her appointment letter says 90 days' notice, with buyout at the company's discretion. She puts this on her Naukri profile. Her current CTC is ₹7 lakh. For the role she wants, the FDE band for 0 to 2 years is ₹12 to 25 lakh. New to AI work and outside Bengaluru, she places herself in the lower half, so she sets her expected CTC at ₹15 to 17 lakh.

**The search, over ten weeks.** She lists 40 target companies and finds alumni or contacts at 15 of them.

| Channel | Applications | Replies | First rounds | Final loops | Offers |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cold (Naukri, LinkedIn) | 30 | 2 | 1 | 0 | 0 |
| Referral or contact | 15 | 6 | 5 | 3 | 2 |
| Total | 45 | 8 | 6 | 3 | 2 |

Her sheet shows two things. Referrals gave six of the eight replies. And she failed her first DSA online test in week three, so she began asking each recruiter for the format and practised timed problems by pattern, 30 minutes a day. She passed the next two.

**Two offers.** An AI startup selling to hospitals offers a forward deployed role. The hiring manager says the capstone case study is why they called.

| Part of the offer | Startup, FDE | GCC, applied AI engineer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fixed pay | ₹15 lakh | ₹16 lakh |
| Variable pay | ₹1.5 lakh, if targets are met | None |
| Joining bonus | None | ₹1 lakh, repayable if she leaves within a year |
| Stock | ESOPs valued at ₹1.5 lakh a year | None |
| CTC as written | ₹18 lakh | ₹17 lakh |
| Cash she is sure of in year one | ₹15 lakh | ₹17 lakh |

The startup's CTC is higher, but the GCC pays ₹2 lakh more in certain cash. Nandhini chooses the startup anyway, for the client-facing work she wants, and asks for ₹16 lakh fixed. They agree, in writing. Her company releases her after 60 days once her handover is done, and the startup agrees to wait.

## Common mistakes

- **Tutorial clones.** A chatbot over a PDF, built from a video, shows little. Add a real problem, evals and deployment.
- **READMEs that start with installation.** Lead with the problem and the result.
- **Numbers you cannot defend.** A result without its test set, or a fictional client's "business impact", collapses in the project deep dive.
- **Fancy two-column templates.** They can scramble in screening software. Use one column.
- **Only cold applications.** Referrals and conversations usually get far more replies per hour spent.
- **Skipping coding practice.** A strong portfolio does not help if you fail the online test that comes first.
- **Hiding your notice period.** It surfaces late and kills the offer.
- **Comparing offers on CTC alone.** Variable pay, joining bonuses and stock are not certain cash.

**For your search (not checked, but do it).**

3. Look up the Jobs page band for your target role and write your expected CTC with a one-line reason.
4. List 20 target companies with one person to contact at each, and start a tracking sheet.

**You are done when you can** send a stranger one link that shows, within 60 seconds, what you built, what it achieved and what that number was measured on.
