## What it is

A **KPI** (key performance indicator) is the number that says whether the project worked. For an AI deployment you usually need:

- **One primary KPI** tied to the business result: minutes per loan file, first-response time, claims processed per officer per day, cost per resolved ticket.
- **A baseline**: the KPI measured before your system, under the same conditions.
- **A target and a date**: "from 40 to 20 minutes per file within eight weeks of pilot start".
- **Guardrail measures**: things that must not get worse, such as error rate, customer complaints or compliance exceptions.
- **Adoption**: whether people actually use the system, because a tool nobody uses cannot move the KPI.

Model metrics such as accuracy or faithfulness are inputs, not the KPI. A 95%-accurate extractor that saves nobody time is a failure.

```text
   KPI PLAN (signed in the scope of work)
   +------------------------------------------------------------------+
   | PRIMARY     median officer minutes per loan file                 |
   | BASELINE    41 min (loan-system timestamps, 4 weeks, 6,200 files)|
   | TARGET      22 min within 10 weeks of pilot start                |
   | GUARDRAILS  rework <= 7%; no rise in audit policy exceptions     |
   | ADOPTION    officers using the draft on >= 80% of their files    |
   +------------------------------------------------------------------+
   model accuracy -> tracked as an INPUT, not the KPI
```

This lesson follows the KPI agreement for a credit-file assistant at a lender in Chennai.

## How it works

**Start from the problem statement.** If discovery found that officers spend 40 minutes per file, the KPI is time per file, not "AI accuracy".

**Make it measurable from data you can get.** Prefer numbers that systems already record (timestamps in the loan system, ticket close times) over surveys. If nothing records it, set up measurement first: a simple log of start and end times for a sample of cases.

**Measure the baseline properly.** Same team, same kind of cases, enough volume (a few hundred cases or two to four weeks). Watch for seasonal effects: a month-end rush is not typical.

**Choose guardrails deliberately.** Faster processing with more errors is not a win. Pick one or two quality measures the client's risk owners care about.

**Agree it in writing.** Put the KPI, baseline method, target, guardrails and measurement period in the scope of work, signed off by the business owner.

**Compare fairly.** During the pilot, compare users with the system against similar users without it over the same period, or the same users before and after, adjusting for obvious differences.

**Report regularly.** A weekly one-line update ("median 26 minutes per file this week, down from 41; rework 5.8% against 7% baseline; 82% of officers active") keeps everyone aligned.

**Inputs versus outcomes:**

| Input (model or system) | Outcome (business) |
| --- | --- |
| Extraction accuracy | Minutes per loan file |
| Retrieval recall | Time to answer a policy question |
| Faithfulness score | Rework or complaint rate |
| Latency | Calls handled per agent per hour |
| Uptime | Claims processed per day |

**Fair comparisons.** If possible, compare pilot users with similar users not yet using the system, over the same weeks, so seasonal changes such as month-end rushes affect both groups. If everyone gets the tool at once, compare the same users before and after, and note anything else that changed, such as a new policy or staffing. Write the method into the measurement plan before the pilot starts, so nobody can pick a favourable comparison afterwards.

**The weekly line.** One sentence with the KPI, the guardrail and adoption, sent every week: "Median 26 minutes per file this week, down from 41; rework 5.8% against a 7% baseline; 82% of officers active." It keeps the business owner informed and makes the final review a formality, because nobody is surprised.

## A worked example

Following discovery at the Chennai lender, you propose:

- **Primary KPI**: median officer time per loan file, from opening the file to submitting the credit note.
- **Baseline**: measured from the loan system's timestamps over four weeks before the pilot: 41 minutes median, with 6,200 files.
- **Target**: 22 minutes median within ten weeks of pilot start, for the 20 pilot officers.
- **Guardrails**: credit committee rework rate (baseline 7%) must not rise; no increase in policy exceptions flagged by audit.
- **Adoption**: share of pilot officers using the draft summary on at least 80% of their files.

The head of operations first wants "90% AI accuracy" as the target. You explain that accuracy will be tracked as a quality input, but the lender's business result is officer time and rework, and agree on both. After ten weeks the pilot shows 24 minutes median, 5.1% rework and 88% adoption. The target was narrowly missed on time but beaten on quality; because the measures were agreed in advance, the conversation is about rolling out, not about what success meant.

| Measure | Baseline | Target | Pilot result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Median minutes per file | 41 | 22 | 24 |
| Rework rate | 7% | Not above 7% | 5.1% |
| Adoption | Not applicable | 80% of files | 88% |
| Conversation at the end | | | About rollout, not about what success meant |

## Common mistakes

- **Model metrics as the KPI.** Accuracy is a means; time, cost or errors in the business are the end.
- **No baseline.** "Officers feel it's faster" will not survive a budget review.
- **Too many KPIs.** Ten numbers and nobody knows which matters. One primary, a few guardrails.
- **Measuring after launch only.** The baseline must be taken before the system changes behaviour.
- **Ignoring adoption.** Low usage explains most "the AI did not help" results.
