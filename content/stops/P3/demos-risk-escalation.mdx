## What it is

Three habits that keep an engagement healthy:

- **A weekly demo**: 20 to 30 minutes showing working software on real cases, not slides. What changed, what users said, what is next.
- **A risk log**: a short, living list of what could go wrong, how likely and serious it is, who owns it and what is being done.
- **Escalation**: raising a blocker to someone with more authority, early and with options, when it cannot be solved at the working level in time.

```text
   EVERY WEEK
   [demo: real software, real masked cases, one failure shown]
            |
            v
   [risk log review: 5 minutes]   risk | likelihood | impact | owner | action | due | status
            |
            v
   [status note]  AMBER  progress: ...  risks: ...  asks: ...
            |
   risk about to hit a milestone and owner stuck?
            |
            v
   [escalation]  facts -> impact -> options -> recommendation -> decision by <date>
```

This lesson follows week six of a credit-summary pilot at a lender in Chennai.

## How it works

**Demo rules.** Show the real system on real (masked) data. Include something that went wrong and how it was handled; it builds credibility. Invite users and the business owner, not only the IT contact. End with decisions needed from the client and the next week's plan. Record decisions in writing after the call.

**Risk log.** For each risk: a clear statement ("Bank statement photos from rural branches may be too blurry to read"), likelihood and impact (low, medium, high), owner (a named person, often on the client side), action and due date, and status. Review it in the weekly call, briefly. Start it in week one with a pre-mortem: imagine the project has failed in three months and list why.

Typical AI engagement risks: data access delays, data quality worse than expected, a key subject expert unavailable, security review late, users not adopting, model provider changes or limits, scope growing.

**Escalation.** Escalate when a risk is about to hit a milestone and the owner cannot resolve it, or when a decision is needed above the working team. Do it early, factually and with options:

"Data access is three weeks late. The proof-of-concept gate moves from 15 August to 5 September unless we start with last year's archived files, which IT can share this week. We recommend the archive. Decision needed from the head of operations by Friday."

Escalation is not blame. Copy the people involved, keep the tone neutral and offer help.

**Status in one line.** A green, amber or red status with one sentence each for progress, risks and asks, sent after each weekly meeting, keeps leaders informed without meetings.

**A risk log extract:**

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Owner | Action | Due | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Security review of production not scheduled | High | High | IT security lead | Escalated to the CIO's office | Week 7 | Open |
| Two branches below 50% adoption | Medium | Medium | Regional operations heads | Branch visits | Week 7 | Open |
| Blurry statement photos from rural branches | Medium | Medium | Product lead | Flag for manual check; guidance to branches | Week 8 | Mitigated |

**Running the pre-mortem.** In week one, gather the core team for twenty minutes. Say: "It is three months from now and the project has failed. Write down why." Collect the reasons silently, then group them. Each group becomes a risk with an owner and an action. Teams that do this find most of the risks that later happen, while there is still time to act.

**Demos that build trust.** Start with what changed since last week, show it on real cases, then show one thing that went wrong and how the system or the team handled it. Close with decisions needed and next week's plan, and send a short written summary of decisions the same day. Clients trust a team that shows its failures far more than one that only shows polished paths.

## A worked example

Week 6 of the Chennai lender pilot. Your weekly update:

> **Status: Amber.** Median file time 27 minutes (baseline 41, target 22); rework 5.4%; adoption 78%.
> **Demo:** the new side-by-side review screen; one failure shown, a salary slip with handwritten edits that the system flagged correctly for manual check.
> **Top risks:**
> 1. *High*: the security review of the production environment has not been scheduled; production needs it by week 12. Owner: IT security lead. Action: escalated to the CIO's office today.
> 2. *Medium*: two branches below 50% adoption. Owner: regional operations heads. Action: visits next week.
> **Decisions needed:** approve the regional summary template by Wednesday.

The security escalation gets the review scheduled for week 9, three weeks before it would have blocked the production gate. In the final review, the head of operations says the weekly updates were why she trusted the timeline.

| --- | --- |
| Status amber: file time 27 minutes against a target of 22 | Kept leaders informed without extra meetings |
| Security review escalated to the CIO's office | Scheduled for week 9, three weeks before it would block production |
| Regional template decision requested by Wednesday | Approved on time |

## Common mistakes

- **Slide demos.** Show software; slides hide problems.
- **Risk logs that are never reviewed.** A list written once and forgotten protects no one.
- **Risks without owners.** "The team" owns nothing. Name a person.
- **Late escalation.** Raising a blocker on the day it hits is complaining, not escalating.
- **Escalation as blame.** Facts, impact, options, a clear ask.

## Practise

Read Atlassian's pages on the risk register and the pre-mortem.

1. Run a 20-minute pre-mortem (alone or with a friend) for your use case and turn the results into a risk log with owners and actions.
2. Write the weekly one-paragraph update for a week where one risk has turned red.
3. Write the escalation note for that risk with two options and a recommended decision.

**You are done when you can** show a risk log with named owners and an escalation note a busy executive could act on in two minutes.
