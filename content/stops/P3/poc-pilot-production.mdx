## What it is

An AI engagement usually moves through three stages:

- **Proof of concept (POC)**, 2 to 6 weeks: can this work on the client's real data? Built quickly, run offline on past cases, judged by evals. No real users yet.
- **Pilot**, 6 to 12 weeks: does it help real people in daily work? A limited group uses it, with safeguards, while you measure the KPI, quality and adoption.
- **Production**: rolled out to everyone intended, run and supported as a normal system, owned by the client's team.

Between each stage is a **stage gate**: a meeting where agreed evidence is reviewed and the client decides to go ahead, change course or stop.

```text
   POC (2-6 weeks)           PILOT (6-12 weeks)              PRODUCTION
   past cases, offline  -->  real users, safeguards     -->  everyone, in waves
   golden set + evals        KPI, guardrails, adoption       runbooks, on-call, dashboards
   riskiest assumption       sign-on, logs, security done    support period, keep measuring
        |                          |
     [GATE]                     [GATE]
   go | change | stop         go | change | stop
```

This lesson follows a credit-file assistant at a lender in Chennai from its first offline test to rollout across 180 officers.

## How it works

**POC.** Target the riskiest assumption first. For document extraction, that is usually "can we read their real, messy documents accurately?", not the user interface. Use real (masked if needed) data, build a golden set with the client's experts, and report accuracy, failure types, cost per case and latency. Skip polish. Gate criteria come from the SOW: for example, at least 95% accuracy on critical fields.

**Pilot.** Pick users who represent the wider group, not only enthusiasts. Keep humans in charge of decisions. Add what production needs early enough to learn from it: sign-on, logging, monitoring, feedback buttons. Measure the KPI against the baseline, guardrail measures and adoption weekly. Expect to change things; that is the point. Gate: KPI moved, guardrails held, users want to keep it, and support and security are ready.

**Production.** Roll out in waves (one region, then more). Hand over operations: runbooks, on-call, dashboards, training. Agree a support period. Keep measuring; production is not the end of evaluation.

**Stopping is a valid outcome.** A POC that shows the documents are unreadable, or a pilot where users ignore the tool, has done its job if it stops cheaply and explains why. Write it up; the learning is valuable.

**Avoid pilot purgatory.** Common causes: no agreed gate criteria, no business owner, production requirements (security, integration) left until after the pilot, and no budget planned for rollout. Address all four in the SOW.

**Gate evidence for each stage:**

| Gate | Evidence reviewed | Typical criteria |
| --- | --- | --- |
| POC to pilot | Golden-set accuracy, failure types, cost per case, latency | At least 95% on critical fields; known failures handled |
| Pilot to production | KPI against baseline, guardrails, adoption, user feedback, security sign-off | KPI moved; guardrails held; users want it; support ready |
| During production | Monthly KPI, cost, incidents | Results hold as usage grows |

**Running a gate meeting.** Send the evidence pack two days before: the agreed criteria, the measured results next to them, the main failures and fixes, the cost, and a recommendation. In the meeting, walk through criteria one by one and ask the business owner to decide go, change or stop. Record the decision and any conditions in writing the same day. A gate with criteria agreed in advance is short; a gate without them becomes a debate about what success means.

**Writing up a stop.** If the evidence says stop, write two pages: what was tested, what was found, why it does not work now, and what would have to change, such as better scans or a different process. Clients remember a team that stopped honestly and cheaply, and often bring them the next project.

## A worked example

The Chennai lender's credit-file assistant:

**POC, weeks 1 to 4.** 300 past files, masked. Critical-field accuracy 93%, below the 95% gate. Error analysis: most misses are on phone photos of bank statements with shadows. You add an image clean-up step and a "low confidence, please check" flag; accuracy rises to 96.4%. Gate passed, with the confidence flag added to the pilot design.

**Pilot, weeks 5 to 14.** 20 officers in two branches. Weekly numbers: median time per file falls from 41 to 24 minutes; rework 5.1% against a 7% baseline; adoption 88%. Officers ask for a side-by-side view of the document and extracted fields, which you add in week 8. Security review and sign-on are completed during the pilot, not after. Gate passed.

**Production, from week 15.** Rollout to 180 officers in three waves of a month each. The lender's IT team runs the system with your runbook and dashboards; you provide eight weeks of support. Monthly KPI reports continue.

| Stage | Result | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| POC, weeks 1 to 4 | 93% on critical fields, then 96.4% after image clean-up and a confidence flag | Gate passed; flag added to the pilot |
| Pilot, weeks 5 to 14 | 41 to 24 minutes per file; rework 5.1%; adoption 88%; security and sign-on done | Gate passed |
| Production, from week 15 | Three monthly waves to 180 officers; client IT runs it with eight weeks of support | Monthly KPI reports continue |

## Common mistakes

- **A polished POC on clean sample data.** It proves nothing about real documents.
- **Pilots without gate criteria.** They never end, and never scale.
- **Leaving security and integration until after the pilot.** That adds months at the worst moment.
- **Only friendly users in the pilot.** Results do not hold for everyone else.
- **Treating "stop" as failure.** A cheap, well-reasoned stop is a good outcome.

## Practise

Read GOV.UK's guides to the alpha, beta and live phases and note what carries over to AI work.

1. For your use case, write the POC plan: riskiest assumption, data, golden set, measures and gate criteria.
2. Write the pilot plan: users, safeguards, weekly measures, what production needs to be in place by the end, and gate criteria.
3. Write the rollout plan: waves, handover, support period and what would make you pause.

**You are done when you can** show a one-page plan with all three stages and the evidence needed at each gate.
