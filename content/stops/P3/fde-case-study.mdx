## What it is

This is a worked engagement, written for Xplaind, that uses the lessons on this path. The client is a fictional mid-sized non-banking lender in Chennai with 180 credit officers across Tamil Nadu and Karnataka. The engagement runs about six months. Read it as a model for your own capstone write-up.

## How it works

The engagement follows the stages of this path, each with its own evidence.

**1. First call and discovery (weeks 1 to 2).** The ask was "a chatbot for customers". Interviews and shadowing showed the real pain: credit officers spent about 41 minutes per loan file reading bank statements, salary slips and bureau reports, retyping figures and writing a summary, and 7% of files came back from the credit committee for errors. 6,000 files a month. The play-back reframed the project, and the business owner, the head of operations, agreed.

**2. KPI and scope (week 2).** Primary KPI: median officer time per file, baseline 41 minutes, target 22. Guardrail: rework must not exceed 7%. Adoption tracked. The scope of work named what was out: credit decisions, customer-facing features and non-English documents.

**3. Proof of concept (weeks 3 to 6).** 300 masked historical files, a golden set labelled with two senior officers. Extraction with a structured-output model call and Pydantic validation; a citation for every figure. First result: 93% on critical fields, below the 95% gate, mostly from shadowed phone photos. An image clean-up step and a low-confidence flag raised it to 96.4%. Gate passed.

**4. Engineering for the pilot (weeks 6 to 8).** The lender's risk team had approved Azure in Central India, so models were used through that tenancy, behind a gateway with a fallback. FastAPI service in containers with CI/CD, evals in the pipeline, tracing with masking, audit logs for every view and edit, sign-on with Entra ID, and document access filtered by each officer's branch. Personal data was masked in logs; erasure flowed through to derived data.

**5. Pilot (weeks 7 to 16).** 20 officers in two branches. Weekly demos and a risk log. Adoption stalled in one branch over fear of blame; fixed with written accountability and a side-by-side review screen. Three change requests were sized: one accepted as a swap, one deferred to phase 2, one declined (credit decisions). Result: 24 minutes median, 5.1% rework, 88% adoption.

**6. Production and handover (weeks 17 to 26).** A one-page memo to the CEO and CFO won approval for full rollout. Three waves across branches; model costs cut by caching and tiering to about ₹9 lakh a month all-in. Four-week handover with runbooks, decision records and reverse shadowing.

## A worked example

Here is how the FDE might summarise it in a portfolio write-up or an interview, in under two minutes:

"A Chennai lender asked for a customer chatbot. Discovery showed the real cost was 41 minutes of manual document work per loan, 6,000 loans a month, with 7% rework. I proposed and scoped a credit-file assistant: extraction with citations and a draft summary, with officers reviewing every figure. The POC first missed the accuracy gate because of phone photos; image clean-up and confidence flags fixed that. I deployed it in their Azure tenancy with sign-on, branch-level access, audit logs and evals in CI. In a 10-week pilot, time per file fell to 24 minutes, rework dropped to 5.1% and adoption reached 88% after we fixed an accountability concern in one branch. It rolled out to 180 officers and the client's team now runs it. What I'd do differently: start the security review in week one, not week six."

Notice the structure: problem with numbers, your decisions and why, one thing that went wrong and how you handled it, measured result, and a lesson.

## Common mistakes

These are the mistakes this engagement avoided, and that sink real ones:

- **Building what was asked instead of what was needed.** Discovery changed the project.
- **Model metrics as success.** Officer time and rework were the measures that won the budget.
- **Security and sign-on left until after the pilot.** Here they ran during it, except for one late review that had to be escalated.
- **Quiet scope growth.** Every request became a sized decision.
- **A handover document instead of a handover.** The client's engineers performed each task themselves.
