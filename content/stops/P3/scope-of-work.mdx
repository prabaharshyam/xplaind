## What it is

A **scope of work (SOW)** is the agreement that turns discovery into a plan. It is usually a few pages, signed by the client's business owner and your side. A good AI SOW contains:

- **Objective**: the problem and the primary KPI with baseline and target.
- **In scope**: what you will build and do, specifically.
- **Out of scope**: what you will not, stated just as clearly.
- **Deliverables**: each concrete output (a deployed service, an eval report, a runbook, training sessions).
- **Milestones**: dates, with the stage gates between proof of concept, pilot and production.
- **Acceptance criteria**: the testable conditions for each deliverable.
- **Assumptions and dependencies**: what you rely on the client for (data access by a date, an IT contact, sign-on set-up, subject experts' time).
- **Roles**: who decides, who reviews, who supports.
- **Change process**: how new requests are assessed and approved.
- **Risks**: the known ones, with owners.

## How it works

**Write acceptance criteria as tests.** "The assistant answers accurately" cannot be accepted or rejected. "On the agreed golden set of 150 questions, at least 85% are judged correct by two credit officers, and 100% of answers cite a source document" can.

**Be explicit about AI uncertainty.** Model outputs are probabilistic. State the quality bar on a defined test set, the human review that stays in place, and what happens if the bar is not met at a gate (extend, narrow scope, or stop).

**Name what is out.** "Out of scope: integration with the core banking system; Tamil-language documents; mobile app; customer-facing use." Most disputes come from things nobody wrote down.

**Make dependencies dated.** "Client provides read access to the document store by 15 July; each week of delay moves milestone 2 by one week."

**Keep it short and plain.** The business owner should be able to read it in fifteen minutes. Put technical detail in an appendix.

**Get the right signatures.** The person who can say "yes, this is done" must sign it.

## A worked example

Extract from the SOW for the Chennai lender's credit-file assistant:

> **Objective.** Reduce median officer time per loan file from 41 minutes (baseline, June) to 22 minutes for the 20 pilot officers, without raising credit committee rework above 7%.
>
> **In scope.** Extraction of 24 agreed fields from bank statements, salary slips and bureau reports (English, PDF and phone photos); a draft credit summary in the lender's template; a review screen for officers; deployment in the lender's Azure tenancy; eval and pilot reports; runbook; two training sessions.
>
> **Out of scope.** Credit decisions; customer-facing features; documents in languages other than English; integration with the loan origination system beyond a file export.
>
> **Acceptance (proof of concept, week 4).** On 300 past files, field accuracy of at least 95% on the 10 critical fields, measured against officers' entries; all summaries cite source pages.
>
> **Dependencies.** Read access to two years of anonymised files by 10 July; one senior credit officer for 4 hours a week.
>
> **Change process.** New requests are logged, sized within three working days and approved in writing by the head of operations before work starts.

Three weeks in, the collections team asks for the same tool for their documents. Because the SOW names the scope and the change process, the request becomes a sized change, scheduled for after the pilot, rather than a quiet extra that delays everything.

## Common mistakes

- **Vague acceptance.** "Works well" invites endless debate. Define tests and data.
- **Silence on what is out.** Unwritten assumptions become disputes.
- **Undated dependencies.** Then client delays become your missed deadlines.
- **Promising accuracy without a test set.** Always tie quality to an agreed dataset and judging method.
- **Signed by the wrong person.** The SOW needs the person who will accept the work.

## Practise

Read Atlassian's guide to project scope and GOV.UK's guide to user stories.

1. Write a two-page SOW for the use case from your discovery and KPI exercises, with every section above.
2. Write acceptance criteria for three deliverables as tests someone else could run.
3. Swap with a friend and ask them to find one ambiguity; fix it.

**You are done when you can** hand your SOW to someone who was not in the room and have them say exactly what will and will not be delivered and how it will be accepted.
