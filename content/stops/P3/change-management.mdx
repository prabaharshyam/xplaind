## What it is

**Change management** is the work of helping people move from the old way of working to the new one. For AI systems it covers:

- explaining why the change is happening and what it means for each person;
- involving users in design and testing;
- training that uses their own real cases;
- support in the first weeks, when habits form;
- measuring adoption and acting on what holds people back.

A common model, ADKAR, says each person needs **awareness** of why, **desire** to take part, **knowledge** of how, **ability** to do it in practice, and **reinforcement** so it sticks. If adoption stalls, find which of these is missing.

```text
   ADKAR: what each person needs                 what you do
   A  awareness      why the change?       -->   leaders explain the reason and what changes
   D  desire         do I want to?         -->   honest answers on jobs, blame, extra work
   K  knowledge      how does it work?     -->   45-minute training on their own cases
   A  ability        can I do it daily?    -->   champions, support channel, fix early annoyances
   R  reinforcement  will it stick?        -->   share results, update processes and targets

   adoption stalled?  find the missing letter for that team
```

This lesson follows a lender in Chennai where one branch adopted a credit-summary assistant quickly and another did not.

## How it works

**Name the worries.** Typical ones: "Will this replace me?", "Will I be blamed when the AI is wrong?", "Is this more work, checking its output?", "Is my work being monitored?" Answer them honestly with leaders present. If roles will change, say how.

**Clarify accountability.** Who is responsible when an AI-assisted decision is wrong? Usually the person who reviewed and approved it, as before. Put this in writing and make sure the review step gives them what they need to check properly.

**Involve champions.** Pick respected users from each team to test early versions and shape the design. Their feedback improves the system and their endorsement persuades peers better than any announcement.

**Train on real work.** Short sessions (45 minutes) using cases from their own queue: how to use it, what it is good at, where it fails, how to check, how to give feedback. Include examples of the system being wrong.

**Support the first weeks.** A visible channel for questions, a champion in each team, quick fixes to early annoyances. Early frustrations become permanent opinions if ignored.

**Measure and act.** Track usage per person and per team, feedback and reasons for not using it. Talk to low users; their reasons are usually fixable (a slow step, a missing field, a manager who never mentioned it).

**Reinforce.** Share results (time saved, fewer reworks), credit teams, and update processes and targets so the new way is the normal way.

**Worries and honest answers:**

| Worry | Answer that helps |
| --- | --- |
| "Will this replace me?" | Say plainly what changes in roles, if anything, and what stays; leaders answer, not only the project team |
| "Will I be blamed when it is wrong?" | Written accountability: officers own review of flagged and critical fields, with the tool showing sources side by side |
| "Is this more work?" | Show measured time per file before and after, from colleagues in a similar team |
| "Am I being monitored?" | State what is measured, why, and who sees it; measure adoption by team, not to rank individuals |

**An adoption dashboard.** Show, per team and per week: share of eligible work done with the tool, median time per item, rework rate, and the top feedback reasons. Add a short note column for actions taken. When a team falls below an agreed level, such as 50%, someone visits that week. The dashboard is for finding where help is needed, not for blaming teams.

**Training that sticks.** Use cases from the team's own queue, including one where the system is wrong, and let people try it during the session rather than watching a demo. End with where to ask questions and who their champion is. Repeat the session for anyone who missed it, because people who skip training are often the ones who later say the tool does not work.

## A worked example

At the Chennai lender, three weeks into the pilot, adoption is 92% in the Anna Nagar branch and 41% in Tambaram.

You visit Tambaram and talk to the officers. Three reasons emerge:

- The branch manager told them that any figure error would be "their responsibility, AI or not", and many decided it was safer to retype everything.
- Two officers had missed the training.
- The draft summary used English headings different from the format their regional credit head prefers.

Actions: the head of operations sends a note clarifying that officers are accountable for reviewing flagged and critical fields, and that the side-by-side view is the approved way to check; the branch manager joins a session where officers show how they verify fields. A repeat training is held. The summary template gains a regional variant. Within two weeks Tambaram reaches 85%, and its median file time drops from 37 to 25 minutes.

| Branch measure | Before visit | Two weeks after |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Adoption at Tambaram | 41% | 85% |
| Median file time at Tambaram | 37 minutes | 25 minutes |
| Causes fixed | Blame message, missed training, template mismatch | Clarified note, repeat training, regional template |

## Common mistakes

- **Launch by email.** An announcement is not adoption.
- **Avoiding the job question.** Silence breeds rumours. Be honest about what changes.
- **Training on generic demos.** Use people's own cases, including failures.
- **Unclear accountability.** If people fear blame, they will not trust or use the tool.
- **Not visiting low adopters.** The reasons are local and usually easy to fix.

## Practise

Read Prosci's description of the ADKAR model.

1. For your use case, list the likely worries of three groups (front-line staff, their managers, risk or compliance) and how you would address each.
2. Plan a 45-minute training session using real cases, including one where the system is wrong.
3. Design an adoption dashboard: what you would measure per team, and what you would do if a team falls below 50%.

**You are done when you can** explain, for a stalled team, which ADKAR element is missing and what you would do this week to fix it.
