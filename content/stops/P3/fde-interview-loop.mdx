## What it is

Companies hiring forward deployed engineers (AI labs, AI startups, data platforms, and consultancies building AI practices) use loops that vary, but most include some of these rounds:

- **Coding**: practical rather than puzzle-heavy. Parse a messy file, call an API, write a small service, debug code.
- **Problem decomposition**: a vague business problem ("a hospital wants to reduce discharge delays") and 45 minutes to turn it into questions, a plan and a design.
- **System design**: an AI system end to end: data, retrieval, models, evals, deployment, security, cost.
- **Client role-play or case**: an interviewer plays a client who is sceptical, demanding or changing requirements; you must listen, clarify, push back and agree next steps.
- **Behavioural**: past situations showing ownership, conflict, ambiguity, failure and learning.
- **Take-home or work sample**: a small project or a written proposal, sometimes presented.

```text
   FDE LOOP
   [coding]  practical: parse, call an API, small service, structured output, tests
   [decomposition]  vague problem -> questions -> KPI + baseline -> options -> POC + gate -> risks
   [system design]  data -> retrieval -> models -> evals -> guardrails -> deploy -> cost
   [client role-play]  listen -> summarise -> need behind the ask -> options -> next steps
   [behavioural]  6-8 STAR stories, each with a number and a lesson
   [take-home]  small project or proposal, sometimes presented
```

This lesson follows Arjun preparing for an FDE loop at an AI startup that sells to Indian banks.

## How it works

**Coding.** Practise realistic tasks in Python: reading CSV and JSON with bad rows, writing a FastAPI endpoint with validation, using an LLM API with structured output and retries, writing tests. Talk while you work; clarity matters as much as speed.

**Problem decomposition.** Use the discovery mindset aloud:

1. Clarify the goal and who cares about it.
2. Ask about the current process, volumes and data.
3. Propose a measurable KPI and baseline.
4. List options, including non-AI ones, and pick one with reasons.
5. Plan a proof of concept with a gate.
6. Name risks and how you would reduce them.

Interviewers look for structured thinking and good questions, not a perfect answer.

**System design.** Cover the full path: ingestion, retrieval, prompting, evals, guardrails, permissions, deployment model, observability, cost, failure handling. Make trade-offs explicit ("hybrid search here because users type policy codes").

**Client role-play.** Listen first and summarise what you heard. Ask about the underlying need behind a demand. Do not promise on the spot; offer options and trade-offs. Stay calm when the "client" pushes. Close with agreed next steps.

**Behavioural.** Prepare six to eight stories using STAR (situation, task, action, result), with numbers and what you learned. Cover: a project you drove end to end, a time you disagreed with a stakeholder, a failure, a time you said no, working with ambiguity, learning something fast.

**Your own questions.** Ask about how much time FDEs spend travelling or on-site, how projects are chosen, how FDEs feed back into the product, and how success is measured.

**Role-play moves that score well:**

| Situation | Weak response | Strong response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Make it live for all branches next week" | "Sure, we'll try" or "Impossible" | Ask what drives the date; name risks; offer a board demo or a partial rollout |
| "The AI made a mistake; we're cancelling" | Defend the model | Ask what happened, look at the case, explain safeguards and a fix plan |
| "Add this feature too" | Agree on the spot | "Let me size it and come back with options by Thursday" |
| Silence after your proposal | Keep talking | Ask a direct question: "Which of these fits your board timeline?" |

**A STAR story with numbers.** Situation: "Our team's monthly sales report took three days of manual spreadsheet work." Task: "I was asked to speed it up." Action: "I mapped the steps, automated the data pulls and checks in Python, and added a validation step the analysts reviewed." Result: "It now takes four hours, and errors found after release dropped from five a month to none in three months." Lesson: "I should have involved the analysts earlier; their checks caught two edge cases I missed."

**Preparing decomposition.** Pick Indian business problems, set a 30-minute timer and speak your questions aloud before any solution. Record and replay: count how many minutes passed before you proposed something. If it was under five, you jumped too fast.

## A worked example

A candidate, Arjun, prepares for an FDE loop at an AI startup selling to Indian banks.

For coding he practises four timed tasks: a statement parser, a FastAPI endpoint, an extraction call with Pydantic validation and retries, and fixing a failing test.

For decomposition he practises with prompts like "a state transport corporation wants fewer complaints", speaking his questions aloud and timing himself.

In the client role-play, the interviewer says: "Your pilot is too slow. Our MD wants it live for all branches next week." Arjun summarises the concern, asks what triggered the deadline (a board meeting), explains what a full rollout next week would risk (security review not done, two branches under 50% adoption) and offers options: a board demo next week with pilot results and a three-wave rollout plan, or a rollout to the five best-prepared branches. The interviewer later says this was the strongest part of his loop: he neither caved nor refused.

His behavioural stories each have a number: "cut report preparation from 3 days to 4 hours", "reduced failed jobs from 12% to 1%".

| Preparation | What Arjun did |
| --- | --- |
| Coding | Four timed tasks: a statement parser, a FastAPI endpoint, extraction with validation and retries, a failing test fixed |
| Decomposition | Timed practice on prompts like "a state transport corporation wants fewer complaints" |
| Role-play | Offered a board demo or a five-branch rollout instead of caving or refusing |
| Behavioural | Stories with numbers, such as reports cut from 3 days to 4 hours |

## Common mistakes

- **Preparing only for coding.** Decomposition and role-play rounds often decide FDE offers.
- **Jumping to a solution.** Ask questions first, aloud.
- **Saying yes to everything in role-play.** Interviewers test whether you can manage expectations.
- **Stories without results.** Every story needs a number or a clear outcome, and a lesson.
- **Generic system designs.** Tie each choice to the scenario's constraints.

## Practise

Read the Tech Interview Handbook's behavioural guide and the a16z essay on the FDE role.

1. Write six STAR stories from your own work or projects, each with a result and a lesson.
2. Do three timed decomposition exercises (30 minutes each) on Indian business problems you choose, speaking aloud and recording yourself.
3. Ask a friend to play a difficult client using one of the scenarios in this path's lessons; practise listening, clarifying, offering options and closing.

**You are done when you can** decompose an unseen business problem into questions, a KPI and a POC plan in 30 minutes, out loud.
