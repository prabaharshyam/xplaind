## What it is

Three sets of rules matter most for people in India:

- **India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (DPDP Act)**, with rules notified in November 2025 and phased in over about eighteen months. It governs how organisations collect and use your digital personal data, including in AI systems.
- **India's AI Governance Guidelines**, released by the Ministry of Electronics and IT in November 2025. They set principles for safe and trusted AI but do not create a separate AI law; instead they rely on existing laws and sector regulators, such as the RBI for banks.
- **The European Union's AI Act**, the first comprehensive AI law, which affects Indian companies whose AI products are used in Europe and influences rules elsewhere.

Other existing laws still apply to AI: the IT Act, consumer protection, criminal law for fraud and impersonation, and sector rules for banking, insurance and health.

## How it works

**Your rights under the DPDP Act, in short.**

- Organisations must tell you clearly what personal data they collect and why, and generally get your consent.
- You can withdraw consent as easily as you gave it.
- You can ask to see a summary of your data, correct it, and have it erased when it is no longer needed.
- They must protect your data and tell you and the Data Protection Board of India if there is a breach.
- Children's data needs a parent's consent, and children may not be tracked or targeted with advertising.
- Large penalties apply to organisations that break the law, up to ₹250 crore for failing to protect data.
- You can complain to the organisation and then to the Data Protection Board.

**India's AI guidelines in short.** They put people first and promote trust, fairness, accountability, transparency and safety, and recommend institutions and voluntary measures for industry. They lean towards encouraging innovation while managing risk through existing laws.

**The EU AI Act in short.** It sorts AI uses by risk. A few uses are banned (such as social scoring by governments). High-risk uses, such as AI in hiring, credit, education and essential services, must meet strict requirements: risk management, good data, human oversight, records and transparency. Chatbots must tell people they are AI, and AI-generated content must be identifiable in certain cases.

**What it means for you.** Read what an app says it does with your data before agreeing. Use your rights to see, correct or delete data. Expect to be told when you are dealing with an AI in many situations. If an AI decision affects you badly (a rejected loan or application), ask how it was made and how to appeal.

## A worked example

Deepa applies for a personal loan through a lending app. The app asks for access to her contacts, photos and SMS.

Knowing her rights, she reads the notice. It says contacts are used "to improve services". She declines that permission; the app still processes her application. Later, she is rejected and suspects an automated decision. She writes to the lender's grievance officer asking how the decision was made and requesting a human review. The lender explains the main reasons and a person reviews the file.

A month later, she stops using the app and asks it to delete her data; it confirms deletion. When a friend's data appears in a leak from another app, Deepa helps her check what the company has disclosed and where to complain.

## Common mistakes

- **Accepting every permission.** Many apps ask for more than they need.
- **Thinking AI is unregulated.** Data protection, consumer and sector laws already apply.
- **Not using your rights.** You can ask to see, correct and delete data.
- **Assuming EU rules do not matter in India.** Indian products serving Europe must follow them, and they shape global practice.
- **Taking automated rejections as final.** Ask for reasons and human review.

## Practise

Read PRS's summary of the DPDP law and the EU AI Act high-level summary.

1. Pick two apps you use and read their privacy notices. Write down what data they collect, why and how to delete it.
2. Check one app's permissions on your phone and turn off any it does not need.
3. Write, in three sentences, what you would say to a company to request your data be erased.

**You are done when you can** explain to a friend three rights they have over their personal data and one thing the EU AI Act bans.
