## What it is

Many people who start learning AI stall because they have no clear route: a video here, a course there, a new tool every week, and after three months no finished skill. Choosing a path fixes that.

A **path** on Xplaind is an ordered route to one job role, built from shared lessons. Each path is split into stages. Many stages come with a guided project, and every path has at least one guided project you can show an employer. Lessons you finish count on **every** path that uses them, so switching later does not waste your work.

Xplaind has ten paths. The length column is Xplaind's rough guide for steady study. It is learning time only: plan for a job search of a few months on top.

| Path | Who it is for | Coding | Rough length | Level test |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **AI Engineer** | Students and developers who want a broad AI engineering role | Yes, from day one | 9 months | Yes |
| **Generative AI & LLM Engineer** | Newcomers and engineers who want to build apps on language models, with RAG in depth | Yes, from level B2 | 9 months | Yes |
| **Agentic AI Engineer** | Developers who can already call a language model API from Python | Yes | 4 months | Yes |
| **Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE)** | Engineers with a year or more of Python who like client work | Yes | 6 months | Yes |
| **Machine Learning Engineer** | Graduates and engineers comfortable with Python and school maths | Yes, plus maths | 9 months | No |
| **MLOps Engineer** | Backend, DevOps, data and cloud engineers | Yes | 6 months | Yes |
| **AI Security Engineer** | Students, developers and IT or security staff; starts from zero security knowledge | Some (light Python) | 6 months | No |
| **AI Automation Specialist** | Operations, support, sales and marketing people, freelancers, small business owners | Some (APIs, JSON, small scripts) | 3 months | No |
| **AI Product Manager** | Product managers, business analysts, founders and consultants | None | 3 months | No |
| **AI Governance & Compliance Specialist** | Risk, compliance, legal, audit, privacy and policy people | None | 3 months | No |

Two things to know before you choose:

- **You may not need a path at all.** If you want to use AI to do your current job faster, and you do not want a new role, start with the B1 using-AI lessons in the Library instead: prompting, writing and summarising, research, and checking AI answers.
- **A whole roadmap needs an account.** Signed out, you see the first stage of each path. Roadmaps and lessons are free with an account, which also saves your path and progress. Accounts are for people aged 18 and over; if you are under 18, the quick answer and the first part of every lesson stay open in the Library.

This lesson follows Imran, an accountant in Hyderabad, as he picks his path. By the end you will have chosen yours, planned your hours and set a first milestone.

## How it works

Choosing well takes one question and then three checks, in this order: **new role or not, then background, goal and time.**

```flow
dir: LR
title: How to choose a path
Start | do I want a new role? -[no]-> Using-AI lessons | work faster in my current job
Start -[yes]-> 1. Background | code, maths, a domain?
1. Background -> 2. Goal | do ten real job posts fit me?
2. Goal -> 3. Time | hours I can keep for months
3. Time -> Your path
```

### Check 1: start from your background

Start from what you already have, not from the role with the most buzz. Each path assumes a starting point, so match yours to it:

| Your starting point | Start with | What the path assumes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| You want to decide what AI to build, and you do not want to code | AI Product Manager | No code; you learn enough to work well with engineers |
| You work in risk, legal, audit, compliance or privacy | AI Governance & Compliance | No code; your domain knowledge is the advantage |
| You run business processes and will learn light code | AI Automation Specialist | You will read APIs and JSON and write small scripts |
| You work in IT support, networking or security, or security interests you | AI Security Engineer | Light Python; security basics are taught from zero |
| You are new to code but ready to learn Python for language-model apps | Generative AI & LLM Engineer | Starts with AI basics; Python from level B2 |
| You code, or will learn Python now, and want the broadest role | AI Engineer | Python from the first stage |
| You already call a language model API from Python | Agentic AI Engineer | Starts at tool calling, so it is short |
| You have a year or more of Python and enjoy client meetings | Forward Deployed Engineer | Production-level Python from stage 1 |
| You know Python and enjoy maths, statistics and data | Machine Learning Engineer | Python and school maths |
| You are a backend, DevOps, data or cloud engineer | MLOps Engineer | Linux, containers and cloud come fast to you |

Check the assumption, not the label. A fresher who picks Agentic AI Engineer without ever having called an API hits a wall in week one; the GenAI path builds up to agents instead.

### Three engineering paths that look alike

AI Engineer, Generative AI & LLM Engineer and Agentic AI Engineer overlap the most. They share many lessons but start in different places and stress different things:

| | AI Engineer | Generative AI & LLM Engineer | Agentic AI Engineer |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| First stage | Python and data foundations | AI basics, no code (Python from B2) | Model APIs, structured output, tool calling |
| Covers | Classical ML, deep learning and transformers, then LLM apps, testing, production, system design | Nine levels from newcomer to system designer: prompts, RAG in depth, agents, evals, production | Agent design, frameworks, MCP, reliability, production |
| Length | 9 months | 9 months | 4 months |
| Pick it if | You want the broad role; many Indian AI engineer posts also ask for ML basics | You are sure you want language-model apps, or you are starting from zero code | You already build with language models and want depth in agents |

**Decision rule:** if you code and cannot decide, take AI Engineer, the broadest. From zero code, take the GenAI path, which starts gentlest. Take Agentic AI Engineer as a second step, once you can call an API. FDE is similar engineering plus client work, for people with a year or more of Python.

### Check 2: test the goal against real jobs

Before you commit months, spend an evening on ten real job posts. Search Naukri or LinkedIn for the role in your city and note four columns:

| Title | City or remote | Years asked | Top three skills or duties |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| AI Automation Engineer | Hyderabad | 1 to 3 | n8n, APIs, process mapping |

Read the duties, not the title: the lesson "AI careers in India" shows how a post called "AI engineer" can really be ML work. Then apply three rules:

- **Do the duties excite you?** If most of the ten posts describe work you would enjoy every week, keep the goal. If not, look at another path now, not in month four.
- **Do most posts ask for three or more years?** Then plan a bridge: a junior role nearby, or an AI project inside your current company first. For example, most FDE and AI product manager posts ask for prior engineering or product experience.
- **Are there almost no posts in your city?** Check remote posts, the nearest hub and the careers pages of global capability centres (GCCs), which often hire directly.

### Freshers and working professionals

- **Freshers** do best on paths with real entry-level hiring and a portfolio of projects: AI Engineer, GenAI, ML Engineer and AI Automation. The Jobs page lists typical pay for 0 to 2 years (checked 2 November 2026), for example 6 to 12 lakh a year CTC for AI Engineer and 4 to 8 lakh for AI Automation Specialist. The higher early bands for FDE and AI Product Manager rarely go to freshers.
- **Working professionals** switch fastest on the path that reuses what they know: DevOps into MLOps, compliance into governance, a Java developer into FDE. The quickest route is often an AI pilot inside your own company.

### Check 3: be honest about time

There is no deadline. Every path works at your own pace, so pick the number of hours a week you can keep up for months, not the most you can manage in a good week:

| Weekly hours | What it feels like |
| --- | --- |
| 3 hours | One short lesson a week, done properly |
| 5 hours | A lesson and its practice each week |
| 8 hours | Two lessons and a small project step |
| 12 hours | Steady progress through a stage, with time for projects |

To turn hours into a date, do a quick sum: each lesson page shows its hours, so add up a stage, divide by your weekly hours and add about a third for practice. On the five paths with a level test (AI Engineer, GenAI, Agentic AI, FDE and MLOps), press **Find my level** on the roadmap: the test skips what you already know, and the result shows about how many weeks the rest will take at your hours. The other five paths start at stage 1. Five steady hours beat twenty hours for two weeks followed by nothing.

### Make a plan

A plan has three parts:

1. **Fixed study slots** in your week, for example Tuesday and Thursday evenings and Sunday morning. Put them in your calendar like meetings, and note busy weeks you already know about, such as exams or a month-end close.
2. **A first milestone within four weeks** that you can show someone: a finished stage, a first workflow, a small working app. Check that its lesson hours fit inside four weeks of your slots, with room to spare.
3. **Accountability**: one named person, a fixed day for your update, and the update itself, such as one line on what you finished and what is next.

### Re-evaluate after the first stage

After stage 1, ask: am I enjoying this work, and is it moving me towards the role in the job posts? If not, switch. Your finished lessons carry over: for example, "What AI, machine learning and a language model are" sits on six of the ten paths. Do not switch because one lesson was hard or one week went badly. That is normal on every path.

## A worked example

Imran, 34, is an accountant at a distribution company in Hyderabad. He has used an AI assistant at work for a few months. He enjoyed using it to speed up bank reconciliations and wants to do more, but he has no wish to become a full-time programmer.

**New role or not?** He wants a role where automation is his main job, so the using-AI lessons alone are not enough.

**Background.** No coding, strong finance and process knowledge, comfortable with Excel formulas. Two paths look possible: AI Automation Specialist (some code) and AI Governance & Compliance (no code), since he knows financial controls.

**Goal.** He reads ten job posts in Hyderabad, five for each. His notes (an illustration, not a survey) show the governance posts mostly ask for risk or compliance experience he does not have, and describe policy writing he finds dull. The automation posts, titled "AI automation engineer" or "finance automation analyst", mention n8n, Power Automate, APIs and process mapping. Their duties read "automate invoice processing" and "reduce manual reconciliation", which is exactly what he enjoyed. Most ask for 0 to 3 years. He accepts that he will need to read APIs and JSON and write small scripts.

**Time.** He has two young children. Realistically: two weekday evenings and one weekend morning, about 5 to 6 hours a week. The path's rough guide is three months; at his pace it may run a little longer once projects are counted.

**Checking the milestone.** Stage 1 of the automation path has seven lessons, about 10 hours in all. The first lesson of stage 2, "Your first n8n workflow", is about 3 hours more. That is 13 hours of lessons. Four weeks at 5.5 hours gives 22 hours, which leaves 9 hours for practice and rework. The milestone fits.

His plan:

| Part | Imran's plan |
| --- | --- |
| Path | AI Automation Specialist |
| Study slots | Tuesday and Thursday 8 to 9:30 pm, Saturday 9 to 11:30 am (5.5 hours) |
| First milestone | In four weeks: stage 1 done, plus his first n8n workflow that logs invoice emails to a sheet |
| Accountability | A short update every Sunday to a colleague who is also learning |
| Final project | The path's capstone, an invoice-to-ledger automation, built on his team's real invoice process |

**What went wrong.** In week 3 the month-end close took two of his evenings, and he missed his milestone date. He did not drop the plan. He moved the date back a week, told his colleague, and marked every month-end week as a light week.

**The move that paid off.** He told his manager about the plan. His manager offered the team's invoice process for the capstone, on one condition: Imran builds and tests it on copies with vendor bank details removed, and gets approval before it touches the live ledger. His final project is now real work with a measured result, which will make his portfolio far stronger than a practice exercise.

## Common mistakes

- **Picking the most hyped or highest-paid path.** Pick the one that fits your background and the work you would enjoy every week.
- **Ignoring what a path assumes.** Agentic AI Engineer expects you to call an API from Python, and FDE expects a year of Python. Start one step earlier if you cannot do that yet.
- **Taking a career path when you only want to use AI at work.** The using-AI lessons get you there in a few hours, not months.
- **Planning unrealistic hours.** Choose hours you can keep for months, not the hours you wish you had.
- **Setting a milestone you have not added up.** Sum the lesson hours first, or the milestone will slip and you will feel behind.
- **Switching paths every week.** Commit to one stage before reconsidering.
- **Skipping projects.** Projects are what employers want to see, and where real confidence comes from.
- **Learning alone.** A fixed weekly update to one person makes it much harder to drift quietly.
- **Waiting for the perfect plan.** A good plan you start this week beats a perfect plan you start next month.
