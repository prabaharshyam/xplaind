## What it is

The most common reason people give up on learning AI is not that it is too hard. It is that they start without a clear route: a YouTube video here, a course there, a new tool every week, and no sense of progress. Choosing a path fixes that.

Xplaind has nine paths. Each is an ordered route through shared lessons, with a project at the end of every stage. Lessons you finish count on **every** path that uses them, so switching later does not waste your work.

| Path | Who it is for | Coding |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **AI Engineer** | People who want a broad AI engineering role: data, ML, LLM apps and production | Yes |
| **Generative AI & LLM Engineer** | People building applications on language models, with RAG in depth, from Python basics to system design | Yes |
| **Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE)** | Engineers who build AI systems with and for clients | Yes |
| **AI Product Manager** | People who decide what AI to build, measure it and ship it responsibly | None |
| **Agentic AI Engineer** | Engineers designing and running AI agents safely | Yes |
| **AI Automation Specialist** | People who want to automate business workflows with n8n and similar tools, plus AI steps | Light |
| **AI Governance & Compliance Specialist** | Risk, compliance, legal and policy people who keep AI safe, fair and lawful | None |
| **Machine Learning Engineer** | People training classical and deep learning models | Yes, plus maths |
| **MLOps Engineer** | Engineers running ML and LLM systems reliably at scale | Yes |

This lesson follows Imran, an accountant in Hyderabad, as he picks his path after a few months of using AI at work. By the end you will have chosen a path, planned your weekly hours and set a first milestone.

## How it works

Choosing well takes three checks, in this order: **background, goal, time.**

```text
  1. Background          2. Goal                 3. Time
 +---------------+     +-----------------+     +------------------+
 | What do I     |     | Do real job     |     | How many hours   |
 | already know? | --> | posts for this  | --> | a week can I     |
 | Coding? Maths?|     | role excite me? |     | keep for months? |
 | Domain?       |     |                 |     |                  |
 +---------------+     +-----------------+     +------------------+
          \                    |                       /
           +-------------> Your path <----------------+
```

### Check 1: start from your background

```text
  Can you code, or are you willing to learn Python?
        |
   no --+--> Build workflows with AI?             --> AI Automation Specialist
        |    Decide what AI to build?             --> AI Product Manager
        |    Risk, legal or compliance work?      --> AI Governance & Compliance
        |
  yes --+--> Strong maths or data background?     --> Machine Learning Engineer
        |    Come from DevOps or cloud?           --> MLOps Engineer
        |    Enjoy working with clients?          --> Forward Deployed Engineer
        |    Want depth in agents?                --> Agentic AI Engineer
        |    Want depth in LLMs and RAG?          --> Generative AI & LLM Engineer
        |    Not sure yet?                        --> AI Engineer (broadest)
```

### Check 2: test the goal against real jobs

Open a job site and read ten real job descriptions for the role the path leads to. If the day-to-day work excites you, good. If it sounds dull, look at another path before you invest months. This ten-minute check saves many people from the most hyped path that does not suit them.

### Check 3: be honest about time

Each path shows an estimate. Divide by your real weekly hours:

| Weekly hours | A 60-hour stage takes | A 300-hour path takes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 3 hours | about 5 months | about 2 years |
| 5 hours | about 3 months | about 14 months |
| 8 hours | about 2 months | about 9 months |
| 12 hours | about 5 weeks | about 6 months |

Five steady hours beat twenty hours for two weeks followed by nothing. On engineering paths, take the level test to skip what you already know.

### Make a plan

A plan has three parts:

1. **Fixed study slots** in your week, for example Tuesday and Thursday evenings and Sunday morning. Put them in your calendar like meetings.
2. **A first milestone within four weeks**, such as finishing stage 1 or building its project.
3. **Accountability**: a friend learning with you, a study group, or sharing your progress every week.

### Re-evaluate after the first stage

After stage 1, ask: am I enjoying this, and is it moving me towards the role? If not, switch. Your finished lessons carry over, so you lose very little.

## A worked example

Imran, 34, is an accountant at a distribution company in Hyderabad. He has been using an AI assistant at work for a few months. He enjoyed using it to speed up bank reconciliations and wants to do more, but he has no interest in becoming a programmer.

**Background.** No coding, strong finance and process knowledge, comfortable with Excel formulas.

**Goal.** He reads ten job posts. Roles titled "AI automation specialist" and "finance automation analyst" mention n8n, Power Automate, APIs and process mapping, and describe work like "automate invoice processing" and "reduce manual reconciliation". That matches exactly what he enjoyed.

**Time.** He has two young children. Realistically: two weekday evenings and one weekend morning, about 5 to 6 hours a week. The AI Automation Specialist path takes about three months at that pace.

His plan:

| Part | Imran's plan |
| --- | --- |
| Path | AI Automation Specialist |
| Study slots | Tuesday and Thursday 8 to 9:30 pm, Saturday 9 to 11 am |
| First milestone | In four weeks: his first n8n workflow that reads invoice emails and logs them to a sheet |
| Accountability | Shares a short update every Sunday with a colleague also learning |
| Final project | A real reconciliation process his manager offered when Imran told him about the plan |

Telling his manager turned out to be the best move: his final project is now real work, which will make his portfolio far stronger than a practice exercise.

## Common mistakes

- **Picking the most hyped path.** Pick the one that fits your background and the work you would enjoy.
- **Planning unrealistic hours.** Choose hours you can keep for months, not the hours you wish you had.
- **Switching paths every week.** Commit to one stage before reconsidering.
- **Skipping projects.** Projects are what employers want to see, and they are where real confidence comes from.
- **Learning alone without accountability.** A weekly check-in with one person makes you much more likely to keep going.
- **Waiting for the perfect plan.** A good plan you start this week beats a perfect plan you start next month.
