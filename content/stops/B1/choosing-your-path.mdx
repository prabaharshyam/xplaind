## What it is

Xplaind has nine paths. Each is an ordered route through shared lessons, with a project at the end of every stage. Lessons you finish count on every path that uses them, so switching later does not waste your work.


The nine, briefly:

- **AI for Beginners** (this path): AI literacy for everyone. No coding.
- **AI Automation Specialist**: automate business workflows with n8n and similar tools, plus AI steps. Light code.
- **AI Product Manager**: decide what AI to build, measure it and ship it responsibly. No coding.
- **GenAI Engineer**: build applications on language models, from Python basics to system design.
- **Forward Deployed Engineer**: build AI systems with and for clients, including deployment, security and client craft.
- **Agentic AI Expert**: design and run AI agents safely.
- **RAG Expert**: build search and question-answering over company documents.
- **LLMOps / AI Platform Engineer**: run AI systems reliably at scale.
- **ML Engineer**: classical machine learning and deep learning, with MLOps.

## How it works

**Start from your background.**

- No coding, and you want to use AI in your current work: you are on the right path; consider AI Automation Specialist or AI Product Manager next.
- Some coding, or willing to learn Python: GenAI Engineer is the broadest start.
- Working software engineer: GenAI Engineer, or FDE if you enjoy client work, Agentic or RAG if you want depth, LLMOps if you come from DevOps or cloud.
- Strong maths or data background: ML Engineer.

**Then check the goal.** Look at real job descriptions for the role the path leads to. If they excite you, good. If not, look at another path.

**Then check the time.** Each path shows an estimate. Be honest about your weekly hours: 5 steady hours beat 20 hours for two weeks and then nothing. Use the level test on engineering paths to skip what you already know.

**Make a plan.**

- Fixed study slots in your week (for example, Tuesday and Thursday evenings, Sunday morning).
- A first milestone within four weeks: finish stage 1 or its project.
- A way to stay accountable: a friend learning too, or sharing progress weekly.

**Re-evaluate after the first stage.** It is fine to switch; your finished lessons carry over.

## A worked example

Imran, 34, an accountant in Hyderabad, finishes AI for Beginners. He enjoys using AI for reconciliations and wants to do more, but has no interest in becoming a programmer.

He reads ten job posts: "AI automation specialist" and "finance automation analyst" roles mention n8n, Power Automate, APIs and process mapping. That matches what he enjoyed.

He chooses the AI Automation Specialist path (about three months at 5 to 6 hours a week). His plan: Tuesday and Thursday 8 to 9:30 pm, Saturday 9 to 11 am. First milestone in four weeks: his first n8n workflow that reads invoice emails and logs them to a sheet. He tells his manager, who offers a real reconciliation process for his final project.

## Common mistakes

- **Picking the most hyped path.** Pick the one that fits you.
- **Planning unrealistic hours.** Choose hours you can keep for months.
- **Switching paths every week.** Commit to one stage before reconsidering.
- **Skipping projects.** Projects are what employers and your own confidence need.
- **Learning alone without any accountability.**
