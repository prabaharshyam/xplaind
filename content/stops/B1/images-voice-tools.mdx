## What it is

Generative AI now works with more than text:

- **Images**: create pictures from a description, edit photos (remove a background, extend a picture), and describe or read images (a photo of a bill, a chart).
- **Speech**: transcribe audio to text, translate speech, read text aloud in natural voices.
- **Meetings**: record, transcribe and summarise calls with action items.
- **Video and music**: generate short clips and soundtracks, still improving fast.

Many of these are built into tools you already use: phone cameras, office suites, video-call apps and chat assistants.

## How it works

**Images.** Describe what you want: subject, setting, style, colours, text to include and the size (square for social media, wide for a banner). Image tools still struggle with exact text, hands and specific real people, and they may refuse some requests. For anything public, check the tool's rules on commercial use.

**Reading images.** Take a photo of a handwritten list, a medicine strip or a timetable and ask the assistant to read or explain it. Check anything important; small or handwritten text is often misread.

**Speech to text.** Transcription is good for clear English and improving for Indian languages and mixed speech, though accents, noise and Hinglish still cause errors. Useful for interviews, lectures and voice notes.

**Meeting assistants.** They join calls, transcribe and summarise. Tell everyone in the meeting that it is being recorded, follow your organisation's rules, and check the summary before sharing.

**Voice generation.** Text-to-speech can produce natural announcements in several Indian languages. Cloning a real person's voice without consent is unethical and, in many cases, illegal; the next lessons on deepfakes cover the risks.

**Responsible use.** Do not create images or audio of real people saying or doing things they did not. Label AI-made images where it matters. Respect copyright: do not pass off AI copies of an artist's work as original. Keep private recordings and photos of others out of unapproved tools.

## A worked example

Anitha runs a home bakery in Tiruchirappalli and wants to promote Diwali sweet boxes.

- She describes the poster: "A festive square poster with a gift box of Indian sweets, warm gold and maroon colours, diyas in the background, space at the top for text." The image tool gives four options; she picks one. The text it added is misspelt, so she adds the words herself in a free design app.
- She records a voice note describing the box contents and prices in Tamil, transcribes it, and asks the assistant to turn it into a short English and Tamil caption. She fixes two words in the Tamil.
- She uses a phone photo of a supplier's handwritten invoice to get the item list into a spreadsheet, then checks each line against the paper.

She does not use a well-known actor's face on the poster, even though a friend suggests it.

## Common mistakes

- **Trusting text inside AI images.** Add important text yourself.
- **Recording meetings without telling people.**
- **Using real people's faces or voices without consent.**
- **Assuming transcripts are exact.** Check names, numbers and Indian-language words.
- **Ignoring commercial-use rules** for images you will sell or advertise with.

## Practise

Do Microsoft Learn's short module on generative AI, and read one image tool's help page.

1. Create a poster for a real or imaginary event and fix any errors in the text by hand.
2. Record a one-minute voice note in your language, transcribe it with an AI tool and count the errors.
3. Photograph a printed or handwritten list and have an assistant read it; check every line.

**You are done when you can** name three non-chat AI tools you used, what each got wrong, and one thing you would not do with them.
