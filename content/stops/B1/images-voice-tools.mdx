## What it is

Most people meet generative AI as a chat box. But the same technology now works with pictures, sound and video, and much of it is already inside apps you use every day: your phone camera, office suite, video-call app and chat assistant.

```text
                    Generative AI beyond chat
 +----------------+----------------+----------------+----------------+
 |    Images      |    Speech      |   Meetings     |  Video, music  |
 | create, edit,  | transcribe,    | record,        | short clips,   |
 | read a photo   | translate,     | transcribe,    | soundtracks    |
 | of a bill      | read aloud     | summarise      | (still early)  |
 +----------------+----------------+----------------+----------------+
```

| Kind | What it does | Everyday example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Image creation** | Makes a picture from a description | A festival poster for a small business |
| **Image editing** | Removes a background, extends a picture, fixes lighting | A product photo on a clean white background |
| **Image reading** | Describes or reads text in a photo | Reading a medicine strip, a bill or a timetable |
| **Speech to text** | Turns audio into written text | Transcribing an interview or lecture |
| **Text to speech** | Reads text aloud in a natural voice | An announcement in Hindi or Tamil |
| **Meeting assistants** | Join calls, transcribe and summarise with actions | Notes and action items from a team call |
| **Video and music** | Generate short clips and soundtracks | A ten-second product teaser |

Models that read images as well as text are called **vision-language models**. They are what lets you photograph a handwritten list and ask an assistant to turn it into a spreadsheet.

This lesson follows Anitha, who runs a home bakery in Tiruchirappalli, as she uses these tools for a Diwali campaign. By the end you will know what each tool is good at, where each fails, and the rules for using them responsibly.

## How it works

### Creating images

Image generators work from a written description called a prompt. Good prompts are specific:

```text
  Weak:   "Diwali sweets poster"

  Strong: subject    a gift box of Indian sweets: kaju katli, ladoo
          setting    on a wooden table, diyas glowing behind
          style      warm, festive photo style
          colours    gold and maroon
          layout     square, empty space at the top for text
```

Image tools still struggle with **exact text** inside the picture, **hands** and **specific real people**, and they may refuse some requests. For anything you will sell or advertise with, check the tool's rules on commercial use.

### Reading images

Take a photo of a handwritten list, a bill, a medicine strip or a train timetable and ask an assistant to read or explain it. This saves a lot of typing. But small, faded or handwritten text is often misread, so check anything important against the original.

### Speech to text and translation

Transcription is very good for clear English and improving fast for Indian languages and mixed speech. It still makes errors with:

- strong accents and fast speech;
- background noise, such as traffic or a busy shop;
- Hinglish and other mixed-language speech;
- names, places and technical words.

It is useful for interviews, lectures and voice notes. Always check names and numbers.

### Meeting assistants

These join a video call, transcribe it and write a summary with action items. Three rules:

1. **Tell everyone** in the meeting that it is being recorded.
2. **Follow your organisation's rules** on which tools may join which meetings.
3. **Check the summary** before sharing; it can miss or misstate decisions.

### Voice generation

Text-to-speech can produce natural announcements in several Indian languages, which is great for shops, schools and public notices. But **cloning a real person's voice** without consent is unethical and in many cases illegal. Cloned voices are a main tool of the scams covered in the lesson on deepfakes.

### Responsible use

| Do | Do not |
| --- | --- |
| Add important text to images yourself | Trust text the image tool wrote |
| Label AI-made images where it matters, such as news or ads | Pass off AI copies of an artist's style as your own original art |
| Get consent before recording people | Record meetings secretly |
| Use generic people in images | Create images of real people doing things they did not do |
| Keep private photos out of unapproved tools | Upload other people's private photos or recordings |

## A worked example

Anitha runs a home bakery in Tiruchirappalli. She wants to promote Diwali sweet boxes on Instagram and WhatsApp, and she has one evening to do it.

**The poster.** She describes it: "A festive square poster with a gift box of Indian sweets, warm gold and maroon colours, diyas in the background, space at the top for text." The image tool gives four options. She picks one. The text it added says "Happy Diwalli", misspelt, so she removes it and adds the words, prices and her phone number herself in a free design app.

**The caption.** She records a voice note describing the box contents and prices in Tamil, transcribes it with an AI tool, and asks the assistant to turn it into a short caption in English and Tamil. She fixes two Tamil words the transcription got wrong, including the name of a sweet.

**The supplier bill.** She takes a phone photo of her supplier's handwritten invoice and asks the assistant to put the items into a spreadsheet. She checks each line against the paper. One quantity was read as 15 instead of 75.

**A suggestion she refuses.** A friend suggests putting a well-known actor's face on the poster "to get more likes". Anitha says no: she has no permission, it would mislead customers, and the tool's rules forbid it.

| Task | Tool | What it got wrong | Her fix |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Poster | Image generator | Misspelt "Diwali" | Added text herself |
| Caption | Speech to text plus assistant | Two Tamil words, one sweet name | Edited by hand |
| Supplier list | Image reading | 75 read as 15 | Checked every line |

## Common mistakes

- **Trusting text inside AI images.** Add important words yourself.
- **Recording meetings without telling people.** Always announce it and follow the rules.
- **Using real people's faces or voices without consent.** Even as a joke, it can harm them and break the law.
- **Assuming transcripts are exact.** Check names, numbers and Indian-language words.
- **Ignoring commercial-use rules** for images you will sell or advertise with.
- **Uploading other people's private photos** to unapproved tools.

## Practise

Do Microsoft Learn's short module on generative AI fundamentals, linked below, and read one image tool's help page.

1. **Make a poster.** Create a poster for a real or imaginary event, and fix any errors in its text by hand.
2. **Test transcription.** Record a one-minute voice note in your own language, transcribe it with an AI tool and count the errors.
3. **Read a list.** Photograph a printed or handwritten list and have an assistant read it. Check every line.

**You are done when you can** name three non-chat AI tools you used, what each got wrong, and one thing you would not do with them.
