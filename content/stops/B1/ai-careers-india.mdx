## What it is

"AI job" is not one job. Companies hire for several families of roles, and the titles change from company to company. A "GenAI developer" at one firm does what an "AI engineer" does at another. So the first rule of an AI career is: **read the job description, not just the title.**

Here is the map of the main roles, grouped by how much code they need:

```text
                  How much code the role needs
  none / little  <---------------------------------->  a lot

  AI-in-your-field   AI product     Automation     AI / GenAI engineer
  (HR, law, sales)   manager        specialist     Forward deployed engineer
  AI governance and                                Agent, RAG specialist
  compliance                                       MLOps / platform engineer
                                                   ML engineer, data scientist
```

### Building with AI (coding roles)

- **AI engineer or GenAI engineer** builds applications on language models: prompts, retrieval over documents (RAG), agents, evaluation and deployment. It is one of the fastest-growing AI titles.
- **Forward deployed engineer** is an AI engineer who works directly with customers, finds the problem worth solving and gets the system into daily use.
- **Agent and RAG specialists** go deep on building agents that take actions, or search over company documents.
- **MLOps, LLMOps or AI platform engineer** runs ML and AI systems reliably at scale: serving, monitoring, cost and security.
- **ML engineer and data scientist** train and deploy machine learning models for prediction, ranking, fraud and forecasting.

### Shaping and using AI (little or no coding)

- **AI product manager** decides which AI features to build and how to measure whether they work.
- **AI automation specialist** automates business workflows with low-code tools and AI steps.
- **AI governance and compliance specialist** checks that AI is safe, fair and lawful: risk assessments, policy, audits and incident handling, under laws such as India's DPDP Act and, for EU customers, the EU AI Act.
- **Your current field, plus AI skills.** AI-literate marketers, analysts, HR staff, teachers, lawyers and doctors are increasingly valued, without changing job title at all.

This lesson follows three people in Chennai, a student, a developer and a hospital manager, as each picks the role that fits them.

## How it works

### Where the jobs are

AI hiring in India comes from five kinds of employers:

| Employer type | What they build | What they look for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| IT services firms | AI solutions for global clients | Engineers who can deliver, client skills, certifications sometimes help |
| Product companies and startups | AI features in their own products | Strong builders, speed, deployed projects |
| Global capability centres (GCCs) | AI for a multinational's own business | Engineering quality, domain knowledge, security awareness |
| Consulting firms | Strategy and implementation | Communication, problem framing, delivery |
| Overseas companies, remote | Product work | Excellent portfolio, written communication; very competitive |

GCCs are the in-house technology offices of multinational banks, retailers and manufacturers, clustered in Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune, Chennai and NCR. They have become a large source of AI roles.

### What employers look for

- **Engineering roles:** Python, APIs, Git and at least one cloud; language-model skills such as prompting, RAG, agents and evals; and projects that show a deployed, measured system, not a notebook.
- **Product and automation roles:** knowing what AI can and cannot do, choosing problems well, defining metrics, mapping processes, communicating clearly, and hands-on use of tools.
- **Everyone:** evidence. A portfolio of real projects, or a story of something you delivered at work, beats a list of certificates.

### Pay and seniority

Pay varies widely by company, city and experience. AI roles generally pay more than comparable non-AI roles, but salary figures in articles are rough signals, often based on small samples. Do not choose a role only for a number you read online.

### Entry routes

| Starting point | Realistic route |
| --- | --- |
| Student or fresher | Strong project portfolio plus internships; AI engineer or ML engineer path |
| Software engineer | Add LLM, RAG and agent skills to your current stack; AI Engineer, Generative AI & LLM Engineer or FDE path |
| Data analyst | Grow into ML engineering or AI evaluation |
| Non-engineer with domain depth | AI product, automation or AI-in-your-field roles |

## A worked example

Three people in Chennai consider AI careers.

**Sneha**, a final-year B.Tech student comfortable with Python, wants to be an AI engineer. She reads twenty job descriptions and sees the same skills again and again: Python, RAG, evaluation, deployment. She follows the AI Engineer path, builds three deployed projects, each with a short evaluation report, and applies to startups and GCCs. Her portfolio, not her marks, gets her interviews.

**Rahul** has five years as a Java backend developer at an IT services firm. He enjoys talking to clients more than most engineers do. He chooses forward deployed engineering. He follows the FDE path, uses his backend and integration experience as a strength, and moves into his company's AI practice by volunteering for an internal pilot.

**Kavitha** is an operations manager at a hospital chain and has never coded. She wants to lead AI adoption in healthcare operations. She takes the AI Product Manager path and the automation lessons. She leads a pilot that drafts discharge summaries for doctors to review, measures time saved and errors caught, and that pilot becomes her new role.

| Person | Background | Role chosen | Main evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sneha | Student, Python | AI engineer | Three deployed projects with evals |
| Rahul | Java developer, client-friendly | Forward deployed engineer | Internal AI pilot delivered |
| Kavitha | Hospital operations, no code | AI product lead | Measured pilot in her own hospital |

Each chose a role that fits their background and interests, not the one with the most buzz.

## Common mistakes

- **Choosing by title or hype.** Read real job descriptions and talk to people in the role before you commit.
- **Collecting certificates instead of projects.** Certificates show you studied. Employers want proof you can build or deliver.
- **Ignoring your existing strengths.** Domain knowledge plus AI skills is a rare and valuable combination.
- **Trying to learn everything.** Pick one path and go deep; you can widen later.
- **Expecting overnight results.** Most career switches into AI take months of steady work alongside a job.
- **Applying only to the biggest names.** GCCs, mid-size product firms and IT services AI practices often hire more people.

## Practise

Watch the video lessons in the Watch & learn panel for a wider view of the skills.

1. **Read ten job descriptions.** Find ten AI job postings in India on any job site. For each, note the title, the three most-mentioned skills and whether it needs coding.
2. **Match yourself.** Pick the two roles that best fit your background. List what you already have and what you lack for each.
3. **Talk to a person.** Message one person working in one of those roles, such as a senior, an alumnus or a LinkedIn contact, and ask what a normal week looks like.

**You are done when you can** name the AI role that suits you best and give three reasons based on evidence, not hype.
