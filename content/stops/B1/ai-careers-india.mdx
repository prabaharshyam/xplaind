## What it is

AI roles fall into a few families. Titles vary between companies, so read the job description, not just the title.

**Building with AI (coding)**

- **AI engineer / GenAI engineer**: builds applications on language models: prompts, retrieval (RAG), agents, evaluation and deployment. The fastest-growing AI title.
- **Forward deployed engineer**: an AI engineer who works directly with customers, finding the right problem and getting the system into daily use.
- **Agent and RAG specialists**: deep expertise in building agents or search over company documents.
- **LLMOps / AI platform engineer**: runs AI systems reliably at scale: serving, monitoring, cost, security.
- **ML engineer and data scientist**: trains and deploys machine learning models for prediction, ranking, fraud and forecasting.

**Shaping and using AI (little or no coding)**

- **AI product manager**: decides what AI features to build and how to measure them.
- **AI automation specialist**: automates business workflows with low-code tools and AI steps.
- **Roles in your current field with AI skills**: AI-literate marketers, analysts, HR staff, teachers, lawyers and doctors are increasingly valued.

## How it works

**Where the jobs are.** IT services companies building AI practices for global clients; product companies and startups building AI features; global capability centres (GCCs) of multinational banks, retailers and manufacturers in Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune, Chennai and NCR; and consulting firms. Remote roles with overseas companies exist but are competitive.

**What employers look for.**

- *Engineering roles*: Python, APIs, at least one cloud, Git; LLM skills such as prompting, RAG, agents and evals; projects that show deployed, measured systems.
- *Product and automation roles*: understanding of what AI can and cannot do, problem selection, metrics, process mapping, communication, and hands-on use of tools.
- *Everyone*: evidence. A portfolio of real projects beats a list of certificates.

**Salaries and seniority.** Pay varies widely by company, city and experience; AI roles generally pay a premium over comparable non-AI roles. Treat salary figures in articles as rough signals.

**Entry routes.**

- Students and freshers: a strong project portfolio plus internships.
- Software engineers: add LLM, RAG and agent skills to your existing stack; the GenAI or FDE path.
- Non-engineers: AI product, automation or AI-in-your-domain roles, building on your current expertise.

## A worked example

Three people in Chennai consider AI careers:

- **Sneha**, final-year B.Tech student, comfortable with Python. Goal: AI engineer. She follows the GenAI Engineer path, builds three deployed projects with evals, and applies to startups and GCCs.
- **Rahul**, five years as a Java backend developer at an IT services firm. Goal: forward deployed engineer, because he enjoys client work. He follows the FDE path, uses his backend experience as a strength and moves internally into his company's AI practice.
- **Kavitha**, operations manager at a hospital chain, no coding. Goal: lead AI adoption in healthcare operations. She takes AI for Beginners, then AI Product Manager and the automation lessons, and leads a pilot that automates discharge summaries, which becomes her new role.

Each chose a role that fits their background and interests, not the one with the most buzz.

## Common mistakes

- **Choosing by title or hype.** Read real job descriptions and talk to people in the role.
- **Collecting certificates instead of projects.** Employers want evidence of building or delivering.
- **Ignoring your existing strengths.** Domain knowledge plus AI skills is a powerful combination.
- **Trying to learn everything.** Pick one path and go deep.
- **Expecting overnight results.** Most transitions take months of steady work.

## Practise

Look at roadmap.sh's AI engineer roadmap for a wider view, and the ILO research summary.

1. Read ten AI job descriptions in India on any job site. For each, write the title, the three most-mentioned skills and whether it needs coding.
2. Match your background to two of the roles above, and list what you already have and what you lack.
3. Talk to one person working in one of those roles (a senior, an alumnus, a LinkedIn contact) and ask what their week looks like.

**You are done when you can** name the AI role that suits you best and give three reasons based on evidence.
