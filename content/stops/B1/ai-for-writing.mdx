## What it is

AI assistants are strongest at working with text. Four everyday uses:

- **Drafting**: a first version of an email, letter, report section or post from your notes.
- **Rewriting**: making text shorter, clearer, more polite, more formal, or right for a different reader.
- **Translating**: between English and Indian languages, or simplifying for non-experts.
- **Summarising**: turning a long document, email thread or meeting transcript into key points and actions.

In all four, you stay the author: you decide what is said, check it and own it.

## How it works

**Drafting from your notes.** Give the assistant your rough points, who the reader is and what you want them to do. "Here are my notes from the vendor meeting. Draft an email to my manager summarising the three options and recommending option 2, in under 150 words."

**Keeping your voice.** Paste one or two of your own past messages and ask it to match the style. Remove phrases you would never use. Readers notice text that sounds machine-made.

**Rewriting.** "Make this kinder but firm." "Explain this for a customer with no technical background." "Cut this to half the length, keep all the numbers."

**Translating.** Good assistants handle Hindi, Tamil, Bengali, Marathi and other languages reasonably, but quality varies, especially for formal or regional usage. For anything important (official letters, health or legal information), have a fluent speaker check it.

**Summarising.** Say what you need: "List the decisions, the open questions and who owns each action." For long documents, ask for page or section references so you can check. Summaries can drop important details or invent ones that are not there; check anything you will act on.

**Responsibility.** If you send it, you said it. Check names, numbers, dates and promises. Follow your organisation's rules about which tools can see which documents.

## A worked example

Fatima, an operations lead at a logistics company in Kolkata, has a one-hour meeting recording transcript and 40 emails about a delayed shipment.

She asks the approved company assistant: "Summarise this transcript into: decisions made, open issues, actions with owner and deadline. Quote the exact words where deadlines were agreed." In two minutes she has a one-page summary. She checks the three deadlines against the transcript; one was "end of next week", not "Friday" as summarised, and she corrects it.

Then: "Draft an update email to the client in a calm, professional tone. Apologise for the delay without blaming our partner, give the new delivery date of 14 November, and offer a call." She edits two sentences that sounded stiff, adds a personal line and sends it.

The job took 25 minutes instead of the usual two hours, and she checked everything that mattered.

## Common mistakes

- **Sending without reading.** Errors and odd phrases go out under your name.
- **Bland text.** Ask for your style, and edit to sound like you.
- **Trusting summaries for decisions.** Check details you will act on against the source.
- **Pasting confidential documents into unapproved tools.**
- **Over-long output.** Ask for a word limit; shorter is usually better.

## Practise

Use any assistant your school or workplace allows, and the AI Fluency course for good habits.

1. Draft a real email from your rough notes, then edit it until it sounds like you. Count how many changes you made.
2. Summarise a long article or report into decisions, numbers and open questions, then check three points against the original.
3. Translate a short notice into an Indian language you know well and mark any errors.

**You are done when you can** show a draft, your edits and a summary you checked, and say what you would never send unchecked.
