## What it is

A **prompt** is whatever you type to an AI assistant. You do not need special tricks, magic phrases or technical words to write good ones. A good prompt looks like a clear brief you would give a capable new colleague who knows a lot in general but nothing about your situation.

Four plain habits cover most of what makes prompts work:

```text
   1. CONTEXT          2. EXAMPLE          3. FORMAT          4. FEEDBACK
 +--------------+   +--------------+   +--------------+   +--------------+
 | who you are, |   | show what    |   | length,      |   | read it, ask |
 | who it's for,|-->| good looks   |-->| structure,   |-->| for specific |
 | why, facts   |   | like         |   | tone,        |   | changes      |
 |              |   |              |   | language     |   |              |
 +--------------+   +--------------+   +--------------+   +--------------+
     always           when you can        always            always
```

The difference these habits make is large. A one-line request gets a generic answer, because the assistant has to guess everything. A good brief gets something close to usable on the first try.

This lesson follows Ravi, who runs a tuition centre in Coimbatore, as he turns a vague request into a message he can send to parents. By the end you will use all four habits without thinking about them.

## How it works

### Habit 1: give context

The assistant cannot know who you are, who will read the answer, or the facts of your situation. Tell it.

| Weak | Strong |
| --- | --- |
| "Write a leave application." | "I'm a nurse at a hospital in Madurai. Write a short, polite leave application to my head nurse for three days next week, 12 to 14 November, for my sister's wedding. I have already arranged for a colleague to cover my shifts." |

The strong version gives the reader, the dates, the reason and a reassuring detail. The assistant does not have to invent any of them, which also means it is less likely to make something up.

### Habit 2: show an example

If you have a past email, report or message you liked, paste it, with private details removed, and say "write it in this style". Examples work better than describing a style in words, because the model is very good at copying patterns. Giving a few input-and-output examples so the model follows the pattern is called **few-shot prompting**.

```text
  Example 1:  Product: steel water bottle  ->  "Stays cold 24 hours. Built for Chennai summers."
  Example 2:  Product: cotton kurta         ->  "Breathes in the heat. Looks sharp at work."
  Now write:  Product: jute tote bag        ->  ?
```

### Habit 3: ask for a format

Say exactly what you want back. Format instructions are cheap to write and save a lot of editing:

- "five bullet points"
- "under 120 words"
- "a table with columns: item, cost, notes"
- "in simple Tamil, then the same in English"
- "formal but warm"
- "start with the recommendation"

### Habit 4: give specific feedback

The first answer is a draft. Read it and say exactly what to change:

| Vague feedback | Specific feedback |
| --- | --- |
| "Make it better." | "Cut it to 80 words and drop the second paragraph." |
| "That's wrong." | "Our office closes at 6, not 7. Fix the timing." |
| "Too formal." | "Write it the way you'd text a colleague you like." |

Two or three rounds of specific feedback usually get you there.

### Other useful moves

- **Ask it to ask you questions first:** "Before you write, ask me anything you need to know." This often saves a round of feedback.
- **Ask for options:** "Give me three different subject lines."
- **Ask it to check your thinking:** "What might be wrong with this plan?"
- **Break big jobs into steps:** ask for an outline first, agree on it, then ask for each section.

### How much you can include

Every model has a **context window**: the amount of text it can take in at once, including your prompt, any pasted documents and the conversation so far. Modern assistants can hold a lot, often hundreds of pages, but very long conversations can drift. If an assistant starts forgetting your earlier instructions, start a new chat and restate the key points.

### What not to share

Keep passwords, Aadhaar, PAN or bank details, and confidential work information out of prompts, unless your organisation has approved that tool for that kind of data.

## A worked example

Ravi runs a small tuition centre in Coimbatore for classes 8 to 10. He wants a WhatsApp message announcing new weekend batches.

**First try:**

> "Write a message about weekend classes."

The result is 180 words, generic, mentions "all subjects" and "expert faculty", and has five emojis. Unusable.

**Second try, with context and format:**

> "I run a tuition centre in Coimbatore for classes 8 to 10, maths and science. Write a WhatsApp message to parents announcing new weekend batches starting 7 December, Saturday and Sunday, 9 to 11 am. Fees ₹1,500 a month. Seats limited to 15. Keep it under 80 words, friendly, with at most one emoji. Write it in English, then the same in simple Tamil."

The result is close. Ravi gives **specific feedback:**

> "Mention that the first class is a free trial, and put the phone number 98xxxxxx21 at the end of both versions."

The third version is ready. Before sending, he asks his sister, who teaches Tamil, to check the Tamil wording. She fixes one word.

| Version | Habits used | Usable? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | None | No: generic, too long |
| 2 | Context, format | Almost: missing two details |
| 3 | Context, format, feedback | Yes, after a Tamil check |

The whole job took about five minutes, and every fact in the message came from Ravi, not from the assistant's imagination.

## Common mistakes

- **One-line requests.** Add context and a format; it takes thirty seconds.
- **Accepting the first answer.** Treat it as a draft and give feedback.
- **Vague feedback.** "Make it better" helps much less than "shorter, and less formal".
- **Describing a style instead of showing it.** Paste an example when you have one.
- **Sharing private data.** Remove names, numbers and confidential details unless the tool is approved.
- **Thinking a good prompt guarantees facts.** Good prompts improve answers; they do not make them always right. Check facts that matter.

## Practise

Take Anthropic's free AI Fluency course, linked below, or browse OpenAI Academy's beginner sessions.

1. **Start weak.** Pick a real task from your week, such as a message, a plan or an explanation. Write a one-line prompt and save the answer.
2. **Apply the habits.** Rewrite the prompt with context, an example if you have one, and a format. Save the new answer.
3. **Give feedback.** Give two rounds of specific feedback and compare all three versions side by side.

**You are done when you can** show a one-line prompt and your improved prompt side by side, and explain what made the difference.
