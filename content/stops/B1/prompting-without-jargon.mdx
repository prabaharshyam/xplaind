## What it is

A **prompt** is what you type to an AI assistant. You do not need special tricks or technical words. Good prompts look like a clear brief you would give a capable new colleague who knows nothing about your situation. Four habits cover most of it:

1. **Context**: who you are, who it is for, why you need it.
2. **Example**: show what good looks like, if you can.
3. **Format**: say the length, structure, tone and language you want.
4. **Feedback**: read the answer and ask for specific changes.

## How it works

**Context.** Compare "Write a leave application" with "I'm a nurse at a hospital in Madurai. Write a short, polite leave application to my head nurse for three days next week for my sister's wedding. I have already arranged a colleague to cover my shifts." The second gives the assistant what it needs to sound right.

**Example.** If you have a past email, report or message you liked, paste it (with private details removed) and say "write it in this style". Examples work better than describing a style in words.

**Format.** Say exactly what you want back: "five bullet points", "under 120 words", "a table with columns item, cost, notes", "in simple Tamil", "formal but warm".

**Feedback.** The first answer is a draft. Be specific: "Make it shorter", "the second point is wrong, our office closes at 6", "use Indian English spellings", "add a line thanking her". Two or three rounds usually get you there.

**Other useful moves.**

- Ask it to **ask you questions first**: "Before you write, ask me anything you need to know."
- Ask for **options**: "Give me three different subject lines."
- Ask it to **explain or check**: "What might be wrong with this plan?"
- **Break big jobs into steps**: outline first, then each section.

**What not to share.** Keep out passwords, Aadhaar or bank details, and confidential work information unless your organisation has approved the tool for it.

## A worked example

Ravi runs a small tuition centre in Coimbatore and wants a WhatsApp message announcing new weekend classes.

First try: "Write a message about weekend classes." The result is generic and long.

Second try:

"I run a tuition centre in Coimbatore for classes 8 to 10 (maths and science). Write a WhatsApp message to parents announcing new weekend batches starting 7 December, Saturday and Sunday, 9 to 11 am. Fees ₹1,500 a month. Seats are limited to 15. Keep it under 80 words, friendly, with one emoji at most. Write it in English, then the same in simple Tamil."

The result is close. Ravi gives feedback: "Mention that the first class is a free trial, and put the phone number 98xxxxxx21 at the end." The third version is ready to send after he checks the Tamil wording with his sister, who teaches Tamil.

## Common mistakes

- **One-line requests.** Add context and format.
- **Accepting the first answer.** Give specific feedback.
- **Vague feedback.** "Make it better" helps less than "shorter, and less formal".
- **Sharing private data.** Remove names, numbers and confidential details unless the tool is approved.
- **Not checking facts.** Good prompts improve answers; they do not make them always right.

## Practise

Take Anthropic's free AI Fluency course, or browse OpenAI Academy's beginner sessions.

1. Pick a real task from your week (a message, a plan, an explanation). Write a one-line prompt and save the answer.
2. Rewrite the prompt with context, an example or format, and save the new answer.
3. Give two rounds of specific feedback and compare all three versions.

**You are done when you can** show a one-line prompt and your improved prompt side by side, and explain what made the difference.
