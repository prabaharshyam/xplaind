## What it is

Four tools cover most workflow automation work you will meet:

- **Zapier**: the best-known no-code tool. Very easy, with a huge catalogue of app connectors. Workflows ("Zaps") are mostly linear with some branching.
- **Make** (formerly Integromat): a visual canvas with more control over data, branching and iteration than Zapier, still no-code.
- **n8n**: a visual tool for more technical users, open source and self-hostable, with code nodes and strong AI and agent features.
- **Microsoft Power Automate**: part of Microsoft's Power Platform, deeply integrated with Microsoft 365, Teams, SharePoint and Dynamics, and including desktop RPA for older Windows applications.

Others exist (Google's tools for Workspace, enterprise integration platforms, open-source alternatives), but these four come up most often in Indian businesses.

## How it works

**Ease of use.** Zapier is easiest to start. Make is a step up in flexibility with a moderate learning curve. n8n suits people comfortable with JSON and a bit of code. Power Automate is easy for simple Microsoft 365 flows and more complex for advanced ones.

**Connectors.** Zapier and Make have the widest catalogues of ready connectors for popular SaaS apps. n8n has many and an HTTP Request node for anything with an API. Power Automate is strongest with Microsoft products and has connectors for many others, some at premium licence levels.

**AI features.** All four now offer AI steps: calling models, extracting data, and agent-like features. n8n is particularly strong for building custom AI agents; Power Automate links to Microsoft's AI services and Copilot.

**Pricing model.** Each counts usage differently: by tasks or actions, by operations, by workflow executions, or by user and flow licences. A workflow that is cheap at 100 runs a day can be expensive at 10,000 on one tool and cheap on another. Self-hosted n8n replaces usage fees with server and maintenance costs. Always estimate at expected volume with current price pages.

**Data control.** Zapier and Make are cloud services; check where data is processed and stored. n8n can run entirely on the client's servers. Power Automate runs within the client's Microsoft tenant and its governance.

**Fit.** The strongest factor is often what the client already has: a company on Microsoft 365 with IT governance will usually prefer Power Automate; a startup living in SaaS apps may prefer Zapier or Make; a company with strict data rules or heavy AI needs may prefer self-hosted n8n.

## A worked example

Three clients, three choices:

- **A Chennai chartered accountancy firm** with 25 staff, using Gmail, Google Sheets and a few SaaS tools, wants client document reminders and intake. Low volume, no technical staff. **Zapier**: fastest to build and for them to maintain.
- **A Pune manufacturer** on Microsoft 365 with an IT team and an old Windows inventory application with no API. **Power Automate**: cloud flows for approvals in Teams and desktop RPA for the old application, all under existing Microsoft governance and licences.
- **A Bengaluru health-tech startup** processing patient intake forms with AI extraction at 20,000 forms a month, with strict data rules. **Self-hosted n8n** in their own cloud account: no per-task fees at that volume, data stays in their environment, and their developers can extend it.

## Common mistakes

- **Choosing by personal preference.** Choose by the client's apps, skills, volume and data rules.
- **Ignoring pricing at scale.** Estimate at real volume, not the free tier.
- **Forgetting maintenance.** Who will fix it when you leave?
- **Overlooking existing licences.** Many companies already pay for Power Automate through Microsoft 365.
- **Self-hosting without an owner.** Servers need updates, backups and monitoring.

## Practise

Do one Microsoft Learn module on Power Automate and browse Zapier's guides; compare with what you know of n8n.

1. Build the same simple workflow (form to sheet to email) in two tools. Note time taken and difficulties.
2. For a workflow at 200, 2,000 and 20,000 runs a month, estimate cost on two tools using their current price pages, noting the date you checked.
