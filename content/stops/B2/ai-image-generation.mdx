## What it is

AI image tools create pictures from words. Most are **diffusion models**: they start from random noise and remove it step by step, steered by your prompt, until an image appears. Many can also take **reference images**, so you can edit a photo or keep a person or product the same across many pictures.

Two tools cover most needs:

- **Gemini's image model**, nicknamed **Nano Banana**, in the Gemini app and Google's AI Studio. It is especially good at **editing in plain words** ("make it night", "swap the shirt colour") and at keeping faces and objects consistent across edits.
- **Midjourney**, known for beautiful, artistic images with a strong look, used through its website. It has settings for aspect ratio, how stylised the result is, and variations.

```flow
Prompt | subject, details, setting, camera, light, style, aspect ratio -[new image from words]-> Generate
Generate -> Pick the best of 4 -> Upscale
Prompt -[your photo + an instruction]-> Edit | "remove the bags, warmer light, keep the person unchanged"
Pick the best of 4 -[reuse as a reference]-> A consistent set of images
```

## How it works

**Prompt like a photographer or illustrator.** Vague prompts get generic images. Name the things a professional would decide:

| Part | Question it answers | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Subject | who or what, exactly | a smiling Malayali grandmother holding a plate of appam |
| Details | clothes, materials, colours | white mundu with a gold border, banana-leaf plate |
| Setting | where and when | a sunlit Kerala kitchen with copper pots |
| Composition | framing and angle | medium shot, eye level, subject on the right third |
| Lighting | quality and direction | soft morning light from a window |
| Style or medium | photo, illustration, 3D, watercolour | editorial food photography |
| Format | where it will be used | 4:5 for Instagram, 16:9 for YouTube |

**Edit instead of regenerating.** With Gemini, upload a photo and describe one change at a time. Say what must stay the same ("keep the face and pose unchanged"). Several small edits beat one big one.

**Midjourney settings worth knowing.** `--ar 16:9` sets the aspect ratio. A stylize setting controls how much of Midjourney's own artistic look it adds. Variations and remix let you nudge a result you nearly like, and you can give image and style references. Check the current documentation, as version names and options change.

**Consistency.** For a character, product or mascot, make one strong reference image, then reuse it as a reference in every new image and keep the same written description.

**From image to video.** Strong still images are also the starting point for AI video. In the Veo and Flow guide you will use an image as the first frame of a shot, or as a character and product reference, so the time spent on a good image pays off twice.

**Text in images.** Newer models write short text far better than before, but long text still goes wrong. For headlines on posts and thumbnails, it is often faster to add the text in Canva or Figma.

**Resolution and upscaling.** Generators produce images at a set size. For print, banners or large screens, use the tool's upscale option or a separate upscaler, and check fine details such as hands, text and logos at full size before you publish; small errors that are invisible on a phone show up in print.

**Rights and labels.** Check each tool's terms for commercial use; paid plans usually allow it. Many platforms now ask you to label AI-generated or heavily edited images, and Google adds an invisible SynthID watermark to images from its models. Being open about AI use builds trust with customers.

## A worked example

Lakshmi runs a home bakery in Kochi and needs images for a new Instagram page, but cannot afford a photographer every week.

She photographs her best-selling plum cake on her kitchen table with her phone. In Gemini she makes three edits, one at a time: "replace the background with a rustic wooden table and a cup of coffee", "warmer, softer light like early morning", and "add a sprig of rosemary on the plate, keep the cake exactly the same". The cake stays truly hers, which matters for honesty with customers.

For a festive banner she uses Midjourney: "Christmas window display of a small Kerala bakery at dusk, fairy lights, star lanterns, plum cakes on the shelf, warm glow, cinematic, --ar 16:9". She picks one of the four results and upscales it.

She designs a mascot, a cheerful cartoon baker, in Gemini, then reuses that image as a reference for three more posts so he looks the same each time. Headlines go on in Canva.

The page looks professional from day one. Her rule: real products are edited photos, never generated, so customers get exactly what they see.

## Common mistakes

**One-word prompts.** "A cake" gives a stock-photo cake. Describe the shot you see in your head.

**Changing many things in one edit.** Gemini follows one or two clear changes far better than five at once.

**Generated "photos" of real products.** Customers expect what they see. Edit real photos of your products instead.

**Using real people or brands.** Do not create images of celebrities, real customers without consent, or other companies' logos.

**Copying a living artist's style for money.** Describe the qualities you like instead of naming the artist.

**Ignoring aspect ratio.** An image made square gets cropped badly as a YouTube thumbnail. Set the format before you generate.

**Accepting the first result.** Generate a few options, compare them, and refine the best one with small, specific changes.
