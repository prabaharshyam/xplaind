## What it is

Model monitoring watches four layers:

- **Service health**: is the model running? Latency, errors, throughput.
- **Data quality and input drift**: are inputs still valid and similar to training data? Missing values, new categories, shifted distributions.
- **Prediction drift**: has the distribution of scores or predicted classes changed?
- **Performance**: when true outcomes arrive, is accuracy still where it was, overall and by segment?

**Data drift** means inputs changed; **concept drift** means the relationship between inputs and outcomes changed (the same features now predict differently, as when fraud patterns shift).

```text
   LAYER                CHECKED                         CATCHES
   service health       latency, errors (every minute)  outages
   inputs               schema, missing rates, drift    upstream bugs, new data shapes
                        per feature vs baseline (daily)
   predictions          approval rate by partner/city   sudden behaviour change
                        (daily)
   performance          default rate by month and       slow decay, concept drift
                        segment (as labels arrive)

   alert -> owner -> runbook: investigate | fallback | roll back | retrain
```

This lesson follows a buy-now-pay-later company in Bengaluru whose credit model approves small loans at checkout.

## How it works

**Baselines.** Save summary statistics of training and validation data (distributions per feature, score distributions, segment shares). Monitoring compares live data with these.

**Data checks.** Schema checks (types, allowed values), missing-value rates, and drift scores per feature, such as the population stability index or statistical distance measures. Flag features whose distribution moves beyond a threshold.

**Prediction checks.** Share of each predicted class, average score, share above the action threshold. A sudden jump often signals an upstream bug before any outcome arrives.

**Delayed ground truth.** Outcomes often arrive late: loan default after 90 days, churn after a month. Track performance when labels arrive, and use proxies (complaints, overrides, early repayment behaviour) meanwhile.

**Segments.** Track by region, channel, product and customer type; drift often starts in one segment.

**Alerts and response.** Not every drift needs action. Define thresholds with severity: investigate, retrain, roll back, or switch to a fallback. Route alerts to an owner with a runbook.

**Retraining policy.** Retrain on a schedule (monthly), on a trigger (drift or performance drop), or both. Every retrained model goes through the same evaluation and promotion checks as the first, including comparison with the current production model.

**Feedback loops.** Models can change the data they later learn from: a fraud model that blocks transactions means you never see whether they were fraud. Keep a small random holdout or human review sample to get unbiased labels.

**Alert thresholds with severities:**

| Signal | Threshold | Severity | Response |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Error rate | Above 1% for 5 minutes | High | Page on-call; check service |
| Feature missing rate | Doubles vs baseline | Medium | Investigate the source |
| Approval rate by partner | Changes by over 10 points in a day | High | Check inputs; fallback model if needed |
| Drift score on a key feature | Above the agreed limit for 3 days | Medium | Review; consider retraining |
| Default rate by segment | Above plan for two months | Medium | Retrain with recent data |

**Reading a drift score.** The population stability index compares how a feature's values fall into buckets in training data versus live data. Small values mean little change; larger values mean a real shift. Treat it as a pointer, not a verdict: a shift in a feature the model barely uses may not matter, while a small shift in its most important feature might. Check feature importance alongside drift before acting.

**Writing the runbook.** For each alert, write who owns it, the first three things to check, how to switch to the fallback, and when to roll back. Keep the fallback ready and tested, such as a simpler model without the troublesome feature or a rules policy, so containment takes minutes.

## A worked example

A Bengaluru BNPL (buy now, pay later) company's credit model approves small loans at checkout.

Monitoring dashboard and alerts:

- Service: p95 latency and error rate.
- Inputs: daily drift scores for 40 features against the training baseline; schema checks on each partner's payloads.
- Predictions: approval rate by partner and city.
- Performance: 60-day default rate by approval month and segment, as outcomes arrive.

In March, the approval rate for one large e-commerce partner jumps from 61% to 79% overnight. Input drift shows the "device age" feature collapsed to zero for that partner: their app update stopped sending it, and the model read zero as "brand-new device", a low-risk signal. The team switches that partner to a fallback model without the feature within two hours, and the partner fixes its payload.

Separately, a slow rise in defaults among first-time borrowers in tier-3 cities over four months triggers a retraining with recent data and a new feature for repayment behaviour on earlier small loans. The retrained model passes promotion checks and is rolled out through shadow and canary stages.

A 2% random sample of borderline applicants is approved regardless of score, so the company keeps learning about the applicants the model would otherwise reject.

| Event | Detected by | Response | Time |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Partner approvals jumped from 61% to 79% | Approval-rate alert, then input drift | Fallback model for that partner | Two hours |
| First-time tier-3 borrowers defaulting more | Performance by segment over four months | Retrained with a new feature, shadow then canary | Weeks |

## Common mistakes

- **Monitoring only uptime.** A running model can be badly wrong.
- **No baselines.** Drift needs something to compare with.
- **Alerting on every small drift.** Set thresholds and severities, or alerts get ignored.
- **Waiting for labels to notice problems.** Watch inputs and predictions daily.
- **Ignoring feedback loops.** Keep unbiased samples for learning.
