## What it is

**Offline evals** test a fixed golden set before you ship. **Online evals** score what happens after you ship, on real traffic. You need both: offline evals stop known regressions; online evals find the unknown ones.

Online signals come in four kinds:

- **Automatic checks** on every response: valid format, citations present, refusal or not, length, latency, guardrail triggers.
- **Model-graded scores** on a sample: faithfulness to sources, relevance, tone, scored by a judge model you have checked against human grades.
- **User feedback**: thumbs up or down, a reason, whether they copied the answer, asked again or escalated to a person.
- **Human review**: an expert reads a weekly sample and labels what went wrong.

```text
   live request --> answer to user (no waiting)
        |
        v  trace stored
   [rules on every trace]  format, citations, refusal, length, latency
   [judge on 10% sample + all negative feedback]  faithfulness, relevance
   [user feedback]  thumbs + reason, rephrased question, escalation
   [weekly human review]  50 traces labelled by cause
        |
        v
   dashboard by day, version, topic, language  --> alerts on sudden changes
        |
        v
   fix the biggest cause  -->  add cases to the offline golden set
```

This lesson follows a telecom's customer assistant in Chennai whose negative feedback almost doubled six weeks after launch.

## How it works

**Score asynchronously.** Run checks and judges after the response has gone to the user, from the traces, so they add no latency.

**Sample sensibly.** Score all traces cheaply with rules; run the judge on a random 5 to 10% plus every trace with negative feedback or a guardrail trigger.

**Make feedback easy and specific.** A thumbs down followed by three quick reasons ("wrong", "incomplete", "not what I asked") gives far more than a bare thumbs down. Implicit signals help too: a rephrased question within a minute often means the first answer missed.

**Watch trends, not single numbers.** Dashboards by day and by version: faithfulness, refusal rate, negative feedback rate, escalations. Alert on sudden changes, such as the refusal rate doubling after a document upload, or faithfulness dropping after a provider's model update.

**Close the loop.** Each week: read the worst-scored and negatively rated traces, group them by cause (retrieval miss, stale document, prompt gap, out-of-scope question), fix the biggest group, and add representative cases to the offline golden set so the fix stays fixed.

**Check the judge.** A judge model has its own errors. Every few weeks, have a person grade 50 judged samples and check agreement. If agreement falls, fix the judge prompt before trusting its numbers.

**Signals and what they catch:**

| Signal | Cost | Catches |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rule checks on every trace | Almost free | Broken formats, missing citations, slow responses |
| Refusal rate by topic | Free | Missing documents, over-strict prompts |
| Judge on a sample | Model calls | Unfaithful or irrelevant answers |
| Thumbs and reasons | Free | What users actually dislike |
| Rephrase within a minute | Free | Answers that missed the question |
| Weekly human review | Staff time | Causes no automatic signal names |

**Running the weekly review.** Pull 50 traces: the 20 worst by judge score, 20 with negative feedback, and 10 at random. For each, write one cause label from a short list: retrieval miss, stale or missing document, prompt gap, out of scope, model error, or fine. Count the labels. The biggest group is next week's fix. Keep the counts week by week, so you and the client can see causes shrink as fixes land.

**Telling the client.** Share the online dashboard and the weekly cause counts, not only the offline score. Clients trust a team that spots a problem, explains it and shows it fixed far more than a team that only reports a high launch number.

## A worked example

A Chennai telecom's customer assistant passed its offline golden set at 91%. Six weeks after launch, negative feedback crept from 6% to 11%.

The online data showed:

- Faithfulness (judged on a 10% sample) steady at 0.93: answers matched their sources.
- Refusal rate up from 8% to 17%.
- Most negative feedback came with the reason "not what I asked".

Reading 60 of those traces revealed the cause: a new prepaid plan had launched, customers asked about it in Tamil and English, and the plan documents had not been added to the index. The assistant correctly refused, and customers were unhappy.

Fixes: the marketing team's plan launches now trigger an indexing job; an alert fires when the refusal rate for a topic jumps; 25 questions about new plans join the golden set. Negative feedback was back to 6% within a week. In the monthly review you showed the client the timeline, which did more for trust than the original 91%.

| Week | Negative feedback | Refusal rate | Action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Launch | 6% | 8% | None |
| Week 6 | 11% | 17% | Read 60 traces: new plan documents missing |
| Week 7 | 6% | 8% | Plan launches trigger indexing; topic refusal alert; 25 golden cases added |

## Common mistakes

- **Shipping without online signals.** You hear about problems from angry users or the client's boss.
- **Trusting an unchecked judge.** Judge scores drift and have blind spots; compare with human grades regularly.
- **Collecting feedback and never reading it.** Schedule the weekly review.
- **Only averages.** A steady average can hide one topic or language getting much worse. Slice by topic, language and version.
- **Fixing without adding tests.** The same failure returns after the next change.

## Practise

Read Langfuse's evaluation overview and Eugene Yan's piece on the evaluation process.

1. Add a thumbs up or down with three reasons to your app, stored with the trace id.
2. Write two automatic checks (citation present, response under a length limit) and one judge for faithfulness, run on a 10% sample after responses are sent.
3. Grade 30 judged samples yourself and compute how often you agree with the judge.

**You are done when you can** show a weekly view of your online scores and one change you made because of them.
