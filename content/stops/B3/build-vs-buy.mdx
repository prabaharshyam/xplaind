## What it is

Two decisions come early in any AI product:

- **Feasibility**: can AI do this task well enough, on our real data, at an acceptable cost and speed?
- **Build versus buy**: should we buy a finished product, configure a platform, or build our own?

## How it works

**Prototype in days.** Take 20 to 50 real examples (masked if needed) and try the task with a capable model and a simple prompt, by hand or in a notebook. Have a domain expert judge the outputs. If results are far off, it may not be feasible yet, or needs more context or data. If results are close, feasibility is likely and the real work is reliability, integration and safety.

**Estimate cost and speed.** From the prototype, measure tokens per request and latency. Multiply by expected volume. Check whether it fits the budget and the user's patience.

**The options.**

- **Buy a product**: a ready tool for the use case (a meeting-notes tool, a support chatbot product, a document-processing service). Fastest. Limited customisation; vendor's roadmap; data goes to the vendor.
- **Configure a platform**: an AI feature inside software you already use (CRM, help desk, office suite) or a low-code AI platform. Fast, integrates with existing data and permissions; limited flexibility.
- **Build**: your own system on model APIs or open models. Most control and differentiation; needs engineers, evaluation and ongoing maintenance.

**Decision factors.**

- *Differentiation*: is this core to how you compete? Build what makes you different; buy commodity functions.
- *Data and compliance*: where may data go? Does the vendor meet your security and privacy requirements?
- *Fit*: does the product handle your languages, formats and workflows?
- *Total cost*: licences or usage, integration, people to run it, over two to three years.
- *Speed*: how soon do you need value?
- *Lock-in*: how hard is it to switch later?

**Hybrid is normal.** Buy for commodity tasks, build where you differentiate, and keep options open with standard interfaces.

## A worked example

A Hyderabad hospital chain wants AI to draft discharge summaries from doctors' notes and lab reports.

**Feasibility**: the PM, Sana, collects 40 de-identified patient records. Using a capable model with a summary template, three senior doctors rate 31 drafts as "usable with small edits", 7 as "needs significant edits" and 2 as "unsafe" (both missed a medication change). Feasible, with mandatory doctor review and a medication cross-check.

**Options**:

- *Buy*: two clinical documentation products. One does not support their hospital information system; the other processes data outside India, which the hospital's legal team rejects.
- *Configure*: their hospital information system vendor offers an AI summary add-on, but it cannot be customised to their template or medication checks.
- *Build*: a small team builds on a model available in the Indian region of their existing cloud, integrated with the hospital system, with the medication cross-check and doctor sign-off.

Sana recommends build, estimating three months and a two-year cost lower than the add-on's licences at their volume, with differentiation in quality of care. Leadership approves, with a review of the market in a year.

## Common mistakes

- **Skipping the prototype.** Feasibility guesses are often wrong in both directions.
- **Building commodity features.** Buy what does not differentiate you.
- **Buying without checking data handling.** Compliance can rule out good products.
- **Comparing licence price with nothing.** Building has people and maintenance costs too.
- **Forgetting lock-in.** Know how you would leave.

## Practise

Reread SVPG's four big risks and Hamel Husain's field guide.

1. Prototype an AI task with 20 real examples and have someone who knows the domain rate the outputs.
2. Find two products and one platform feature that claim to solve the same task. Compare them on fit, data handling, cost and lock-in.
3. Write a one-page build, configure or buy recommendation with a two-year cost estimate.

**You are done when you can** show prototype results rated by an expert and defend your build-versus-buy choice on four factors.
