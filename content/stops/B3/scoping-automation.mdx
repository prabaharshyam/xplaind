## What it is

"Can you automate our orders from WhatsApp?" sounds like one question. In reality it hides dozens: Which orders? Voice notes too? What about unknown products? Who approves? Who fixes it in six months? An **automation scope** answers them before the work starts, so both sides know what they are getting.

An automation scope is a short agreement, often one page:

| Section | Says | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Process and boundaries** | Where it starts (trigger) and ends (outcome) | From order message received to CSV ready for billing |
| **Inputs** | Formats, sources, volumes | Text and photos on WhatsApp, about 150 a day |
| **Automated and manual steps** | What the automation does and what people still do | AI extracts; staff confirm each order |
| **Exceptions** | What happens with unusual cases | Unknown products go to staff with the original message |
| **Systems and access** | Apps, accounts, permissions, who provides them and when | WhatsApp Business account, set up by the client by 10 December |
| **Success measures** | What improves, with a baseline | Entry time from 3 minutes to under 1 minute |
| **Acceptance test** | How both sides check it works | 200 past orders replayed; 90% of lines matched |
| **Ownership and support** | Who maintains it, support period, what counts as a change | 30 days of fixes; changes at an agreed day rate |
| **Price and timeline** | For client work | Fixed fee plus monthly running costs |

## How it works

### Start from the process map

Use the map from discovery to agree the boundaries. Mark each step as **automated**, **assisted** (AI drafts, a person approves) or **manual**. The scope is the map plus decisions.

### List inputs and exceptions honestly

Ask for **20 to 50 real samples** before agreeing anything. Count the formats and the exceptions:

| Input type | Share of samples | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Typed text orders | 55% | In scope |
| Photos of handwritten lists | 30% | In scope, with staff review |
| Voice notes | 12% | Out of scope for now; phase 2 |
| Forwarded PDFs from other apps | 3% | Sent to staff as an exception |

For each exception, decide: handle it, send it to a person, or exclude it. Write the decision down.

### Agree the human checkpoints

Which steps need approval? Payments, messages to customers and deletions usually do. Who approves, and in which tool? A checkpoint nobody has time for will be skipped.

### Make access a dependency

List every system, the type of account needed and the date access is due. Access delays often hold up projects more than technical problems do. If access is late, the timeline moves, and the scope should say so.

### Define acceptance as a test

Vague: "the system works well". Testable: "On 100 real invoices from last month, at least 85 are processed with no human touch and all fields match the accountant's entries; every failure appears in the review queue." Someone other than you should be able to run it.

### Support and changes

State the support period, for example 30 days of fixes after go-live. Define a **change request**: a new supplier format, a new system, a new feature. Say how changes are priced. Requests that arrive mid-project without changing time or price are **scope creep**; put them on a phase 2 list with a quote.

### Pricing models

| Model | Best when | Risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fixed price | Scope is clear and samples are seen | You absorb surprises |
| Time and materials | Scope is unclear or exploratory | Client absorbs surprises |
| Monthly retainer | Ongoing maintenance and small changes | Needs a clear monthly limit |

Always include tool and AI usage costs, and say who pays them.

## A worked example

A freelance automation specialist in Indore is asked by a distributor to "automate orders from WhatsApp".

**Discovery** shows about 150 orders a day from 300 retailers, sent as WhatsApp text, voice notes and photos of handwritten lists in Hindi and English. Staff type them into the billing software, which can import CSV files.

**The one-page scope:**

- **In:** text and photo orders from the business WhatsApp number; AI extraction of retailer, products and quantities, matched to the product list; a review screen where staff confirm each order; a daily CSV export for the billing software.
- **Out:** voice notes (phase 2), payments, stock checks, and automatic replies to retailers.
- **Exceptions:** unknown products or retailers go to staff with the original message.
- **Access:** WhatsApp Business Platform account through a provider, set up by the client by 10 December; product and retailer lists as sheets.
- **Success:** order entry time from about 3 minutes to under 1 minute per order; errors in shipped quantities no higher than today.
- **Acceptance:** 200 past orders replayed; at least 90% of lines matched correctly before staff review.
- **Support:** 30 days of fixes; new formats or features as change requests at an agreed day rate.
- **Price:** fixed fee, plus monthly running costs for the WhatsApp provider and AI usage, paid by the client.

**Two weeks in**, the owner asks to add automatic payment reminders to retailers. It is a good idea, and it is not in the scope. It goes on the phase 2 list with its own quote. Because the scope said clearly what was out, the conversation takes two minutes instead of turning into an argument.

## Common mistakes

- **Agreeing scope without samples.** Real inputs reveal the exceptions.
- **Silence on what is out.** Unwritten exclusions become free extra work.
- **Access as an afterthought.** It is one of the most common causes of delay.
- **Vague acceptance.** Define a test on real data, with numbers.
- **No support terms.** You become unpaid support forever.
- **Hiding running costs.** Clients feel misled when the first provider bill arrives.

## Practise

Read Atlassian's guide to project scope, linked below, and GOV.UK's guide to user stories.

1. **Samples.** Take an automation idea from your process map and collect at least 20 real or realistic input samples.
2. **Scope.** Write a one-page scope with every section above.
3. **Acceptance.** Write the acceptance test as something someone else could run.

**You are done when you can** hand your scope to the business owner and have them agree, in writing, what is in, what is out and how success is measured.
