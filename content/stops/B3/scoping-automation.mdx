## What it is

An **automation scope** is a short agreement, often one page, that says exactly what the automation will do. It covers:

- **Process and boundaries**: where it starts (trigger) and ends (outcome).
- **Inputs**: formats, sources and volumes ("PDF invoices by email, about 600 a month, 80% from 20 suppliers").
- **Steps automated** and **steps that stay manual**.
- **Exceptions**: what happens with unusual cases.
- **Systems and access**: apps, accounts and permissions needed, and who provides them.
- **Success measures**: time saved, error rate, turnaround, with a baseline.
- **Acceptance test**: how both sides will check it works.
- **Ownership and support**: who maintains it after delivery, for how long you support it, and what counts as a change.
- **Price and timeline**, if it is a client project.

## How it works

**Start from the process map.** Use the map from discovery to agree the boundaries. Mark each step automated, assisted (AI drafts, person approves) or manual.

**List inputs and exceptions honestly.** Ask for 20 to 50 real samples before agreeing anything. Count formats and exceptions. Decide for each exception: handle it, send it to a person, or exclude it.

**Agree the human checkpoints.** Which steps need approval (payments, customer messages, deletions)? Who approves, in which tool?

**Make access a dependency.** List every system, the type of account needed and the date access is due. Delays here are the most common cause of late projects.

**Define acceptance as a test.** "On 100 real invoices from last month, at least 85 are processed with no human touch and all fields match the accountant's entries; every failure appears in the review queue."

**Support and changes.** State the support period (for example, 30 days of fixes), what a change request is (a new supplier format, a new system), and how it is priced.

**Pricing models.** Fixed price for a clear scope; time and materials for unclear ones; a monthly retainer for ongoing maintenance. Include tool and AI usage costs, and who pays them.

## A worked example

A freelance automation specialist in Indore is asked by a distributor to "automate orders from WhatsApp".

Discovery shows: about 150 orders a day from 300 retailers, sent as WhatsApp text, voice notes and photos of handwritten lists in Hindi and English; staff type them into the billing software, which has an import function for CSV files.

The one-page scope:

- **In**: text and photo orders from the business WhatsApp number; AI extraction of retailer, products and quantities matched to the product list; a review screen where staff confirm each order; a daily CSV export for the billing software.
- **Out**: voice notes (phase 2), payments, stock checks, replying to retailers automatically.
- **Exceptions**: unknown products or retailers go to staff with the original message.
- **Access**: WhatsApp Business Platform account via a provider (client to set up by 10 December), product and retailer lists as sheets.
- **Success**: order entry time from about 3 minutes to under 1 minute per order; errors in shipped quantities not above the current rate.
- **Acceptance**: 200 past orders replayed; 90% of lines matched correctly before staff review.
- **Support**: 30 days of fixes; new formats or features as change requests at an agreed day rate.
- **Price**: fixed fee plus monthly running costs for the WhatsApp provider and AI usage, paid by the client.

Two weeks in, the owner asks to add automatic payment reminders. It goes on the phase 2 list with a quote.

## Common mistakes

- **Agreeing scope without samples.** Real inputs reveal the exceptions.
- **Silence on what is out.** Unwritten exclusions become free extra work.
- **Access as an afterthought.** It delays more projects than anything else.
- **Vague acceptance.** Define a test on real data.
- **No support terms.** You become unpaid support forever.

## Practise

Read Atlassian's guide to project scope and GOV.UK's guide to user stories.

1. Take an automation idea from your process map and collect at least 20 real or realistic input samples.
2. Write a one-page scope with every section above.
3. Write the acceptance test as something someone else could run.

**You are done when you can** hand your scope to the business owner and have them agree, in writing, what is in, what is out and how success is measured.
