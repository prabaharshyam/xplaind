## What it is

**Veo** is Google DeepMind's family of **text-to-video** models. You describe a shot, or give it images, and it generates a short, realistic clip. Since Veo 3 (2025), clips come with **synchronised sound**: ambience, effects and spoken lines that match the picture.

**Flow** is Google's AI filmmaking studio. You work like a director: generate shots, keep characters and products as reusable references, extend shots that end too early, and arrange clips into scenes.

The models inside Flow change often. As of 28 November 2026, Google's Flow help pages are reported to say:

- **Gemini Omni Flash 1.2**, released on 5 November 2026, is one of the two models Google suggests for new projects. It is not a Veo model. It makes clips of 3 to 15 seconds, with lip-synced dialogue, voices you pick, and editing of existing clips.
- **Veo 3.1 Lite** is the other, and the only Veo model that stays. It makes clips of 4, 6 or 8 seconds with sound, for fewer credits per second than Omni at 720p.
- **Veo 3.1 Fast**, **Veo 3.1 Quality** and **Omni Flash 1.1** retire from Flow on 5 December 2026. Many tutorials, including the videos linked here, still show them. Their directing advice still applies; only the names and prices have moved.

### How the model makes a clip

Veo is a **diffusion model**: its model card says Veo 3 uses latent diffusion, and Veo 3.1 Lite is built on Veo 3. It starts from random noise in a compressed form of video and removes the noise step by step, guided by your prompt and images, until frames appear that hang together over time. Two things follow for a director:

- The model predicts what *looks* likely, not what is true. It cannot know what your real saree or logo looks like unless you show it.
- Every generation is a fresh random draw, so the same prompt gives a different take each time. So make several takes, and lean on reference images.

## How it works

### Think in shots, not videos

Each generation is one shot of a few seconds; a 30-second ad is five or six shots joined in an editor. Write a **shot list** first: what we see, camera, sound and seconds. It is also your budget, since credits are charged per second or per clip.

A good default for a 30-second Reel: one establishing shot, two or three detail shots, one shot of a person using the product, and a title card. Keep generated shots between 4 and 8 seconds: they cost less, fail less and cut together more easily.

### Write prompts the way a cinematographer talks

A strong prompt describes one shot with one action. It covers six things:

| Element | What to say | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Subject | who or what, with fixed details | an elderly weaver with silver hair, cream kurta |
| Action | one clear action | slides the shuttle across the loom |
| Setting | place, time, weather | a dim workshop in Varanasi, morning light through a window |
| Camera | shot size, angle, movement, lens | close-up, slow dolly in, 50 mm, shallow depth of field |
| Lighting and look | mood, colour, texture | warm window light, low contrast, gentle film grain |
| Sound | ambience, effects, dialogue | clack of the loom, distant temple bells, no music |

"Make it look like a movie" means nothing to the model. Name the craft instead: lens (35 mm, anamorphic), movement (slow dolly in, handheld, drone push), lighting (low-key, practical lamps, golden hour), and texture (film grain, 24 fps motion blur). Say what you do *not* want too: "no text on screen", "no music", "no people".

For dialogue, give one short line per speaker per shot, in quotes, and say who speaks it. In Flow, with Omni Flash and ingredients, you can pick one voice per generation under the prompt box and refer to it in the prompt, so a character keeps the same voice.

### Choose the aspect ratio first

Use portrait 9:16 for Instagram Reels and Stories, YouTube Shorts and WhatsApp Status, and landscape 16:9 for YouTube videos, websites and presentations. Flow's help page lists both for Veo 3.1 Lite, and reports say Omni Flash 1.2 offers both; check that your mode offers 9:16. Set it before the first take and keep it for every shot: cropping landscape to portrait later throws away about two-thirds of the frame, often with your subject's face.

### Use the three ways to generate

- **Text to video:** the prompt alone. Good for establishing shots and moods, where exact looks do not matter.
- **Frames to video:** you give the first frame, and optionally the last. You control composition and the start and end of the move. Best for product shots and repairing continuity.
- **Ingredients to video** (also called references): you give several images, such as a character, a product and a style frame, and the model keeps them consistent. Best for the same person or product across shots.

Two finishing tools help too. **Extend** adds seconds to a clip that ends too early, and **editing** (Omni Flash 1.2) changes an existing clip by instruction, for example "make it dusk".

### Pick the model for the shot

| Shot need | Model to try first, as of November 2026 | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Character or product that must stay the same | Omni Flash 1.2 with ingredients | takes several references, can edit clips |
| A shot longer than 8 seconds | Omni Flash 1.2 | up to 15 seconds on paid plans, at a higher rate |
| Dialogue | Omni Flash 1.2 with ingredients and a chosen voice | lip-sync; one chosen voice per generation |
| Simple establishing or mood shot, up to 8 seconds | Veo 3.1 Lite | lower cost per second, sound included |
| Cheap test of an idea | Omni Flash 1.2 at 360p | 1 credit per second |

Treat this as a starting point: run one shot on both models and compare before committing a whole ad.

### Keep characters and products consistent

Consistency is the hardest part of AI video, and what most separates amateur from professional work.

1. **Make one strong reference image** per character and product. Photograph a real product in even daylight. For a character, generate a clear, front-facing image (Flow also has Google's image models; see the B2 lesson on AI image generation).
2. **Attach the same reference to every shot** where it appears, as an ingredient or first frame.
3. **Paste the same short description into every prompt**, word for word: "Meera, 25, short curly hair, mustard kurta, silver jhumkas".
4. **Join shots with frames.** Use the last frame of shot 4 as the first frame of shot 5.
5. **Change one thing per retry.** Make two or three takes and keep the best. If a take fails, change one thing; if you change the prompt, model and reference at once, you cannot tell what helped. Specific instructions beat adjectives: "hands stay on the loom", "no text on screen".

### Know what the models are bad at

Google's model card for Gemini Omni Flash says complex motion, perfectly accurate text and full consistency across edits are still hard. The other rows are common practice. Plan around them.

| Weakness | What you see | Workaround |
|---|---|---|
| On-screen text and logos | misspelt words, warped letters | add all text, prices and logos in the editor |
| Complex motion | limbs bend, objects merge in fast action | one simple action per shot, slower moves |
| Hands and fine detail | odd fingers, hands merging with objects | simple hand moves; budget extra takes |
| Crowds | faces blur and repeat | few people in front, a soft crowd behind |
| Drift between shots | a dress or face changes colour or shape | reuse the same reference and description; use frames to video |
| Long dialogue | lip-sync drifts, repeated words, voice changes | one short line per shot, a fixed voice |

### Edit outside the generator

Cut the clips in CapCut, DaVinci Resolve, Premiere or Flow's scene tools. Add music, voice-over, all text, the logo and the title card there. Even out loudness, since each clip's sound is mixed on its own.

### Budget your credits

Flow charges **credits** per generation. These figures are as reported from Google's pages, checked on 28 November 2026; the cost Flow shows when you pick a model wins if it differs.

| Access | Price in India | Flow credits |
|---|---|---|
| Free Google account | free | 50 credits a day, unused credits do not roll over |
| Google AI Plus | ₹399 a month | 200 credits a month |
| Google AI Pro | ₹1,950 a month | 1,000 credits a month |

Costs per generation, checked the same day:

- **Omni Flash 1.2:** 1 credit per second at 360p, 3 credits per second at 720p. Free users can make clips up to 10 seconds; 11 to 15 seconds and extending need a paid plan, and reports say each second after 10 costs double.
- **Veo 3.1 Lite:** 10 credits per clip of 4, 6 or 8 seconds at 720p on plans below Ultra.

Monthly credits reset each billing cycle and do not carry over.

### Publishing: labels, watermarks and rights

Check these before an ad goes live (as of 28 November 2026).

- **Watermarks.** Every Flow clip carries **SynthID**, an invisible watermark Google's tools can detect. Flow's help page also says a visible watermark is applied automatically if you live in India; press reports say only AI Ultra subscribers there can turn it off. Frame shots so it covers nothing important, and never crop or blur it out.
- **India's IT Rules.** Amendments in force from 20 February 2026 cover "synthetically generated" audio and video. Large social media platforms must ask users whether an upload is synthetic, check the answer, label synthetic content clearly, and not let labels or metadata be removed. Your part: answer truthfully and use the platform's AI label.
- **Instagram and Facebook.** Meta says it may require people to use its AI disclosure tool when posting photorealistic AI video or realistic AI audio, and may add an "AI info" label.
- **Advertising code.** ASCI, India's advertising self-regulator, issued guidelines on labelling AI content in ads in September 2026, due to take effect about three months later. They are risk-based: a label such as "Audio/Video created using AI" is needed where AI content could mislead a buyer, and fake endorsements or deepfakes made without consent are not allowed even with a label.
- **People and brands.** No public figures or other companies' logos; written consent for any real face in a reference.
- **Music.** A track allowed in a personal Reel may not be licensed for ads. Use music cleared for advertising, such as Meta's Sound Collection (its terms allow commercial use inside Meta's apps) or a stock licence that covers paid ads.
- **Commercial use.** Google says it does not claim ownership of what you generate, and Flow's help page says its Terms of Service govern how outputs may be used. Read the current terms before a paid campaign.

## A worked example

Rohan runs marketing for a small Banarasi handloom label. It cannot pay for a film crew, but he needs a 30-second Instagram Reel before Diwali. He has Google AI Pro (₹1,950 a month, 1,000 Flow credits, as of 28 November 2026). The numbers are an illustration of planning, not measured results.

**Step 1: the shot list,** all in portrait 9:16.

| Shot | What we see | Mode | Length |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Dawn in the workshop lane, a cycle passes, temple bells | text to video | 6 s |
| 2 | Close-up of the weaver's hands at the loom, clack of the shuttle | ingredients (weaver) | 5 s |
| 3 | Gold and red silk threads on the loom, slow dolly in | ingredients (saree photo) | 5 s |
| 4 | Meera in a Bengaluru flat opens the box, cardboard rustle | ingredients (Meera, saree) | 6 s |
| 5 | Meera drapes the saree in front of a mirror | ingredients (Meera, saree) | 5 s |
| 6 | Title card: brand name, "Handwoven in Varanasi" | made in the editor | 3 s |

The five generated shots total 27 seconds; the title card is made in the editor.

**Step 2: references.** He photographs the real saree flat in daylight, so the ad shows the true pattern. He generates one front-facing image of Meera, a fictional young woman, and one of the weaver, each with a fixed description he pastes into every prompt.

**Step 3: cheap drafts.** One draft per shot with Omni Flash 1.2 at 360p, to check framing and movement: 27 seconds × 1 credit = **27 credits**.

**Step 4: final takes.** Three takes per shot at 720p: 27 seconds × 3 credits × 3 takes = **243 credits**.

**Step 5: fixes.** In shot 2 the weaver has an extra finger. In shot 5 the saree border turns from red to maroon.

- Shot 2: he keeps the prompt, adds "two hands, natural anatomy, hands stay on the loom" and makes two more takes: 5 × 3 × 2 = **30 credits**.
- Shot 5: more adjectives would not help, because the model cannot know which red he means. He takes a still from shot 4 where the border is right and switches shot 5 to **frames to video** from that still. Two takes: 5 × 3 × 2 = **30 credits**.

**Total:** 27 + 243 + 30 + 30 = **330 credits**, about a third of the month's 1,000, or roughly ₹640 of the plan price (330 ÷ 1,000 × ₹1,950). On a free account it would take seven days of 50 daily credits; AI Plus's 200 monthly credits alone would not cover it.

Would Veo 3.1 Lite be cheaper? For shot 1, yes: 10 credits for 6 seconds, against 18 on Omni Flash 1.2 at 720p. Rohan tries shot 1 on both, but keeps the reference shots on Omni Flash 1.2.

**Step 6: edit and publish.** He cuts the best takes in CapCut, evens out loudness, and adds a sitar track licensed for ads. The brand name and "Handwoven in Varanasi" go in as editor text. The visible watermark sits over plain background. On upload he answers yes to Instagram's AI question, and the caption says the film was made with AI using the label's real sarees.

The brand keeps the references and descriptions, so its next festival posts start from them.

## Common mistakes

**One vague prompt for a whole video.** The model makes one shot, not a film. Plan a shot list, one action per shot.

**Changing descriptions between shots.** "A woman in a red saree" and "a lady in maroon" become two different people. Reuse exact wording and the same references.

**Too much in one shot.** Several actions, crowds and fast hands cause artefacts. Keep shots simple and slow; add energy with cuts.

**Asking the model for text, prices or logos.** Letters warp. Add all text in the editor.

**Wrong aspect ratio.** A landscape clip cropped for a Reel loses most of the frame. Choose 9:16 or 16:9 before the first take.

**Rewriting the whole prompt after a bad take.** You lose what worked. Change one thing, or switch mode, such as frames to video from a correct still.

**Trusting an old tutorial's models or prices.** Check Flow's model menu and costs the day you start.

**Hiding that it is AI, or using what you have no rights to.** Keep the watermark, answer the platform's AI question truthfully, and use only licensed music and consented faces.
