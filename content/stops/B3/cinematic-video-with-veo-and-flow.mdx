## What it is

**Veo** is Google DeepMind's family of **text-to-video** models. You describe a shot, or give it images, and it generates a short, realistic clip. Recent versions such as Veo 3 and Veo 3.1 also generate **synchronised sound**: ambience, effects and even spoken lines, which made AI video feel far more real.

**Flow** is Google's AI filmmaking tool built around Veo. Instead of one clip at a time, you work like a director: generate shots, keep your characters and objects as reusable references, extend shots, and arrange them into scenes. You reach Flow through Google's AI plans; check the current plans and credits on Google's site, as they change often.

Like image generators, Veo is based on **diffusion models**: it starts from noise and refines it, guided by your prompt, into frames that hang together over time.

```text
 IDEA ─► SHOT LIST (4-8 shots: subject, action, camera, light, sound)
            │
            ▼
 FLOW + VEO   text to video      ─► clip from words only
              frames to video    ─► clip from a first (and last) image
              ingredients        ─► clip built from character / product / style images
            │
            ▼
 review ─► regenerate weak shots ─► extend or trim ─► EDIT (order, music, titles) ─► export
```

## How it works

**Think in shots, not videos.** Each generation is a few seconds. A 30-second ad is five or six shots joined in an editor. Write a **shot list** first, like a real director.

**Write prompts the way a cinematographer talks.** A strong prompt covers:

| Element | What to say | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Subject | who or what, with specific details | an elderly weaver with silver hair, cream kurta |
| Action | one clear action per shot | slides the shuttle across the loom |
| Setting | place, time, weather | a dim workshop in Varanasi, morning light through a window |
| Camera | shot size, angle, movement, lens | close-up, slow dolly in, 50 mm, shallow depth of field |
| Lighting and look | mood, colour grade | warm, soft window light, gentle film grain |
| Sound (Veo with audio) | ambience, effects, dialogue | the clack of the loom, distant temple bells |

**Use the three main ways to generate in Flow.**

- **Text to video:** the prompt alone. Good for establishing shots and ideas.
- **Frames to video:** start from an image you made or photographed, optionally ending on another, so you control composition. Ideal for product shots and continuity.
- **Ingredients to video:** give several reference images, such as your character, your product and a style frame, and Veo builds a clip that keeps them consistent.

**Keep things consistent.** Create a strong reference image of each character or product first (with an image model, see the next guide). Reuse it in every shot and repeat the same short description in each prompt.

**Iterate.** Generate two to four versions of each shot and keep the best. Fix problems with specific changes ("hands stay on the loom", "no text on screen") rather than rewriting everything.

**Edit.** Download the clips and cut them in any editor, such as CapCut, DaVinci Resolve or Premiere, or Flow's own scene tools. Add music, a voice-over and a title card.

**Budget your credits.** Each generation uses credits from your plan, and higher-quality modes use more. Plan your shots on paper first, test ideas with faster or cheaper modes where offered, and save the best settings for final takes.

## A worked example

Rohan runs marketing for a small Banarasi handloom label. Their budget cannot cover a film crew, but he needs a 30-second ad for Instagram before Diwali.

He writes six shots: a dawn exterior of the workshop lane; a close-up of the weaver's hands at the loom; silk threads in gold and red; a young woman in Bengaluru unboxing the saree; her draping it in a mirror; and a title card with the brand name.

He photographs the real saree flat in daylight and uses it as an **ingredient** in shots 3 to 5, so the pattern stays true. He makes a reference image of the young woman with an image model and reuses it in shots 4 and 5. His prompts specify camera moves and sound: "clack of the loom", "soft temple bells", "cardboard rustle".

The first pass has two problems: the weaver has six fingers in one shot, and the saree's border changes colour in shot 5. He regenerates the hand shot with "close-up of two hands, natural anatomy" and switches shot 5 to **frames to video**, starting from a still of the correct border. He edits the best takes in CapCut, adds a licensed sitar track and a title card, and labels the post as made with AI.

The ad costs a day of work and a few hundred credits instead of a shoot. The brand later uses the same references for three more festival posts.

## Common mistakes

**One vague prompt for a whole video.** Plan shots. Each prompt should describe one shot with one action.

**Changing descriptions between shots.** The model treats "a woman in a red saree" and "a lady in maroon" as different people. Reuse exact wording and reference images.

**Too much in one shot.** Several actions, many people and complex hand movements cause artefacts. Keep each shot simple.

**Ignoring sound.** Veo's audio is part of the realism. Describe it, or turn it off and add your own.

**Using real people or brands without permission.** Do not generate public figures or other companies' logos, and get consent for anyone you base a character on.

**Not labelling AI content.** Platforms increasingly require it, and audiences trust brands that are open about it.
