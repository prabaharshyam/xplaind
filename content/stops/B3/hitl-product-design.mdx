## What it is

**Human in the loop** means people are part of how an AI feature works, not just users of its output. Four common roles:

- **Review**: a person checks AI output before it is used (a draft reply, an extracted invoice).
- **Approve**: a person authorises an AI-proposed action (a refund, a message to a customer).
- **Handle exceptions**: the AI handles routine cases; people take the hard or uncertain ones.
- **Audit**: people check a sample after the fact to monitor quality.

The product question is not "should there be a human?" but **where, for which cases, with what information, and how fast**.

## How it works

**Decide by risk and confidence.** Map cases by how costly a mistake is and how likely the AI is to be right. Low cost and high confidence: automate, audit a sample. High cost or low confidence: review or approve. Unusual cases: route to experts.

**Design the review moment.** Show the AI output next to its sources, highlight what needs checking (amounts, names, dates, uncertain fields), and make approve, edit and reject one action each. If reviewers must open other systems to decide, they will stop checking.

**Watch for automation bias.** People tend to accept machine suggestions, especially when busy. Counter it: highlight uncertainty, sometimes require the reviewer to confirm key fields actively, measure override rates, and inject known test cases occasionally to check attention.

**Plan capacity.** Reviews take time. Estimate volume × review minutes and make sure the team has it; otherwise queues build and people rush.

**Clarify accountability.** Who is responsible when an approved AI output is wrong? Usually the approver, which is only fair if they had what they needed. Write it down.

**Use the loop to improve.** Edits and rejections are the best data you have: feed them into evaluation sets and fixes.

**Evolve oversight with evidence.** Start strict. As measured quality holds, move low-risk cases from approval to audit. Keep high-risk cases with people. Document each change.

**Users as the human.** In consumer products, the user often is the reviewer: drafts they edit, suggestions they accept. Design so accepting is never the only easy option.

## A worked example

A Bengaluru lending app uses AI to pre-fill loan application reviews: it extracts income from bank statements, flags risks and suggests approve, reject or refer.

The PM, Arjun, designs the human role:

- **Approve or reject decisions** stay with credit officers for every application; the AI only suggests. Regulation and fairness require it.
- **Review screen**: the suggested decision, the three main reasons, the extracted income with the bank statement lines highlighted, and flags in red. Officers must confirm the income figure with one tap before deciding.
- **Overrides** are tracked by officer and reason. In the first month, officers override 14% of AI suggestions; the most common reason ("salary credited in two parts") leads to an extraction fix.
- **Capacity**: 600 applications a day × 3 minutes = 30 officer-hours, within the team's capacity.
- **Audit**: a senior credit manager reviews 5% of approved files weekly.

After three months the review time drops from 9 minutes to 3, and default rates on AI-assisted approvals match the previous manual process. Decisions stay with officers.

## Common mistakes

- **A human in name only.** Without information and time, review is a rubber stamp.
- **Reviewing everything forever.** Move low-risk cases to audit as evidence builds.
- **Ignoring capacity.** Review queues grow and quality drops.
- **Unclear accountability.** Write down who answers for approved outputs.
- **Wasting overrides.** They are the best signal for improvement.

## Practise

Read the PAIR Guidebook chapters on feedback and control, and the Human-AI Interaction guidelines.

1. For an AI feature of your choice, map case types by mistake cost and AI confidence, and assign each to automate, review, approve, exception or audit.
2. Sketch the review screen with sources, highlighted fields and one-click actions.
3. Estimate review capacity and write the rule for when oversight can be relaxed.

**You are done when you can** show where people sit in your feature, what they see, how long it takes and how it will change with evidence.
