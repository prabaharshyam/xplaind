## What it is

Many Indian businesses still run on software that cannot talk to anything else: a 15-year-old desktop ERP, a bank's back-office screens, a government portal with no API. People spend their days copying values from one screen to another. **Robotic process automation (RPA)** takes over that copying.

RPA uses software "robots" that operate applications through their **user interface**, as a person would: opening programs, clicking buttons, typing into fields, copying values from screens, downloading reports. It is used where systems have no API, or where building an integration would take too long.

```text
   API automation                          RPA
 +-----------+   data   +-----------+    +-----------+  clicks,   +--------------+
 | System A  | -------> | System B  |    |  Robot    |  typing    | Old desktop  |
 | (has API) |  direct  | (has API) |    | (UiPath,  | ---------> | app, no API  |
 +-----------+          +-----------+    |  PA Desk) |  on screen +--------------+
   fast, sturdy, preferred               works anywhere a person can, more fragile
```

Leading RPA tools include **UiPath**, **Automation Anywhere** and **Microsoft Power Automate Desktop**. They let you record or design the steps, then run them on a schedule or on demand, on a desktop or a server.

This lesson follows the claims team of a general insurer in Kolkata as an AI step and an RPA robot together take over data entry into an old claims system. By the end you will know when RPA is the right tool and what it costs to keep running.

## How it works

### Attended and unattended robots

| Type | Runs | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Attended** | On a person's computer, when they start it | A robot that fills a 40-field form after the agent clicks "Go" |
| **Unattended** | On its own, on dedicated machines, working through a queue | A robot that enters 300 claims overnight |

### How robots find things on screen

Good RPA does not click at fixed screen positions. It identifies elements by their properties: a button's name, a field's label, a web page's structure. These identifiers are called **selectors**. Small layout changes then do not break the robot.

For old applications or remote desktops, where properties are not available, robots may fall back on **image matching** or **OCR**, which are more fragile: a changed colour or font can break them.

### Where RPA fits

- Legacy desktop applications, such as older ERPs and banking back-office screens, and portals without APIs.
- High-volume, stable, rule-based tasks: copying data between systems, generating and downloading daily reports, entering orders.
- A bridge while a proper integration is built.

### Where RPA does not fit

| Situation | Better choice |
| --- | --- |
| The system has a good API | Use the API: faster and sturdier |
| The process or screens change often | Fix the process first, or wait |
| The task needs judgement on varied inputs | An AI step plus a person, with RPA only for data entry |

### RPA plus AI

RPA moves data through screens; AI reads documents, classifies emails or decides the next step. Together they cover processes neither handles alone:

```text
  Email with claim  -->  [AI step] read email and attachment,
                            extract policy no., vehicle no., date, place
                                 |
                          [Validate against policy database]
                                 |
                     ok ---------+--------- not ok
                     v                        v
             [RPA robot] type into       [Person's queue]
             old claims system           with screenshots
                     |
             claim number back to tracking sheet
```

**Computer-use agents**, AI that operates screens from screenshots, are newer and more flexible, but slower, costlier and less predictable than designed RPA steps. A common pattern: RPA for the stable steps, AI only where variation needs judgement.

### Running costs and governance

Robots break when screens change, passwords expire or unexpected pop-ups appear. Budget for:

- **licences** for the RPA platform and robot machines;
- **monitoring**, so a stopped robot raises an alert;
- **maintenance**, someone who can fix selectors quickly.

Keep a register of every robot: its owner, the systems it touches, and the credentials it uses. Give robots **least-privilege** accounts, with only the access they need, and store their passwords in a vault, not in the robot's script.

## A worked example

A general insurer in Kolkata receives motor claim intimations by email: about 300 a day. The claims team copies details from each email and its attachments into an old desktop claims system that has no API. Each claim takes about 6 minutes, roughly 30 hours of work a day across the team.

**The solution:**

1. An **AI extraction step** reads each intimation email and attachment and produces structured fields: policy number, vehicle number, date and place of loss, description.
2. The fields are **validated** against the policy database: does the policy exist, is it active, does the vehicle match?
3. An **unattended RPA robot** opens the claims system, goes to "New intimation", fills in the validated fields, saves, and writes the new claim number back to the tracking sheet.
4. Failed validations, and any screen the robot does not recognise, go to a **person's queue** with screenshots.

**Results in the first quarter:**

| Measure | Before | After |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Staff time on data entry | about 30 hours a day | about 6 hours a day, mostly exceptions |
| Claims entered the same day | about 70% | about 98% |
| Robot outages | Not applicable | 2, both after system updates moved fields |
| Time to fix each outage | Not applicable | under a day; monitoring alerted the RPA developer |

The insurer plans to replace the robot with a direct API integration when the claims system is upgraded next year. Until then, the robot is the bridge.

## Common mistakes

- **RPA where an API exists.** APIs are faster and sturdier.
- **Fixed screen positions.** Use element properties so small changes do not break robots.
- **Automating unstable processes.** Robots break whenever the process or screens change.
- **No monitoring or owner.** Broken robots fail silently, and work piles up unseen.
- **Robots with admin credentials.** Use least-privilege accounts stored in a vault.
- **Forgetting the exits.** Plan which cases go to a person, and make that queue easy to work.

## Practise

Take a free beginner course at UiPath Academy, linked below, or the Power Automate Desktop modules on Microsoft Learn.

1. **Build.** Make a simple robot that opens a website or desktop app, fills a form from a spreadsheet row and saves a confirmation.
2. **Break it.** Change the layout slightly, by resizing the window or changing screen resolution, and see whether it still works. Fix it with better selectors.
3. **Choose.** For one process in your organisation, write down whether RPA, an API integration or an AI agent fits best, and why.

**You are done when you can** explain when you would choose RPA over an API or an AI agent, and show a robot that survives a small layout change.
