## What it is

**Return on investment (ROI)** compares what an automation gives back with what it costs. For automation the gains are usually:

- **Time saved**: staff hours no longer spent on the task.
- **Errors avoided**: rework, penalties, wrong payments or lost orders prevented.
- **Speed**: faster responses or processing, which can mean more sales or happier customers.
- **Capacity**: handling more volume without hiring.

The costs are:

- **Build**: your time or fee.
- **Running**: tool subscriptions, AI usage, messaging fees, hosting.
- **Maintenance**: fixing and updating the automation.
- **Human review**: time people still spend checking or handling exceptions.

## How it works

**Measure the baseline first.** Volume per month, time per item, error rate, turnaround time, before you change anything. Use real data where possible: timestamps, logs, or a two-week sample where staff note start and end times.

**Estimate before building.**

Monthly time saved (hours) = items per month × minutes saved per item ÷ 60.

Value of time = hours saved × loaded hourly cost (salary plus overheads), or the value of what staff now do instead.

Add errors avoided × cost per error, and any revenue from speed (with care; be conservative).

Subtract running and maintenance costs per month. Payback period = build cost ÷ net monthly benefit.

**Be honest about assumptions.** Show your inputs, use ranges (low, expected, high) and remember that staff time saved is only valuable if it is used for something worthwhile.

**Measure after launch.** Compare the same measures with the baseline after one to three months: actual items processed automatically, remaining human time on exceptions and reviews, error rates, running costs from invoices.

**Report simply.** One page: baseline, now, savings, costs, net benefit, payback, and what you would improve next.

## A worked example

The Indore distributor's WhatsApp order automation (from the scoping lesson):

**Baseline** (two-week sample): 150 orders a day, 26 working days, about 3 minutes each to type in; 2% of orders had a wrong quantity, each costing about ₹400 in returns and re-delivery.

**Estimate**: 3,900 orders a month. If entry drops to 1 minute (review only), time saved = 3,900 × 2 ÷ 60 = 130 hours a month. At a loaded cost of ₹250 an hour, that is ₹32,500 a month. If wrong quantities halve, 39 fewer errors × ₹400 = ₹15,600. Running costs (WhatsApp provider, AI usage, hosting): about ₹9,000 a month. Net benefit about ₹39,100 a month. Build fee ₹90,000; payback about 2.3 months.

**After three months**: 82% of orders processed with only a quick confirmation, average handling 1.2 minutes, wrong quantities down to 0.9%. Measured net benefit about ₹36,000 a month, slightly below estimate because voice-note orders (out of scope) still need typing. The report recommends phase 2 for voice notes, with its own estimate. The owner approves.

## Common mistakes

- **No baseline.** You cannot prove anything afterwards.
- **Counting only build cost.** Running and maintenance costs continue every month.
- **Ignoring remaining human work.** Reviews and exceptions still take time.
- **Optimistic single numbers.** Use ranges and conservative assumptions.
- **Claiming savings nobody uses.** Saved hours matter when they go to valuable work or avoided hiring.

## Practise

Read GOV.UK's guide to measuring success, and look up current model prices on an independent comparison site for the AI-cost line.

1. Measure a baseline for one task you could automate: volume, minutes per item and error rate over a week.
2. Build an ROI estimate with low, expected and high cases, including running costs and payback.
3. Write the one-page after-launch report template you will fill in.

**You are done when you can** present an ROI estimate with clear assumptions and say what you will measure to prove it.
