## What it is

Every automation project eventually faces the question: **was it worth it?** Business owners and managers will ask before they pay and again after launch. **Return on investment (ROI)** compares what an automation gives back with what it costs.

| Gains | Costs |
| --- | --- |
| **Time saved**: staff hours no longer spent on the task | **Build**: your time or fee |
| **Errors avoided**: rework, penalties, wrong payments or lost orders prevented | **Running**: tool subscriptions, AI usage, messaging fees, hosting |
| **Speed**: faster responses or processing, which can mean more sales or happier customers | **Maintenance**: fixing and updating the automation |
| **Capacity**: handling more volume without hiring | **Human review**: time people still spend checking or handling exceptions |

```text
   BEFORE (baseline)            AFTER (measured)
 +--------------------+       +--------------------+
 | 150 orders a day   |       | 150 orders a day   |
 | 3 min each         |  -->  | 1.2 min each       |
 | 2% wrong qty       |       | 0.9% wrong qty     |
 +--------------------+       +--------------------+
            \                        /
             +-- difference x value -+--> gains
                                          minus costs
                                          = net benefit
```

This lesson follows a distributor in Indore whose WhatsApp order automation must prove its value to the owner. By the end you will present an ROI estimate with clear assumptions and know what to measure to prove it.

## How it works

### 1. Measure the baseline first

Before you change anything, measure: volume per month, time per item, error rate and turnaround time. Use real data where you can: timestamps in emails, system logs, or a two-week sample in which staff note start and end times for each item. Without a baseline you can never prove anything afterwards.

### 2. Estimate before building

```text
  Monthly hours saved  =  items per month  x  minutes saved per item  /  60

  Value of time        =  hours saved  x  loaded hourly cost

  Value of errors      =  errors avoided per month  x  cost per error

  Net monthly benefit  =  value of time + value of errors (+ careful revenue gains)
                          - running costs - maintenance

  Payback (months)     =  build cost  /  net monthly benefit
```

A **loaded hourly cost** is salary plus overheads, such as benefits, office and equipment, divided by working hours. It is usually well above the salary alone.

Be conservative about **revenue from speed**. Faster replies may win more orders, but it is hard to prove, so show it separately or leave it out.

### 3. Be honest about assumptions

- Show every input, so others can check and change them.
- Use **ranges**: low, expected and high.
- Remember that saved staff time is only valuable if it is used for something worthwhile, or avoids hiring.

| Input | Low | Expected | High |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Minutes saved per order | 1.5 | 2 | 2.5 |
| Error reduction | 25% | 50% | 70% |
| Running cost a month | ₹11,000 | ₹9,000 | ₹8,000 |

### Benefits that are not in rupees

Some gains are real but hard to price: staff who no longer dread month-end, customers who get answers in minutes instead of a day, fewer late-night calls to the owner. Do not invent rupee values for them. List them separately under "other benefits", with whatever evidence you have, such as a drop in complaints or overtime hours. Decision-makers value honesty here more than a bigger number, and it protects your credibility when the measured figures arrive.

### 4. Measure after launch

After one to three months, compare the same measures with the baseline: items processed automatically, remaining human time on exceptions and reviews, error rates, and running costs from real invoices, not estimates.

### 5. Report simply

One page: baseline, now, savings, costs, net benefit, payback, and what you would improve next. Owners read one page; they do not read twelve.

## A worked example

A distributor in Indore takes orders from 300 shops over WhatsApp. Staff type each order into the billing system by hand. The automation reads WhatsApp orders, extracts items and quantities with an AI step, and puts them into the billing system for a quick human confirmation.

**Baseline** (two-week sample): 150 orders a day over 26 working days, about 3 minutes each to type in. 2% of orders had a wrong quantity, each costing about ₹400 in returns and re-delivery.

**Estimate:**

| Line | Calculation | Monthly value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Orders a month | 150 × 26 | 3,900 |
| Hours saved | 3,900 × (3 − 1) ÷ 60 | 130 hours |
| Value of time | 130 × ₹250 loaded cost | ₹32,500 |
| Errors avoided | 2% to 1% of 3,900 = 39 fewer × ₹400 | ₹15,600 |
| Running costs | WhatsApp provider, AI usage, hosting | −₹9,000 |
| **Net benefit** | | **₹39,100** |
| Payback | ₹90,000 build fee ÷ ₹39,100 | **about 2.3 months** |

**After three months, measured:**

| Measure | Baseline | Estimated | Measured |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Orders handled with only a quick confirmation | 0% | 100% | 82% |
| Average handling time | 3 min | 1 min | 1.2 min |
| Wrong quantities | 2% | 1% | 0.9% |
| Net monthly benefit | | ₹39,100 | about ₹36,000 |

The measured benefit is slightly below the estimate, because voice-note orders, which were out of scope, still need typing. The one-page report says so plainly and recommends a phase 2 for voice notes, with its own estimate. The owner approves, because the first estimate turned out honest.

## Common mistakes

- **No baseline.** You cannot prove anything afterwards.
- **Counting only build cost.** Running and maintenance costs continue every month.
- **Ignoring remaining human work.** Reviews and exceptions still take time.
- **Optimistic single numbers.** Use ranges and conservative assumptions.
- **Claiming savings nobody uses.** Saved hours matter when they go to valuable work or avoid hiring.
- **Using salary instead of loaded cost**, or the reverse, without saying which.
