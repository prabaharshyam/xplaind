## What it is

A **human approval step** pauses an agent before an action takes effect, shows a person what the agent wants to do and why, and continues only when they approve, edit or reject it. It is the most common form of human-in-the-loop in production agents.

Approval is for actions that are costly, irreversible, external or regulated: refunds and payments, emails and messages to customers, changes to records, deleting anything, anything that touches legal or medical decisions. Reading data rarely needs approval.

## How it works

**Classify actions by risk.** List every tool and rate it: read-only, reversible internal change, irreversible or external. A simple rule set decides what needs approval: always for irreversible or external actions; for others above a threshold (refunds over ₹2,000); never for reads.

**Pause durably.** The agent must stop and wait, possibly for hours, without holding a server process open. Frameworks with checkpoints do this: state is saved, the run ends in an "interrupted" state, and a later call resumes from the same point with the person's decision.

**Show the right information.** The approval screen should show the proposed action in plain words ("Refund ₹4,500 to UPI ID ...@okaxis for order 7781"), the agent's reason, the evidence it used (the order, the policy clause), and one-click approve, edit and reject. If the approver must open three other systems to decide, they will start approving blindly.

**Edit and feedback.** Let the approver change parameters (₹4,500 to ₹3,000) and add a note. Both go back into the agent's state so it can explain the change to the customer, and into your logs as training and eval data.

**Measure.** Track approval rate, edit rate, rejection reasons and time to decide. A rejection rate near zero for months may mean the step can be loosened for low-risk cases. A falling time-to-decide with no edits may mean rubber-stamping.

**Timeouts and escalation.** Decide what happens if nobody responds: remind, escalate to another approver, or cancel safely. Never default to "approve".

## A worked example

An e-commerce company in Jaipur deploys an agent to handle return and refund requests.

Risk rules: reading orders needs no approval; issuing a return label is reversible and auto-approved; refunds under ₹1,000 for orders under 30 days auto-approve; all other refunds need a support lead's approval; any message mentioning legal action goes to a person entirely.

The approval card reads: "Refund ₹2,499 for order 7781 (blue kurta, size M). Customer says it arrived torn; photo attached. Policy 4.2 allows full refund for damage reported within 7 days; reported on day 3." Approve, Edit amount, Reject with reason.

In the first month, leads approve 91%, edit 6% (mostly partial refunds where only one item of a set was damaged) and reject 3% (photos showing no damage). Median decision time is 40 seconds. You add the edit cases to the agent's examples, after which edits fall to 2%. With that evidence, the client raises the auto-approve threshold to ₹1,500, which removes a third of approvals without increasing complaints.

## Common mistakes

- **Approving everything or nothing.** Approval on every read wastes people's time; none on payments is reckless. Rate actions by risk.
- **Holding a process open while waiting.** Approvals can take hours. Save state and resume later.
- **Approval screens without evidence.** People cannot judge, so they click approve.
- **Defaulting to approve on timeout.** Silence is not consent. Escalate or cancel.
- **Not learning from edits.** Edits and rejections are the best data you have about where the agent is wrong.

## Practise

Read LangGraph's interrupts guide, or the approval section of the agents-in-LangGraph course.

1. List the tools of an agent you have built and rate each read-only, reversible or irreversible. Write the approval rules.
2. Add a durable pause before one risky tool, and a small script or page that shows the proposed action with its reason and lets you approve, edit or reject.
3. Log every decision with the time taken, and write the query that would show approval, edit and rejection rates.

**You are done when you can** pause your agent, close the program, restart it the next day and resume from the approval.
