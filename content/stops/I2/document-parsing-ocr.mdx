## What it is

**Document parsing** turns files into text and structure a RAG system can use: paragraphs in the right reading order, headings that mark sections, tables with rows and columns intact, and page numbers for citations. Three approaches, often combined:

- **Text extraction** from digital PDFs and Office files: fast and cheap, but blind to layout.
- **OCR** (optical character recognition) for scans and photos: turns images of text into text.
- **Layout-aware parsing**: models that detect headings, columns, tables, figures and reading order, then extract each part properly. Open-source tools such as Docling and Unstructured, and cloud document services, do this. Vision-language models can also read pages directly.

## How it works

**Detect the document type first.** Digital PDF, scanned PDF, mixed, Word, slides, spreadsheet or image. A page with very few extractable characters is probably a scan.

**Reading order.** Simple extraction reads across two-column pages line by line, mixing the columns. Layout analysis finds the columns and reads each in turn.

**Tables.** Flattened tables lose meaning: "Premium 12,450 Sum insured 5,00,000" becomes ambiguous across rows. Extract tables as structured data, then turn each into text that keeps headers with values (Markdown or one line per row: "Plan Gold: premium ₹12,450, sum insured ₹5,00,000"). Keep a whole table, or whole rows with headers, in one chunk.

**Headings and sections.** Keep heading text and the section path ("Part B > Exclusions > 4.2") with each chunk. It helps retrieval, filters and citations.

**OCR quality.** Image quality decides accuracy: resolution, skew, shadows, stamps and handwriting. Indian documents often mix English with Hindi, Tamil or other scripts; use OCR that supports them and set the languages. Pre-process images (straighten, increase contrast) and keep OCR confidence scores to flag weak pages.

**Headers, footers and noise.** Repeated page headers, footers, page numbers and watermarks pollute chunks. Remove them by detecting text repeated on most pages.

**Check the output.** Sample pages and compare with the originals, especially tables and scanned pages. Track per document: pages, characters per page, tables found, OCR confidence.

## A worked example

A Kochi insurance company's RAG assistant gives wrong answers about premiums and exclusions. Retrieval logs show the right pages are found, but the text is garbled.

Inspection of its 600 policy documents shows:

- Two-column policy wordings were read across columns, merging unrelated sentences.
- Premium tables were flattened, so the model mixed up plans and ages.
- 70 older endorsements were scans with no text at all.
- Every page carried a repeated header and a disclaimer footer.

You switch to a layout-aware parser for all PDFs, with OCR enabled for English and Malayalam on pages with little text. Tables are converted to one line per row with column headers repeated. Section paths are stored with each chunk, and repeated headers and footers are stripped.

On the 80-question golden set, correct answers rise from 62% to 84% without any change to retrieval or prompts. Twelve pages still have OCR confidence below the threshold; you list them for the client, who supplies better scans for nine.

## Common mistakes

- **One extractor for everything.** Scans, digital PDFs and slides need different handling.
- **Flattening tables.** Keep headers with values, and rows together.
- **Ignoring reading order.** Multi-column pages are common in policies, circulars and reports.
- **English-only OCR.** Set the languages your documents use.
- **Never comparing output with the page.** Ten minutes of spot checks finds most parsing bugs.

## Practise

Read Docling's overview and Unstructured's article on parsing PDFs.

1. Collect five varied documents: a two-column report, a document with a table, a scanned page, a slide deck and a government circular in an Indian language.
2. Parse them with simple text extraction and with a layout-aware tool, and compare the outputs for reading order and tables.
3. Convert one table into row-wise text with headers, and measure whether answers to three table questions improve in your RAG app.

**You are done when you can** show the same page parsed badly and well, and explain which parsing choice fixed it.
