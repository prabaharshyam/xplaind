## What it is

A **chat assistant** answers customers' questions in a chat window on a website or in a messaging app. Built well, it:

- answers from the business's own information (FAQs, price lists, policies, timings) rather than general knowledge;
- handles common tasks (order status, appointment booking) through connections to business systems;
- **hands over to a person** when it cannot help or the customer asks;
- respects privacy and the messaging platform's rules.

On WhatsApp, businesses use the **WhatsApp Business Platform** (the API), usually through a provider. It has rules: customers must opt in, business-started messages outside a 24-hour customer-service window need pre-approved templates, and messages are charged according to WhatsApp's pricing model.

```flow
Customer on WhatsApp -> Provider webhook -> Assistant
Assistant -> Retrieval | FAQ sheet, price list
Assistant -> Tools | order status, booking, with confirmation
Assistant -> Hand-over to a person | with a summary
Retrieval -> Reply in the customer's language
Tools -> Reply in the customer's language
```

This lesson follows a multi-specialty clinic in Madurai whose reception is flooded with WhatsApp messages.

## How it works

**Knowledge.** Collect the business's FAQs, price list, policies and timings. Keep them in a document or sheet the business can update. Use retrieval so answers come from these sources, and instruct the assistant to say when it does not know.

**Actions.** For order status or booking, connect tools: look up an order by number and phone, check slots, create a booking. Confirm details back to the customer before acting.

**Hand-over.** Offer a "talk to a person" option at any time. Hand over automatically for complaints, payments disputes, medical or legal questions, angry customers, or after two failed answers. Pass the conversation and a summary so the person need not ask again, and tell the customer when they will hear back.

**WhatsApp rules.**

- Customers must have opted in to receive messages from the business.
- Inside 24 hours of the customer's last message, the business can reply freely. After that, only approved template messages may start a conversation.
- Follow WhatsApp's commerce and business policies on what may be sold or promoted.

**Language and tone.** Answer in the customer's language where possible; test Hindi, Tamil, Hinglish and others your customers use. Keep messages short; chat is not email.

**Privacy.** Collect only what the task needs; do not ask for full card numbers or passwords; follow DPDP consent and retention rules; mask personal data in logs.

**Measure.** Share of conversations resolved without a person, hand-over rate and reasons, customer ratings, and wrong-answer reports.

**What the assistant handles, and what it never does:**

| Request type | Assistant's job | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Information | Answer from the business's sources | Timings, fees, doctor schedules |
| Simple actions | Use a tool, confirm before acting | Book an appointment, check order status |
| Sensitive or complex | Hand over with a summary | Complaints, refunds, disputes |
| Professional advice | Never; hand over | Medical, legal, financial questions |
| Emergencies | Fixed safe message and hand-over | "Call our emergency number..." |

**Message costs.** WhatsApp charges businesses according to its pricing model, which depends on the type of message and the country. Check the current pricing page and include it in the client's running-cost estimate, with the date you checked.

**A WhatsApp Cloud API webhook in code.** Meta calls your URL once to verify it, then posts every incoming message. A minimal FastAPI version; check Meta's current docs for the API version and fields:

```python
import hashlib, hmac, os
import httpx
from fastapi import FastAPI, Request, Response, BackgroundTasks, HTTPException

app = FastAPI()
VERIFY_TOKEN = os.environ["WA_VERIFY_TOKEN"]
APP_SECRET = os.environ["WA_APP_SECRET"].encode()
TOKEN, PHONE_ID = os.environ["WA_TOKEN"], os.environ["WA_PHONE_NUMBER_ID"]
SEEN = set()                                     # use Redis or a table in production

@app.get("/whatsapp")
def verify(request: Request):
    q = request.query_params
    if q.get("hub.mode") == "subscribe" and q.get("hub.verify_token") == VERIFY_TOKEN:
        return Response(q.get("hub.challenge"))
    raise HTTPException(status_code=403)

@app.post("/whatsapp")
async def receive(request: Request, tasks: BackgroundTasks):
    raw = await request.body()
    sig = request.headers.get("X-Hub-Signature-256", "")
    expected = "sha256=" + hmac.new(APP_SECRET, raw, hashlib.sha256).hexdigest()
    if not hmac.compare_digest(sig, expected):
        raise HTTPException(status_code=401)
    for entry in (await request.json()).get("entry", []):
        for change in entry.get("changes", []):
            for msg in change["value"].get("messages", []):
                if msg["id"] in SEEN:
                    continue                     # a repeat delivery
                SEEN.add(msg["id"])
                if msg.get("type") == "text":
                    tasks.add_task(handle, msg["from"], msg["text"]["body"])
    return {"ok": True}                          # reply fast; work happens in the background

async def handle(wa_id, text):
    answer = assistant_reply(wa_id, text)        # retrieval, tools and hand-over rules
    async with httpx.AsyncClient() as http:
        await http.post(f"https://graph.facebook.com/v21.0/{PHONE_ID}/messages",
                        headers={"Authorization": f"Bearer {TOKEN}"},
                        json={"messaging_product": "whatsapp", "to": wa_id,
                              "type": "text", "text": {"body": answer[:4000]}})
```

Answer the webhook within a few seconds and do the model call in the background, or WhatsApp may retry and you will see duplicates.

**Hand-over rules in code, not only the prompt.** Decide before calling the model, from the message and the conversation so far:

```python
HANDOVER_WORDS = {"complaint", "refund", "lawyer", "consumer court", "chargeback"}

def needs_person(text, state):
    t = text.lower()
    return (any(w in t for w in HANDOVER_WORDS)
            or state["failed_answers"] >= 2
            or state.get("asked_for_person"))
```

When it returns true, stop the bot for that chat, post the conversation and a summary to the staff inbox, and tell the customer when they will hear back.

**The 24-hour window in code.** Store the time of each customer's last message. Before sending, check whether it was within 24 hours; if not, send an approved template instead of free text, or do not send at all.

**Staying grounded.** Answer from the business's own sheet or documents with retrieval, include the source in logs, and refuse when nothing relevant is found: "I don't have that information; I've asked the team to reply." A wrong price or timing sent on WhatsApp is screenshotted and shared.

## A worked example

A multi-specialty clinic in Madurai gets hundreds of WhatsApp messages a day: timings, doctor availability, fees, lab report status, appointment booking.

The assistant, built in n8n with a WhatsApp Business Platform provider:

- answers timings, fees and doctor schedules from a sheet the reception staff update daily;
- books appointments by checking the clinic's booking system and confirming name, doctor, date and time before booking;
- tells patients whether lab reports are ready (after verifying the registered phone number), but never shares results in chat;
- hands over immediately for symptoms, medical questions or emergencies, with a fixed message giving the emergency number;
- replies in Tamil or English, matching the patient.

After a month, 68% of conversations are fully handled, reception's phone calls drop by a third, and after-hours bookings increase. Two wrong answers about a doctor's leave day trace back to the schedule sheet not being updated; the clinic adds a daily check.

| Measure after one month | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Conversations fully handled by the assistant | 68% |
| Reception phone calls | Down by about a third |
| After-hours bookings | Up |
| Wrong answers found | 2, both from an out-of-date schedule sheet |
| Fix | A daily check that the schedule sheet was updated |

**How the hand-over works in practice.** When a patient writes "my father has chest pain", the assistant does not try to help medically. It sends the fixed emergency message with the ambulance and clinic emergency numbers, flags the conversation as urgent in the staff inbox, and stops replying automatically to that patient for the rest of the day, so a person stays in charge. When a patient asks about a bill they think is wrong, the assistant collects the bill number, says a staff member will reply within two working hours, and passes a one-line summary with the bill attached. Reception staff said this was the change they valued most: they no longer had to scroll back through a long chat to understand what the patient wanted.

## Common mistakes

- **Answering from general knowledge.** Ground answers in the business's own information.
- **No easy route to a person.** Frustrated customers leave and complain publicly.
- **Breaking platform rules.** Unsolicited messages and unapproved templates get numbers blocked.
- **Giving medical, legal or financial advice.** Hand over.
- **Stale information.** Make updating the source part of someone's daily routine.

- **Long, email-style replies.** Chat users read on phones; keep answers to a few lines and offer "more details?" instead.
- **No owner for the knowledge sheet.** Name the person who updates timings, prices and leave days, and when.
- **Skipping the signature check** on the webhook.
- **Doing slow work before replying** to the webhook, which causes retries and duplicate answers.
- **Free-text messages outside the 24-hour window.** They fail or break policy; use approved templates.

## When a chat assistant is not worth it

- **Very few messages a day**: a person with saved quick replies is cheaper and warmer.
- **Most questions need judgement**, such as custom quotes or complaints.
- **The business cannot keep its information up to date**: the bot will repeat stale prices and timings.
- **Customers mostly call**, not message: a voice assistant or better IVR may fit better.

## From demo to production

- **Verify signatures and deduplicate** every incoming message.
- **Keep the knowledge sheet owned by the business**, with a date on each entry and a weekly check.
- **Log every conversation** with sources, tools called, hand-overs and ratings, masked.
- **Watch hand-over reasons weekly** and add the most common answer to the sheet.
- **Budget messaging costs** from the current WhatsApp pricing, with the date you checked it.
