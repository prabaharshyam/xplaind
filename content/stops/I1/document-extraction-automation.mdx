## What it is

**Document extraction** turns an unstructured document into structured fields: from an invoice PDF to vendor name, GSTIN, invoice number, date, line items, tax amounts and total. An automation does this for every document that arrives, then checks the result and passes it on to a sheet, an accounting system or an approval step.

Modern AI models handle varied layouts far better than older template-based tools, which needed a template per supplier.

## How it works

**1. Receive.** Documents arrive by email attachment, upload form, shared folder or WhatsApp. The trigger picks them up and stores the original.

**2. Get the content.** Digital PDFs: extract text. Scans and phone photos: OCR, or send the image directly to a vision-capable model. Keep the page reference.

**3. Extract with a schema.** Send the content to the model with a clear instruction and a fixed output shape (field names, types, which are required). Use the tool's structured-output feature or a JSON schema so you get exactly those fields every time. Ask for null when a field is not present instead of a guess.

**4. Validate with rules.** Never trust extraction blindly. Check:

- formats: GSTIN is 15 characters in the right pattern; dates are valid; PIN codes have six digits;
- arithmetic: line totals add up to the subtotal; subtotal plus tax equals the total;
- business rules: the vendor exists in your master list; the PO number matches an open order; the amount is within expected range.

**5. Route.** If every check passes, continue automatically. If anything fails or a required field is missing, send it to a person with the document and the failed check highlighted. Their correction becomes the final record and a useful test case.

**6. Record.** Keep the extracted data, the checks and who approved, for audit.

**Measure.** Track straight-through rate (share with no human touch), field accuracy on a sample checked by people, and time saved.

## A worked example

A Surat textile trader receives about 900 supplier invoices a month as PDFs and phone photos on WhatsApp and email.

The automation:

- Collects attachments from a dedicated email address and a WhatsApp Business number.
- Sends each page to a vision-capable model with a schema: supplier name, supplier GSTIN, invoice number, invoice date, line items (description, HSN code, quantity, rate, amount), CGST, SGST, IGST, total.
- Validates: GSTIN pattern and match against the supplier master; lines add up; tax amounts consistent with rates; invoice number not already recorded for that supplier.
- Writes passing invoices into the accounting import sheet; sends failures to the accounts assistant on a review screen with the image beside the fields.

First month: 71% straight through; most failures were blurry photos and handwritten corrections. After asking frequent suppliers to email PDFs and improving the photo prompt, straight-through reaches 84%. Accounts staff time on invoice entry drops from about 60 hours a month to 15, and the duplicate-invoice check catches four duplicate payments worth ₹2.3 lakh.

## Common mistakes

- **Free-form output.** Use a schema so fields are always present and typed.
- **No validation rules.** Arithmetic and format checks catch most extraction errors.
- **Letting the model guess missing fields.** Ask for null and route to a person.
- **Losing the original document.** Keep it linked to the record for checking and audit.
- **Measuring nothing.** Track straight-through rate and sample accuracy.

Read n8n's AI integration docs and a model provider's structured outputs guide.

1. Collect ten invoices or receipts (your own bills work) and write a schema for the fields you need.
2. Build an n8n workflow that extracts the fields with an AI step and validates the arithmetic and one format rule.
3. Route failures to a review sheet and measure how many of the ten went straight through and how many fields were correct.

**You are done when you can** show your straight-through rate, your field accuracy and one error your validation caught.
