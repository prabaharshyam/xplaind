## What it is

**Pricing an AI feature** means deciding how customers pay for it and checking that revenue covers its costs with a healthy margin. Two facts make it different from ordinary software:

- **Variable cost per use**: every request costs model tokens and infrastructure. Heavy users cost far more than light ones.
- **Uncertain value**: customers are still learning what AI is worth to them.

## How it works

**Know your unit economics.** From real usage, measure cost per request and requests per user per month, for light, typical and heavy users. Cost per user = requests × cost per request. Compare with what the user pays. Model costs have tended to fall over time, but usage often rises faster as features improve.

**Pricing models.**

- **Bundled**: included in the existing plan. Simple and drives adoption, but costs are hidden in the margin; needs fair-use limits.
- **Tiered**: higher plans include AI or more of it ("100 AI summaries a month on Pro, unlimited on Business"). Common and easy to understand.
- **Add-on**: a separate fee per user for the AI feature. Clear value signal; lower adoption.
- **Usage-based**: pay per document, per minute, per conversation or per credit. Aligns price with cost; harder for customers to budget.
- **Outcome-based**: pay per resolved ticket, per qualified lead. Aligns with value; requires clear measurement and shared risk.

Many products combine them: a tier with included usage and charges above it.

**Protect margins.** Fair-use limits, model tiering (cheap models for simple requests), caching, shorter outputs and batch processing for non-urgent work. Watch the top 5% of users; they often drive most cost.

**Price on value, not cost.** Cost sets the floor. Value sets the ceiling: hours saved, revenue gained. Talk to customers about the value; test prices with real offers, not surveys alone.

**Indian market notes.** Price sensitivity is high in consumer and small-business segments; rupee pricing, UPI payments and smaller pack sizes ("₹199 for 50 credits") help. Enterprise clients prefer predictable annual pricing with usage bands.

## A worked example

A Bengaluru SaaS company sells accounting software to small businesses at ₹999 a month. It launches an AI feature that reads invoices and suggests ledger entries.

Measured during beta: average cost ₹0.80 per invoice. Light users process 30 invoices a month (₹24), typical 150 (₹120), heavy 2,000 (₹1,600, more than the whole subscription).

The PM, Varun, considers bundling it free: average cost would be about ₹180 per customer, cutting margin sharply, and heavy users would be loss-making.

He chooses:

- 100 AI invoices a month included in the ₹999 plan (good for adoption);
- packs of 500 for ₹299 above that;
- a Business plan at ₹2,499 with 2,000 included, for accountants managing many clients.

Engineering cuts cost per invoice to ₹0.45 with a smaller model for clear printed invoices and the larger one only for photos and handwriting. Three months later, 62% of customers use the feature, 11% buy packs or upgrade, and the feature's gross margin is positive at every tier.

## Common mistakes

- **Unlimited AI in a flat price.** Heavy users can make you lose money.
- **Ignoring cost per request.** Measure it before setting prices.
- **Pricing only on cost.** Customers pay for value; capture some of it.
- **Complex credit systems.** Customers must understand what they are buying.
- **Never revisiting.** Costs and usage change; review pricing regularly.

## Practise

Read a16z's essay on the economics of generative AI, and check current model prices on an independent comparison site.

1. For an AI feature you know, estimate cost per request and requests per month for light, typical and heavy users.
2. Design two pricing options (for example, tiered with included usage, and usage-based), and compute margin for each user type.
3. Write the questions you would ask five customers to test willingness to pay.

**You are done when you can** show margins for light, typical and heavy users under your chosen pricing, and explain how you protect against the heaviest users.
