## What it is

**Pricing an AI feature** means deciding how customers pay for it and checking that revenue covers its cost with a healthy margin. Two facts make it different from ordinary software:

- **Variable cost per use.** A normal software feature costs almost nothing extra when one more customer clicks it. An AI feature pays for model tokens on every request. Heavy users cost far more than light ones.
- **Uncertain value.** Customers are still learning what AI is worth to them, so there is no settled "normal" price to copy.

This lesson follows Varun, a product manager at an accounting SaaS company in Bengaluru. His company sells a ₹999 a month plan (plus GST) to small businesses and is adding a feature that reads invoices and suggests ledger entries. All company numbers in this lesson are illustrative. Suppose each invoice costs ₹1.00 to process; you will see where that number comes from below.

| User | Invoices a month | Cost at ₹1.00 each | Compared with the ₹999 plan |
|---|---|---|---|
| Light | 30 | ₹30 | Small |
| Typical | 150 | ₹150 | Fine |
| Heavy (an accountant) | 2,000 | **₹2,000** | Double what they pay |

```flow
dir: LR
title: Why a flat price can lose money
Invoices a month × cost per invoice -> Cost for that user
Cost for that user -> Above the ₹999 plan price?
Above the ₹999 plan price? -[yes, the heavy user]-> You lose money on them
Above the ₹999 plan price? -[no]-> Margin
```

The rest of the lesson answers four questions in order: what does one use really cost, which pricing model fits, how do you stop heavy users eating the margin, and what will customers pay.

## How it works

### Step 1: build the cost of one request

You do not need a beta to estimate cost. You need four numbers: tokens in, tokens out, the price per token and the exchange rate. Then add what the model bill does not show.

```flow
title: Building cost per request
Input tokens × input price -> Model cost in dollars
Visible output plus thinking tokens × output price -> Model cost in dollars
Model cost in dollars -[× exchange rate]-> Model cost in rupees
Model cost in rupees -[× retry factor]-> Cost with retries
Cost with retries -[+ storage, logs, hosting]-> Cost per request
```

As of 28 November 2026, Anthropic lists these API prices in US dollars per million tokens (Haiku prices apply to prompts up to 100,000 tokens):

| Model | Input | Output | Good for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Claude Haiku 5.5 | $0.10 | $0.50 | Clear, simple, high-volume tasks |
| Claude Sonnet 5.5 | $2 | $10 | Harder inputs, such as photos and handwriting |

Other providers use the same structure (separate input and output prices) with different numbers. Read their current price page and write the check date next to every number you use.

Varun's estimate for one invoice on Sonnet 5.5 (token counts are illustrative; measure your own on 50 real invoices):

- Input: 1,500 tokens (instructions plus the invoice image) × $2 per million = $0.003.
- Output: 200 visible tokens (the ledger entries) plus 400 thinking tokens = 600 × $10 per million = $0.006.
- Model cost: $0.009. At an assumed ₹94 per dollar, that is ₹0.85.
- Retries: suppose 1 invoice in 10 fails a format check and runs again, so multiply by 1.1: ₹0.93.
- Other per-use costs, such as storage, logs and hosting spread per invoice: suppose ₹0.07.
- **Cost per invoice: about ₹1.00.**

### Step 2: watch the hidden thinking tokens

Most current models "think" before they answer. Those thinking tokens bill as output, at the full output price, even when the user and your app never see them. Anthropic's documentation says you pay for the full thinking even when the response shows only a summary.

This is the most common reason a beta bill surprises a PM. In Varun's first beta week, the model ran at its default effort level, which is `high` for Sonnet 5.5 on Anthropic's API. It thought for about 1,600 tokens per invoice to produce 200 visible ones. The output line became 1,800 × $10 per million = $0.018, and the full cost per invoice rose to about **₹2.24**, more than double the estimate. Setting a lower effort level for extraction, then checking accuracy on a test set of invoices, brought thinking back to about 400 tokens and the cost back to ₹1.00.

Two rules follow. Log thinking tokens separately (Anthropic reports them in the usage data of each response, and OpenAI reports reasoning tokens in a similar field). And never cap the maximum output length to the visible reply size: thinking counts toward that limit, so a tight cap cuts off the answer and the retry costs more.

### Step 3: account for GST, gateway fees and the rupee

Three India-specific items quietly move the margin.

- **GST.** Software subscriptions in India carry 18% GST. If you advertise "₹999 + GST", the customer pays ₹1,178.82 and you keep ₹999. If you advertise "₹999 including GST", you keep only 999 ÷ 1.18 = ₹846.61, about 15% less than you may have assumed. Always work out margin on the price excluding GST, and say clearly on the pricing page which one you mean. Business buyers registered for GST can usually claim the GST back; consumers cannot, so for consumer apps the inclusive price is the one that matters to the buyer.
- **Payment gateway fees.** Razorpay's standard domestic rate is 2% of each payment plus GST on that fee (checked 28 November 2026). On a ₹1,178.82 payment, the 2% is about ₹24, plus about ₹4 of GST on it. A registered business can usually claim back the GST on the fee.
- **The exchange rate.** Most model APIs bill in US dollars. The rupee was about ₹88.7 to the dollar in mid-November 2025 and about ₹93.5 to ₹94 in mid-November 2026, after touching about ₹96 in May 2026. That move alone made the same dollar bill about 6% more expensive in rupees in a year: a $10,000 monthly bill went from about ₹8.9 lakh to ₹9.4 lakh. Price with a buffer, and recompute when the rupee moves. When an Indian GST-registered company buys a foreign API, it usually pays the GST itself under the reverse charge and claims it back; check the details with your accountant.

### Step 4: choose a pricing model

| Model | Example (illustrative unless dated) | Good for | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bundled | AI included in every plan | Adoption | Hidden cost; needs fair-use limits |
| Tiered | 200 a month on Pro, 2,500 on Business | Clarity, upgrades | Choosing the included amounts |
| Add-on | ₹299 per user per month for AI | A clear value signal | Lower adoption |
| Usage-based | ₹1.50 per invoice | Price follows cost | Customers find it hard to budget |
| Outcome-based | Intercom Fin: $0.99 per resolution (checked 28 November 2026) | Price follows value | Needs an agreed definition of success |

Most products combine them: a tier with included usage and paid packs above it. This hybrid keeps the bill predictable for most customers and still charges the heaviest ones.

Outcome-based pricing is attractive but demanding. Intercom defines a billable Fin resolution as a conversation where the customer asks for no further help after Fin's last answer. Notice what that does: the vendor carries the cost of every failed attempt, so it only works if your success rate is high and stable. Before you choose it, write the exact definition of an "outcome" that both you and the customer can count from logs.

### Step 5: design credits customers trust

If you sell packs or credits, decide three things up front.

- **Expiry.** Credits that expire at month end without warning feel like a trick and create angry support tickets. Prefer packs valid for 12 months, or roll unused included usage over for one month.
- **Visibility.** Show a usage meter in the app, and warn at 80% and 100% of included use, by email and in the product.
- **Breakage.** Breakage is revenue from credits that are paid for but never used. Do not build your margin on it. Customers who notice they are paying for unused credits leave.

### Step 6: protect the margin

- **Per-account rate limits.** A daily cap per account (for example, 3 times a normal heavy day) stops one script or abused free trial from burning a month of budget overnight.
- **Model tiering.** Send clear, simple requests to the small model and only hard ones to the large model.
- **Caching.** Reuse a cached prompt prefix (the fixed instructions) instead of paying full price for it on every request.
- **Shorter outputs and batch processing** for work that is not urgent.
- **Watch the top 5% of users.** In Varun's mix below, 5% of customers drive 55% of the cost.

### Step 7: price on value, not cost

Cost sets the floor. Value sets the ceiling: hours saved, errors avoided, revenue gained. Before fixing a price, Varun's team asked ten customers how long invoice entry took and who did it. Small shops said one or two hours a week, done by the owner. Accountants said whole days of staff time each month. That difference in value, not the difference in cost, justified a separate Business plan for accountants. Test prices with real offers (a pricing page, a sales quote), not only with surveys: people say one thing in a survey and pay another.

Costs and usage both change. Token prices have tended to fall, but better features bring more and longer requests, and the rupee moves. Recompute unit economics every quarter from real usage, not from the beta.

**Try it in your browser.** This works out cost per invoice from tokens and the margin left for each customer type. Change the numbers to your own.

```python
# Illustrative token counts; prices are Anthropic list prices, USD per million tokens, checked 28 November 2026
INR_PER_USD = 94          # assumption; check today's rate
RETRY = 1.1               # 1 in 10 requests runs twice
OTHER_INR = 0.07          # storage, logs, hosting per request

def cost_per_invoice(tokens_in, visible_out, thinking, price_in, price_out):
    usd = tokens_in * price_in / 1e6 + (visible_out + thinking) * price_out / 1e6
    return usd * INR_PER_USD * RETRY + OTHER_INR

print(f"Sonnet 5.5, high effort: ₹{cost_per_invoice(1500, 200, 1600, 2, 10):.2f}")
print(f"Sonnet 5.5, low effort:  ₹{cost_per_invoice(1500, 200, 400, 2, 10):.2f}")
print(f"Haiku 5.5:               ₹{cost_per_invoice(1500, 200, 400, 0.10, 0.50):.2f}")

cost = 0.45               # blended cost after tiering, from the worked example
for name, invoices, pays in [("Light", 30, 999), ("Typical", 150, 999), ("Heavy", 2000, 2499)]:
    ai_cost = invoices * cost
    print(f"{name}: pays ₹{pays} before GST, AI cost ₹{ai_cost:.2f}, left ₹{pays - ai_cost:.2f}")
```

## A worked example

Varun has the beta cost: ₹1.00 per invoice on Sonnet 5.5 at low effort. Now he prices.

**The average hides the problem.** Suppose the customer base is 50% light (30 invoices a month), 45% typical (150) and 5% heavy (2,000). The weighted average is 0.5 × 30 + 0.45 × 150 + 0.05 × 2,000 = 182.5 invoices, so about ₹183 per customer a month. That looks affordable on a ₹999 plan. But the heavy 5% account for 100 of those 182.5 invoices, which is 55% of the total cost, and each of them costs ₹2,000 against ₹999 paid. Bundling unlimited AI would lose about ₹1,000 a month on every accountant.

**He chooses a hybrid** (all prices exclude GST):

- **Pro, ₹999:** 200 AI invoices a month included, which covers light and typical users and drives adoption.
- **Packs:** 500 more invoices for ₹399, valid for 12 months, with a usage meter and an alert at 80%.
- **Business, ₹2,499:** 2,500 invoices included, plus multi-client features for accountants.

The pack price is deliberate. An accountant on Pro who needs 2,000 invoices would buy four packs: ₹999 + 4 × ₹399 = ₹2,595. Business is cheaper and includes more, so heavy users move up a tier instead of sitting on a loss-making plan.

These prices only work at a lower cost per invoice. At ₹1.00, a full pack costs ₹500 to serve against ₹399, and a Business customer at the 2,500 cap costs ₹2,500 against ₹2,499. So Varun sets a cost target of ₹0.50 and ships model tiering before the new plans go live.

**Engineering cuts the cost.** Suppose 70% of invoices are clear, printed PDFs. These go to Haiku 5.5, costing about ₹0.12 each (same token counts, about 20 times cheaper per token, plus the same ₹0.07 of other costs). The other 30%, photos and handwriting, stay on Sonnet 5.5 at ₹1.00. A check catches Haiku mistakes, and suppose 1 in 10 of the Haiku invoices then re-runs on Sonnet 5.5 at ₹1.00 extra. The blend:

- Haiku share: 0.70 × ₹0.12 = ₹0.08
- Sonnet share: 0.30 × ₹1.00 = ₹0.30
- Escalations: a tenth of the 70%, so 7% of all invoices, × ₹1.00 = ₹0.07
- **Blended cost: about ₹0.45 per invoice**

| Customer type | Invoices a month | Plan | Pays (before GST) | AI cost at ₹1.00 | AI cost at ₹0.45 | Left after AI cost at ₹0.45 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Light | 30 | Pro | ₹999 | ₹30 | ₹13.50 | ₹985.50 |
| Typical | 150 | Pro | ₹999 | ₹150 | ₹67.50 | ₹931.50 |
| Heavy (accountant) | 2,000 | Business | ₹2,499 | ₹2,000 | ₹900 | ₹1,599 |

The "left" column is not profit: it still has to pay for the rest of the product, support and sales, and about 2% goes to the payment gateway. At ₹1.00 the heavy accountant would leave only ₹499; at ₹0.45 it leaves ₹1,599, and a full pack (₹225 of cost against ₹399) is profitable too. Varun sets three alerts for the quarterly review: cost per invoice above ₹0.60, the rupee above ₹100 to the dollar, and any account using more than 3 times its plan's included invoices.

## Common mistakes

- **Unlimited AI in a flat price.** The heaviest 5% of users can drive more than half the cost and pay less than they cost.
- **Pricing from the visible reply length.** Thinking tokens bill as output. Measure them before setting prices, and set the effort level per task.
- **Working out margin on the GST-inclusive price.** 18% of it is never yours, and the gateway takes about 2% more.
- **Ignoring the exchange rate.** A dollar-billed model gets more expensive when the rupee weakens, with no change in usage.
- **Pricing from beta usage.** Beta users are keen and few. Usage, request length and the user mix change after launch.
- **No per-account rate limit.** One script or abused free trial can burn a month of budget in a night.
- **Credits that expire unseen.** Customers feel cheated and leave. Show a meter, warn early and allow rollover.
- **Pricing only on cost.** Customers pay for value; capture some of it.
- **Copying a competitor's price.** Their costs, customers and value differ from yours.
