## What it is

**Triage** means deciding what each incoming message is, how urgent it is and who should handle it. AI triage automates the first pass:

- **Classify**: what kind of message is it (billing, delivery, technical issue, complaint, sales enquiry, spam)?
- **Extract**: the details the team needs (order number, account id, product, location).
- **Prioritise**: urgent or not (service down, payment failed, legal threat, VIP customer).
- **Route**: to the right team or person, in the right tool.
- **Draft**: a suggested reply for a person to check and send.

## How it works

**Define the categories with the team.** Use the categories your support or operations team already works with, with a one-line definition and two or three example messages each. Include "other / unclear".

**Classify with structured output.** The AI step returns category, priority, extracted fields, a one-line summary and a confidence or "unsure" flag, in a fixed shape.

**Rules around the AI.** Some decisions should be rules, not AI judgement: messages from a list of key accounts are always high priority; any message mentioning legal action, police, or a consumer court goes to a senior person; anything about data deletion goes to the privacy contact.

**Route.** Create or update the ticket in the help desk, assign the team, set the priority and add the extracted fields and summary. Unsure cases go to a general queue for a person to sort.

**Draft replies, do not send them (at first).** Generate a reply draft using the customer's message, the extracted details and your knowledge base, and place it in the ticket for an agent to edit and send. Only after measuring quality for weeks should simple, low-risk replies (such as "we have received your request") be sent automatically.

**Languages.** Indian customers write in English, Hindi, Tamil, Hinglish and more. Test triage on real messages in each language you receive.

**Measure.** Classification accuracy on a weekly sample, share routed correctly first time, time to first response, and how much agents edit drafts.

## A worked example

A Chennai broadband provider's support inbox gets about 2,500 emails a day. Previously two staff spent the morning sorting them; urgent outages waited hours.

The automation classifies each email into eight categories (outage, slow speed, billing, plan change, new connection, relocation, complaint, other), extracts the customer id and area, and sets priority. Rules force high priority for "outage" in areas with an open network incident and for any mention of "consumer forum" or "legal notice". Tickets go to the right team in the help desk with a summary and a reply draft.

On a 400-email test set in English, Tamil and Tanglish, classification accuracy is 92%; the main confusion is between "slow speed" and "outage", which the team decides to route to the same network queue. After launch, median time to first response falls from 5 hours to 40 minutes, and agents send 60% of drafts with light edits.

## Common mistakes

- **Inventing categories the team does not use.** Use theirs.
- **Letting AI decide legal or safety escalations alone.** Use rules for these.
- **Auto-sending replies too early.** Start with drafts and measure.
- **Testing only in English.** Test the languages customers actually write in.
- **No "unsure" path.** Forcing a category on every message hides errors.

## Practise

Read n8n's AI docs and a prompting best-practices guide.

1. Collect 50 real or realistic messages for a support inbox, in at least two languages, and label them with five categories and a priority.
2. Build a triage workflow that classifies, extracts one key field and routes to a sheet per category, with an "unsure" path.
3. Measure accuracy against your labels and add one rule-based escalation.

**You are done when you can** show your accuracy on the test set, the most common confusion and how you handled it.
