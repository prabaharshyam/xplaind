## Flagged for the owner

1. **DPDP timing (ai-data-strategy, build-vs-buy, writing-ai-policy).** These lessons describe notice and consent duties under the DPDP Act as if they already apply, for example: "Under India's DPDP Act … personal data may be used only for purposes people were told about." The DPDP Rules were notified on 13 November 2025. The core notice, consent and security duties take effect about 18 months later, around 13 May 2027. As planning advice the lessons are fine. You may want one line saying the duties phase in by May 2027. Source: law-firm summaries, for example https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/indias-digital-personal-data-protection-act-phased-rollout-and-key-compliance-milestones/. I did not read the Gazette text itself.
2. **writing-ai-policy: "India's AI Governance Guidelines … are good sources for principles."** MeitY did release the India AI Governance Guidelines on 5 November 2025 under the IndiaAI Mission (news and law-firm coverage, for example https://ssrana.in/articles/meity-unveils-india-ai-governance-guidelines/). I did not open the PIB or MeitY PDF itself. The glossary term `india-ai-governance-guidelines` should point to the official document.
3. **sql-for-engineers: `ROUND(AVG(EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM …)) / 3600, 1)`.** This works on PostgreSQL 14 and later, where EXTRACT returns numeric. On 13 and older, it fails with "function round(double precision, integer) does not exist". I could not run Postgres in the sandbox. Consider a `::numeric` cast if learners may meet older client databases.
4. **choosing-ai-problem exercise: "(B) and (E)… need a licensed professional by law".** Roughly right for India: prescribing and diagnosis are for registered medical practitioners. A pharmacy chain, though, deals with pharmacists rather than doctors. A person should confirm the wording is precise enough.
5. **build-vs-buy worked example.** The build is justified by a two-year cost "lower than the add-on's licences", but that add-on had already been ruled out on fit and safety. It is not wrong, but it is a weak comparison. Comparing with a compliant vendor's fees, or giving the build cost alone, would read better.
6. **measuring-roi.** The formula lists "− maintenance", but the worked example has no maintenance line. The ranges table also puts the highest running cost (₹11,000) under "Low", which is correct for a pessimistic case but may confuse readers. Consider a maintenance line and renaming the columns "Cautious / Expected / Optimistic".
