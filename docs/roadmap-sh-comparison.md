# Xplaind and roadmap.sh: what is missing, and why one works

Written 2026-10-26 from roadmap.sh's home page, changelog and pricing page as they stood that day. Their numbers are what their own footer says, and they vary between page snapshots, so treat them as approximate.

## The two sites in numbers

| | roadmap.sh | Xplaind today |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Roadmaps | 31 role, 60 skill, 5 best-practice | 9, all AI roles |
| Users | About 3.2 million registered | None yet: the site is not public or indexed (`allowIndexing` is off until launch) |
| Community | About 369,000 GitHub stars (one of the top 10 repositories on GitHub), about 52,000 on Discord | None |
| Lessons | Topic pages on every roadmap, plus 6 lesson packs with projects | 163 deep lessons, a check on each, 22 guided projects |
| AI | AI Tutor on every roadmap, topic and guide; course, guide, quiz and learning-plan generators; AI roadmap editor | Coach, lesson tutor, X Bot, roadmap and course generators, AI interview review |
| Price | Free, with Pro at about US$10 a month (unlimited AI) and Teams at US$10 a seat | Free, with AI behind sign-in and a US$20 monthly spend cap |
| Release pace | A changelog entry every month since 2023 | Built in sprints, nothing released |

## Why roadmap.sh works

1. **It started as a GitHub repository, and that is still its engine.** Developers star it, share it and send fixes. The stars bring trust and search ranking, and the contributors keep 90 roadmaps up to date without a paid staff.
2. **Search brings people in for free.** Every topic on every roadmap is its own page, so there are tens of thousands of pages that each answer a narrow question ("what is a Docker volume"). The traffic is mostly people who did not know the site existed.
3. **One picture answers the first question.** "What should I learn to become a backend developer?" is answered in one image you can save, print or share. The map is the product, and people share it before they ever sign up.
4. **It is broad, so it is the default.** Whatever a developer wants to learn next, there is probably a roadmap for it. People come back for the next skill, not only the first one.
5. **Progress makes people come back.** Done, Learning and Skip on every topic, synced to an account, with a public profile and team dashboards. Ticking boxes is satisfying, and an account makes leaving cost something.
6. **It ships every month and says so.** The changelog, newsletters and YouTube give people a reason to come back and show that the site is alive. Their AI tools, for example, went from launch to "on every page" in about nine months.
7. **Money pays for the work.** Pro and Teams pay for AI and staff without ads ruining the free map.

## What Xplaind is missing, compared with roadmap.sh

**Getting found and shared**
- A public, indexed site at all. This is the gap that matters most: none of the rest counts until people can find it.
- Open content people can contribute to, with an "edit this page" link and a way to suggest a course.
- A roadmap you can download as an image or PDF, and embed on a blog or a GitHub README.
- A newsletter (roadmap.sh has AI Weekly), a public changelog, and a YouTube or Discord presence.
- Breadth near AI: Python, SQL, Docker, Git, Linux, cloud and system design are on their own roadmaps there. Ours touch them only as lessons inside AI paths.

**Coming back**
- Progress synced to an account by default. Ours lives in the browser until Supabase is set up, so a new phone or a cleared browser loses it.
- A public profile that shows progress, and team or classroom dashboards for colleges and companies.
- Custom roadmaps people can share by link. Ours are saved in the browser only.
- Community: a gallery of project submissions with feedback or upvotes. Our project links stay on the device.
- A feedback button on every page.

**Trust**
- A review process. Our lessons, interview answers and project guides were drafted with AI and are marked unreviewed. The content check lists 1,384 to-do items before launch. Their contributor community does this review for them.
- Proof that someone is keeping it current, such as the "actively maintained" banner and the monthly changelog.

**Things we have that they do not.** Keep these as the reason to choose us:
- A placement test that skips what you already know.
- India-first content: rupee prices with the date we checked them, Indian examples, and paths for people who do not code (AI for Beginners, AI Product Manager, AI Automation).
- One named best course per topic, with no paid ranking.
- A spoken mock interview with a scored report, and an interview question bank per role.
- Guided projects with steps, hints and a rubric.
- A deep lesson for every topic, not only a list of links.

## Why Xplaind would not work as it stands

1. **No distribution.** roadmap.sh's strengths (stars, search ranking, word of mouth) build up over years. A new site with no audience has none of them, and nothing in the current plan says who the first 1,000 learners are or where they come from.
2. **The niche overlaps their newest work.** roadmap.sh has AI Engineer, Forward Deployed Engineer, MLOps, Machine Learning, AI Agents, Prompt Engineering, AI Red Teaming and AI Product Builder roadmaps, plus an AI tutor on each. A learner who already uses roadmap.sh gets our core idea there for free. "Same thing for AI" is not a reason to switch. The reason has to be the parts they lack: India, the test, the interview practice, and paths for people who do not code.
3. **Unreviewed AI content is a risk to the promise.** The pitch is honest, checked guidance. If an early visitor finds a wrong lesson or a dead course link, that promise breaks. roadmap.sh gets away with thin topic pages because the map itself is the value. We are selling the lessons.
4. **Progress that lives in the browser does not bring people back.** Without accounts working, there is no email, no streak reminder and no sync, so there is no reason to come back next week.
5. **No money and a small AI budget.** The US$20 monthly cap is right for cost, but it means AI features run out quickly if the site grows, and nothing yet pays for review or upkeep.
6. **Too much at once.** Nine roadmaps, AI tools, a simulator, projects and X Bot, built before a single learner has used one roadmap end to end. roadmap.sh grew one roadmap at a time, from what users asked for.

## What to do about it, in order

1. **Launch one sharp promise**, for example "the AI interview prep site for India": the simulator, the question bank and two or three roadmaps, reviewed by a person. Leave the rest unindexed until each part is reviewed.
2. **Turn on accounts and email**, so progress syncs, and send a weekly email with your streak and next five steps.
3. **Make the map shareable**: download it as an image, embed it, and add share cards for level results and finished projects.
4. **Open the content for contributions**: an "edit on GitHub" link and "suggest a better course", with a named reviewer for each path.
5. **Build search pages on purpose**: one page per interview question and glossary term already exists. Add pages targeting Indian searches, such as "AI engineer salary in India", "free AI course with certificate in Hindi" or "AI course for B.Com graduates".
6. **Find the first learners** in places that already gather them: college placement cells, LinkedIn, r/developersIndia, YouTube creators who make AI career videos.
7. **Publish a public changelog** and ship small things often.
